# Partner Center reporting and analytics — what a partner can actually measure

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/insights/insights-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/insights/insights-overview-report · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/insights/referral-cosell-insights · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/account-settings/permissions-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/payout-policy-details
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Where do I find analytics in Partner Center?

In the **Insights** workspace, which Microsoft describes as "a unified analytics hub that addresses all the data analytics and business intelligence needs," containing reports, forecasts and recommendations. You reach it by signing in to Partner Center and selecting **Insights**. Referral analytics are the exception to the single-hub rule — Microsoft notes they can be accessed from both the Insights workspace and the Referrals workspace, so a partner working co-sell can stay in one place. Marketplace publishers additionally have Analytics sections within the Marketplace offers experience, plus a Downloads hub and analytics APIs for pulling data programmatically.

## Which roles do I need to see reports?

Access is role-gated and it is the most common reason a partner reports "there's no data." The Insights workspace overview lists **Account admin**, **Executive report viewer** and **Report viewer** as the appropriate roles, and the Cloud product performance summary additionally names **Admin agent**. Referral and co-sell insights require **Referrals admin**. Marketplace analytics follow the seller-account roles — Owner, Manager, Developer, Business Contributor, Finance Contributor and Marketer all carry some level of marketplace insights access, with Marketer limited to non-financial insights. Two revenue-specific restrictions matter: **Azure Consumed Revenue data is only available to users assigned the Executive report viewer role**, and CSP Resellers cannot access revenue-related information on the summary page at all.

## What determines which report sections I can see?

Program enrolment, on top of roles. Microsoft states that "access to the Analytics sections of the different programs is based on the roles and access levels a user has and follows access to various programs in Partner Center" — and gives the concrete example that Insights displays a **Microsoft Marketplace offers** section only if you have access to the Marketplace offers workspace. The program-based analytics areas Microsoft documents are **Memberships**, **Microsoft Marketplace offers**, **Office add-ins**, **Referrals**, and **Apps and Games**. A services-only partner with no marketplace offers will therefore see a materially smaller Insights workspace than an ISV, which is expected behaviour rather than a permissions fault.

## What does the cloud product performance summary show?

It is the top-level business snapshot, built around four KPIs — Customers, Subscriptions, Azure Consumed Revenue and Licenses — each shown with a current count, percentage growth over the selected date range, and a month-over-month trend chart. **Customers** counts all customers with at least one active subscription attributed to your company across attribution types and cloud products. **Azure Consumed Revenue (ACR)** shows total ACR in US dollars attributed to you. **Subscriptions sold** counts cloud product subscriptions active and inactive that you sold or manage, while **Active subscriptions** counts only those with active usage measured from product telemetry, excluding trial Azure subscriptions. **Licenses deployed** counts licences deployed in customer subscriptions over the period.

## What breakdowns does the summary report give me?

Five views beyond the headline KPIs. A **geographical spread** view distributes total customers, subscriptions and licences by customer country or region, with a searchable table showing the metric value and percentage of total per country. **Customers growth trend**, **Subscriptions growth trend**, **Azure Consumed Revenue growth trend** and **Licenses growth trend** each plot the metric month by month over the selected range, with a slider to zoom into a specific period. **Top 25 customers by revenue** and **Top 25 resellers by revenue** plot revenue trends and let you pivot by product family — Office 365, Dynamics 365, Azure, EMS or Power BI. Microsoft flags that this revenue view excludes the Cloud-Other category; for total revenue, download the *Customers and Tenants* detailed report from the Downloads hub.

## What can I measure about my referrals and co-sell performance?

The **Co-sell opportunities** page under the **Analyze** section in Referrals covers both IP co-sell and Services co-sell referrals, split across an IP co-sell tab, a Services co-sell tab and an **All** tab. The **Summary** card shows total deals, deals accepted, deals won and lost, and total deal value won in USD for the period. For IP co-sell only, it also shows deals registered, deals approved, deals rejected, total deal approved value and annual contract value. Microsoft explains the percentage-change indicators precisely: they compare the **last full month** in the selected range against the **first full month** in that range — so on a 3-month filter in mid-June, the comparison is May against March, not a rolling calculation.

## What is the co-sell conversion funnel and what does it tell me?

It shows how deals move between states across their lifecycle, viewable by deal volume or by deal value in USD, with the first section labelled by both state and deal type so you can see the mix. Inbound co-sell deals merge into **accepted, declined or expired**, because partners must accept or decline them. Partner-led, private and outbound co-sell deals merge into **Created**, because the partner originated them. A **Referrals from the past** section captures deals you acted on during the selected window — accepted, declined, won or lost — even if they were created earlier. Two operational metrics sit in this widget and are the ones to watch: **average acceptance time** and **average time from opportunity created or received to win**.

## Can I see how my deal registrations are performing?

Yes, there is a dedicated **Deal registration** funnel showing how registered deals move between states through their lifecycle, by volume or by USD value. Microsoft is explicit about who sees it: the section "is shown only to the partners who have Azure IP co-sell eligible solutions and have registered deals," and the deal-registration widgets on the Summary card carry the same condition. The widget is not applicable to Services co-sell referrals and does not appear on the Services co-sell tab. For a partner whose registration approval rate is the number that matters commercially — because that is what converts a won deal into an IP incentive — this funnel is the single most important view in the workspace.

## What else does the co-sell analytics dashboard break down?

Four further sections. **Geographical spread** shows which countries and regions deals came from, with a per-country detail table. **Solutions** shows which of your solutions drive the most referrals and highest deal value, pivotable by All, Co-sell, Partner-led and Private, with totals for deals won, lost and expired and won/lost value in USD. **Capabilities** does the same aggregation pivoted by Partner Program and Partner Role, which is the Services co-sell view. **Declined and Lost reasons** shows the reasons your own sellers chose when closing deals as declined or lost. **Comparison charts** let you compare across four dimensions — deal type, markets, solutions, and co-sell type — on volume, deals value won, or deals approved value, with up to three options selectable for markets and solutions.

## Why do the solution and capability numbers not add up to my deal totals?

Because a deal with multiple solutions is counted once against each of them. Microsoft warns explicitly: "If multiple solutions are included in a deal, the table will show the same deal counted against all those solutions. You should not add up the values related to solutions and compare it with other referral volume metrics." The identical caveat applies to the Capabilities table when multiple Partner Programs are on a deal. These views exist to show relative performance by solution or by capability, not to reconcile to a total. A partner building a board metric from this table without reading the note will overstate their pipeline.

## How do I filter and slice the co-sell data?

The filter panel offers customer name, country, deal type, deal direction, solution name, marketplace purchase intent, partner program and partner role. Two behaviours are worth knowing because they cause "missing data" confusion. First, **the deal type filter is pre-applied with Co-sell and Partner-led selected** — if you only run Private deals, you will see an empty dashboard until you reset that filter. Second, **marketplace purchase intent** has a *Not Applicable* value covering referrals created before that field existed, which Microsoft dates to **1 June 2023**. The time range selector sits top-right and offers the past 1, 3, 6 or 12 months or a custom range, defaulting to three months. Partner Program and Partner Role filters apply only to Services co-sell and appear only on the All and Services co-sell tabs.

## How do I get the data out of Partner Center?

Several routes, with different limits. From the co-sell analytics page you can **Download data** as CSV or TSV, capped at a **maximum of 5,000 records** sorted descending by referral creation date, honouring the applied timeline and filters — and you must stay on the page, because navigating away cancels the download. Microsoft notes this downloads *analytics* data for the selected period and directs you to the **Co-sell opportunities** page in the Referrals workspace to export *transactional* data instead. Leads can be downloaded to CSV from the Referrals workspace, with the export honouring applied filters and including a `LeadCreationDateTime` field. Marketplace publishers additionally have the Downloads hub, the analytics API, and the ISV Revenue API.

## Why is my report empty or out of date?

Microsoft lists the causes for a blank co-sell chart, and most are not faults. There may genuinely be no data for the account. There may be a network connectivity issue. **The page loads with the default filter set to co-sell deals, so a partner with only private deals sees nothing until they reset it.** The applied filters may exclude everything. Or there may simply be lag: Microsoft states "there's a delay between the opportunity state change and for the same to be updated in the analytics report. Check the report after 24 hours." Referrals closed as **error** are excluded from the insights widgets and from data downloads by design, so error-closed deals will never appear no matter how the filters are set.

## What reporting exists on the revenue and payout side?

The **Earnings** workspace carries its own reporting, separate from Insights, and marketplace publishers get dedicated dashboards. The Earnings **Reports** page is the central download hub for Earnings-Default, Earnings-Growth, Earnings-Store, Earnings-Microsoft Marketplace, Payments, and ineligible revenue and usage. The **Revenue** page gives eligibility outcomes for Microsoft Commerce Incentives, including top-50 customer views by eligible and ineligible revenue and usage with ineligibility reason codes. Marketplace publishers use the **Usage**, **Orders** and **Revenue** dashboards to trace a transaction through its payout lifecycle — Microsoft's payout timing tables reference these dashboards at each step, and notes that invoice payment terms can be determined from the Revenue dashboard's Invoice (Created) Dates and Due Dates.

## What are Partner contribution indicators?

Partner contribution indicators, abbreviated PCI, are described in Microsoft's Partner Center overview as showing "partner effects on seven key measures of customer success." They sit alongside the partner insights data reports, which Microsoft describes as identifying where sales are high, helping find customers who deployed their subscriptions or need support, and showing how well products are selling. PCI detail lives in the Membership analytics area of Insights, accessible with the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program Partner Admin role, which Microsoft documents as unlocking "Solutions partner insights, customer eligibility, score simulator, qualification criteria and learn analytics." A partner working toward a Solutions Partner designation will spend most of their Insights time here rather than in the co-sell dashboards.

## Currency warning

Report names, KPI definitions, role requirements and export limits change with Partner Center releases: the **5,000-record** analytics download cap, the **24-hour** analytics refresh lag, the **1 June 2023** cutoff for marketplace purchase intent data, the pre-applied Co-sell/Partner-led deal type filter, the top-25 and top-50 result caps, and the Executive-report-viewer restriction on ACR data are all current behaviours rather than permanent ones. The Insights overview article carries a Microsoft date of 2024-01-30 and the cloud product performance summary 2024-10-08, both older than the co-sell insights article at 2025-01-31 — so the summary-level documentation may lag the product. This reflects Microsoft Learn as retrieved on 14 August 2026. Where a partner is making a business decision on a number, cross-check the dashboard figure against the corresponding detailed report download rather than the UI tile.
