# Deal registration in Partner Center — what it is, who qualifies, and how to do it

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/register-deals · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/referrals-faq · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-overview
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is deal registration in Partner Center?

Deal registration is the step where a partner tells Microsoft the commercial detail of a deal it has already won, so that Microsoft can validate it and credit the partner for it. Microsoft's framing is direct: "You can register deals that you win in Partner Center by providing more information about the contract. The information helps Microsoft to provide you with more opportunities in the future." Registration happens *after* a deal is marked **Won** — it is not a pre-sales claim on an opportunity and it is not the same thing as creating a referral. Registering supplies the contract dates, contract value, solution value, tenant type and pricing model that Microsoft's validation team uses to confirm the sale actually happened as described.

## Why does registering a deal matter to a partner?

Registration is the mechanism that converts a won co-sell deal into Microsoft-side credit. Microsoft states that only IP incentives are eligible for deal registration, and names them: Azure IP co-sell, Business Applications premium, and Business Applications standard. In other words, if your solution carries Azure IP co-sell eligibility or a Biz Apps co-sell incentive tag, registration is how that incentive is claimed against a specific customer contract. Beyond the incentive itself, registered and approved deals feed the Microsoft-side view of your performance — deal registration counts, approved counts, rejected counts, approved deal value and annual contract value all surface in the partner's own co-sell analytics, and Microsoft uses win reporting to shape the quality of future referrals it sends you.

## Which deals are eligible for Azure IP co-sell deal registration?

Microsoft publishes a hard eligibility list, and a deal must meet **all** of it. The deal type must be either **co-sell** or **partner-led** — partner-led means you answered *Yes* to "Would you like Microsoft sellers to view this deal?" The deal value must be greater than or equal to **USD 25,000**, with currency conversion based on monthly exchange rates published by Reuters. The customer account selected on the deal must be **Microsoft-managed**, meaning Microsoft's sales team already has a relationship with that company. There must be at least one **Azure IP co-sell eligible solution** in the deal. The deal status must be **won**. And if the deal type is co-sell, Microsoft must have either accepted the invitation or marked the deal won — you can check this on the Microsoft card below your deal details.

## How do I know whether my deal qualifies before I try to register it?

Partner Center shows eligibility for you rather than making you work it out. Deal registration eligibility is always displayed below the deal progress bar, and once you mark the deal as won a **Register now** button appears at the bottom of the screen. Microsoft is explicit about the negative case too: "If the deal registration banner doesn't appear in the deal progress bar for your deal, the deal doesn't meet all the requirements." For Business Applications ISV Connect deals a banner appears at the top of the deal reading *"Registration for Business Applications ISV Connect is required on this sale."* You can register immediately after marking the deal won, or come back later and use the **Register now** button in the deal lifecycle.

## What are the two paths that lead to deal registration?

Microsoft documents exactly two entry points. The first is the co-sell path: you created a deal in the **Co-sell opportunities** section of the Referrals workspace and that deal meets the registration criteria. Existing registrations that are in review, and registrations already closed as approved or rejected, live in the same section. The second is the **ISV Connect deals** path, used to report a closed ISV Connect deal that was not co-sold with Microsoft; if there is a co-sell opportunity associated with the ISV Connect deal the link to start registration appears in that opportunity, and if there is no corresponding opportunity you start from the **ISV Connect deals** tab. One important exclusion: **Solution Assessment deals are not eligible for deal registration.**

## How do I register a co-sell deal in Partner Center?

The mechanics are short. In the **Referrals** workspace, open the **Co-sell opportunities** tab and go to the won deal. Select **Register** at the bottom of the screen, then complete the registration form with the required contract information. After you submit, you can watch validation progress in the same deal lifecycle view; if Microsoft needs anything from you, messages appear in that lifecycle telling you what is required. The deal moves into a **Closed** state once validation completes. Microsoft adds a compliance caution worth repeating to any partner: register the deal only if your company name and the Azure IP co-sell eligible solution are clearly named in the contract with the customer.

## How long do I have to register a deal after the contract is signed?

You have **60 days from the date the contract is signed** to register a co-sell deal, and Microsoft notes that all dates are in Coordinated Universal Time. This deadline is enforced through the form's date validation: the contract signed date can be at most 60 days in the past, cannot be in the future, and must fall on or after the later of the referral creation date and the Microsoft invitation date. The contract start date is allowed to be up to 90 days in the past and may be earlier than the signed date. Missing the 60-day window is one of the most common causes of a lost registration, so treat contract signature as the trigger to register rather than waiting for deployment or invoicing.

## Why should I wait 72 hours after creating a deal before marking it Won?

Because closing it faster than that can get the registration thrown out. Microsoft states it plainly: "Wait 72 hours after creating an eligible deal before marking it *Won*. If you mark the deal as won earlier than that, the registration might be rejected." The same warning appears in the co-sell opportunity guidance — make sure there is a gap of 72 hours between creating the deal and marking it won. Practically this means a partner should create the referral in Partner Center at the point the opportunity is real, not retroactively on the day the paperwork is signed, because a same-day create-and-close pattern is exactly what the rule is designed to catch.

## What information does the deal registration form ask for?

The form captures contract economics and deployment facts. You are asked whether this is a **marketplace transaction** — answering Yes enables the simplified Marketplace deal registration and requires an **estimated marketplace transaction date**. You must state **whether the solution will be deployed on Azure**; if you answer *Others*, you are prompted to close the deal without registration, because only Azure solutions are eligible. You state whether the contract is **perpetual**, give the **total contract value** (software and services, but not hardware), and the **solution value** (total contract value less any non-recurring implementation fee, and which must be less than or equal to total contract value). You supply the **contract signed, start and end dates**, the **tenant type** (whether the solution is deployed on the customer or partner tenant), and the **pricing model** (whether or not it is pay-as-you-go).

## How is a perpetual contract valued for registration?

Perpetual contracts are annualised rather than taken at face value. Microsoft's rule is that if the contract between the partner and the customer is perpetual, the total contract value entered on the registration form is spread over a **six-year period** to determine the annual contract value, and registration is allowed only if that annual contract value is greater than USD 25,000. The same six-year assumption governs the end date: a perpetual contract does not require an end date on the form, and Microsoft deems the end date to be six years from the contract start date for the purposes of the annual contract value calculation. A partner registering a perpetual deal should therefore check the arithmetic before submitting — a USD 120,000 perpetual contract annualises to USD 20,000 and will not clear the threshold.

## How do I register a pay-as-you-go deal?

Pay-as-you-go deals qualify for Azure IP co-sell eligibility when the same customer's payments to the same partner, for the same solution, equal or exceed **USD 25,000 in one year**. To register one, indicate pay-as-you-go in the pricing model field — this moves the deal to **Review Pending**, because pay-as-you-go deals do not qualify for automated validation. Microsoft adds several specific rules: the deal must be shared before the first contract or invoice date; set the sign date and start date to the date of the first invoice and the end date to the date of the last or most recent invoice; and the contract signing date can be up to one year after the later of the referral creation date and the Microsoft invitation date.

## Can I revise a pay-as-you-go registration if the deal grows?

Only narrowly, and only in one direction. Microsoft allows a **one-time-only** update to a previously registered pay-as-you-go deal within the same fiscal year, and only when the total contract value of the deal exceeds **USD 1 million**; a revalidation is then required. Revalidations across fiscal years are not accepted. Critically, a previously approved deal that was **not** identified as pay-as-you-go at registration time cannot be revalidated to adjust to a higher total contract value. This makes the pricing-model answer on the original form consequential — mislabelling a genuinely consumption-based deal as a fixed contract closes off the only upward-revision path Microsoft offers.

## What are the deal registration states and what do they mean?

A registration moves through a documented state machine. **Registration (Pending)** means the deal is eligible and can be registered. **Approved** means it met the requirements and was approved automatically. **Review (Pending)** means the Microsoft review team is checking the registration details, which can also be triggered for other reasons such as suspected duplicates. **Action required** means the review team sent it back to you with comments for editing, and it returns to Review (Pending) once resubmitted. **Review (Passed)** and **Review (Failed)** are the review outcomes, both leading to Closed; failure reasons are communicated via a banner in Partner Center. For marketplace-transacted deals there is a parallel set — **Marketplace Validation (Pending / Passed / Failed)**.

## Which registrations are approved automatically, and which get manually reviewed?

Most deals are approved automatically. Microsoft names three exclusions that instead route a registration to **Review (Pending)** for manual review: the **annual contract value is greater than USD 1 million**; there are **multiple partners involved in the same referral**; or the **pricing type is pay-as-you-go**. Automatic approval is not final, though — Microsoft states that registrations approved automatically can still be selected for further review, in which case you are notified by email and by a banner in Partner Center. No action is needed from you unless the review team sends the registration back and moves it to **Action required**.

## My deal registration was rejected — what can I do?

There is a formal exception process, but it is a single shot. Partners can request an exception related to Azure IP co-sell deal registration **within 60 days from the date of initial registration**. Two situations qualify: a referral marked won that is not eligible for registration because of a programmatic check failure, and a referral where registration is in progress or has been decided by the validation team, for example a registration in a failed state after review. The **Request Exception** link appears in the bottom-right corner of the screen and is visible only for deals in the **Closed** state, including the Won, Lost and Error substatuses. Microsoft states that **only one exception request per referral is allowed** and the decision is final and binding.

## What makes a good exception request?

Microsoft gives explicit guidance, and partners get up to **5,000 characters** to make the case. Provide a detailed business justification for why the deal should receive an exception. For pre-registration exception requests, provide all contract dates — signed, start and end. If a tooling error blocked the registration, specify the error and include any relevant support ticket numbers opened with other teams. For re-registrations, reference the old deal ID (referral ID) and state that the new registration replaces it. For updates to approved deals, name the specific fields to be updated, the new values, and the business justification. The exception council can take **up to 14 working days** to decide, and may contact the partner for more evidence first.

## Can support overturn a rejected exception?

No. Microsoft is unusually blunt on this point: "Support can't override a decision the exception council makes, so support teams can't help if an exception request is rejected. **Don't create support requests for cases in which an exception request is rejected by the exception council**." This is why the quality of the original 5,000-character justification matters so much — there is no second attempt and no escalation path afterwards. Set partner expectations accordingly: the exception process is the appeal, not a first step before one.

## Which registration fields can be changed through the exception process?

Microsoft lists the fields eligible for change: whether the contract is perpetual, total contract value, contract signed date, contract start date, contract end date, and tenant type. If an exception is approved for any of these, the deal is reopened and the partner is responsible for making the changes promptly in line with program guidelines. Tenant type carries an extra condition — a change from customer tenant type to partner tenant type is allowed only if it was done by mistake **within 90 days of deal registration** and evidence is provided showing the deployment is genuinely on the partner tenant. Microsoft also notes that any change request to contract details is subject to standard proof-of-execution verification, and that these changes are not allowed if the deal was validated through a DCF form exception.

## Which fields cannot be changed, and what is the workaround?

Several fields are rejected outright for change requests. **Tenant type changes after 90 days** are not allowed, and Microsoft states the partner cannot recreate the referral with a different tenant type — any re-registration will be rejected, because it is program policy that tenant change requests are unsupported in any manner after 90 days. **"Will the solution be deployed on Azure?"** cannot be changed at all, since only Azure solutions are eligible for IP co-sell benefits, and there is no mitigation. For **solution value**, **estimated marketplace transaction date**, **is a marketplace transaction**, and **pricing model**, the documented mitigation is the same: create a new referral, then file an exception request to address the contract sign date requirement, referencing the original referral ID.

## What happens after an exception is approved?

The outcome depends on what was blocked. If the referral was originally ineligible for registration and the exception is approved, the partner is given the option to register the deal for Azure IP co-sell. If the Azure IP co-sell deal registration was in a failed state and the exception is approved, the partner can edit the registration details with the correct information and resubmit to the deal validation team for review. In both cases Microsoft relaxes some of the programmatic date validations so the partner can enter the correct data as it appears in the contract documents — which is why the exception request needs to carry the real contract dates in the first place.

## What is ISV Connect deal registration and how does it differ?

ISV Connect registration is the Business Applications track, and it has a much lower value bar: the only criterion is that the **value of the deal must be greater than USD 5,000**. It applies to solutions carrying an ISV Connect **Standard** or **Premium** tag, and Microsoft notes the registration is for Dynamics 365 Dataverse / Power Apps or Dynamics 365 Operations published solutions. Two scenarios exist. If the solution in the referral has only a Biz Apps standard or premium incentive status, you register the ISV Connect deal from the banner. If the solution has **both** Azure IP co-sell eligibility and a Biz Apps incentive, you register the ISV Connect deal *in addition to* the co-sell deal registration — and it is better to complete the co-sell registration first, because that prefills the ISV Connect details and saves entering the data twice.

## What does the ISV Connect registration form require?

The ISV Connect **Deal Registration** form asks for customer details (company name, country/region, city, state); the **solution**, chosen from a list of ISV Connect Standard or Premium tagged solutions — if none appear, check that the offer is tagged correctly; **total contract value and currency** including software and service fees but not hardware; **solution value and currency**, generally total contract value less any non-recurring implementation fees; **solution deployment** (Azure or Other); **solution consumption** on partner or customer tenant, where a customer tenant requires the customer tenant ID as a GUID; **contract date details** where the start date must be on or after the signed date; and **partner contact information**. Note that if the contract start date precedes the signed date in the co-sell registration, the ISV Connect start date is filled with the signed date.

## Can I register ISV Connect deals in bulk?

Yes — Partner Center supports CSV bulk upload for ISV Connect deal registrations, and the function is available to users with the **Referrals admin** permission. The upload link sits on the command bar of the grid view on the **Deal Registrations** page. You can create or update a maximum of **1,000 records** per file, and a file with more than 1,000 records cannot be processed. You can build the template from scratch by downloading it from the upload page, or download existing records and edit them. After processing, a summary shows how many records were created, updated and not processed; errors are listed in an **Errors** column that you must clear before re-uploading corrected rows. Microsoft notes a 1,000-record file can take up to 30 minutes to process.

## What is the difference between creating a referral and registering a deal?

They are separate steps at opposite ends of the deal, and conflating them is a common partner mistake. Creating a referral or co-sell opportunity happens at the *start* — it puts the deal into Partner Center, optionally shares it with Microsoft sellers, and starts the collaboration. Deal registration happens at the *end*, only after the deal is marked won, and only for deals carrying an eligible IP solution. A deal that was never created as a referral cannot be registered through the co-sell path at all, which is why the 72-hour rule and the requirement that the contract signed date fall on or after the referral creation date both push partners to create the referral early. Register late and the registration fails; create the referral late and there is nothing to register.
