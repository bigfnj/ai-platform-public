# Co-sell with Microsoft — statuses, eligibility, and how deals reach Microsoft sellers

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-requirements · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-configure · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/services-co-sell · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/referrals-faq
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What does co-sell actually mean at Microsoft?

Microsoft defines co-selling broadly as "any collaborative engagement between Microsoft and its partner ecosystem," and lists what that includes: building demand, sales planning, sharing sales leads, accelerating partner-to-partner empowered selling, and delivering marketplace-led commerce. In practice a co-sell opportunity is a collaboration between a partner and one or more Microsoft sellers, other Microsoft partners, or both, to meet a customer need. The solutions involved — Microsoft calls them offers — can be software built with the partner's own intellectual property, or services that support Microsoft technologies. Microsoft's stated value proposition is that co-sell lets a partner use the Microsoft brand, sales teams, demand-generation capability and Microsoft Marketplace to reach new customers worldwide.

## What are the types of co-sell opportunity?

Partner Center recognises several distinct deal types and the type governs what Microsoft can see and do. **Azure IP co-sell** is where a software development company co-sells using at least one Azure IP co-sell eligible solution. **Services co-sell** is where a services partner holding at least one Solutions Partner designation co-sells on a customer opportunity. **Partner-to-partner (P2P)** is where one partner invites other partners in the Microsoft co-sell ecosystem to collaborate, optionally also inviting a Microsoft seller. **Solution assessments (SA)** can only be created by partners vetted by the solution assessments business team. **Partner-led** and **Private** are the two self-run types, described separately below.

## What is the difference between a private deal, a partner-led deal, and a co-sell deal?

The difference is entirely about what Microsoft can see and whether you asked for help, and it is set by two questions on the create-deal form. A **private deal** answers "No help required at this point of time" to *Identify the kind of help that you need from Microsoft* and **No** to *Would you like Microsoft sellers to view this deal?* — Microsoft sellers have no visibility into it. A **partner-led deal** answers "No help required" but **Yes** to seller visibility, giving Microsoft pipeline visibility without active involvement. A **co-sell deal** selects any help option other than "No help required," which invites a Microsoft seller in. Crucially, partner-led deals are still eligible for deal registration even though no Microsoft seller is involved.

## Can I upgrade a private deal later?

Yes, and Microsoft designed the workspace so you can. Private deals are included in the **Co-sell opportunities** tab specifically "to provide an easy path to upgrade any pipeline deal to a co-sell opportunity" — you can upgrade a private deal into a partner-led or active co-sell deal at any time before it reaches a terminal state. Terminal states are *won*, *lost*, *declined* and *expired*, and once a deal enters one it cannot be modified at all. The same applies to inviting partners or Microsoft into a P2P deal: both can be invited after the deal is created, as long as the deal is not yet terminal. Microsoft's advice is to exercise caution before moving any deal into a terminal state, because there is no undo.

## What are the co-sell statuses a solution can hold?

Microsoft applies co-sell statuses to solutions, and they form a ladder. For Azure the statuses are **In market**, **Co-sell ready**, and **Azure IP co-sell eligible**. *In market* means the solution is linked to a live Microsoft Marketplace offer but has not met co-sell-ready requirements. *Co-sell ready* means the co-sell-ready requirements have been met. *Azure IP co-sell eligible* means co-sell-ready has been met **plus** four further requirements. For business applications there are two statuses — **Business Applications Co-sell Incentive Standard** and **Premium** — which incentivise Microsoft sales teams to sell your offer, and which apply to Dynamics 365 apps on Dataverse and Power Apps, and Dynamics 365 Operations Apps.

## What does co-sell-ready status get me, and what does IP co-sell eligibility add?

Co-sell-ready status is what exposes your solutions to Microsoft sales teams — it is the entry ticket to reaching Microsoft-managed customers through Microsoft's own sellers. Azure IP co-sell eligibility adds two things Microsoft names explicitly: it "allows you to submit co-sell referrals and for your offers to contribute toward customers' Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitments (MACC)." Eligible customers then see the offer marked **Azure benefit eligible** in the Azure portal. Offers that achieve Azure IP co-sell eligible status — or that are enrolled in ISV Success with co-sell-ready status — also receive a **Microsoft preferred solutions** badge on the offer listing page, which can take up to 30 days to appear in the online store.

## What do I have to do to become co-sell ready?

Microsoft lists the requirements for all partners: have a PartnerID (formerly MPNID) and an active Microsoft Marketplace account in Partner Center; have a **complete business profile** in Partner Center; **publish the offer** live on Microsoft Marketplace; provide a **sales contact for each co-sell-eligible geography**; and provide the required listing and document information on the **Co-sell → Solutions** page. Services partners have one extra requirement — for Professional service solutions of any *Solution* type, hold at least one Solutions Partner designation in the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program. Business Applications ISVs must have **ISV Success** enrolment for Dynamics 365 apps on Dataverse and Power Apps and Dynamics 365 Operations Apps solutions.

## Are there offers that can never be co-sell ready?

Yes. Microsoft states that any offer published through the Microsoft Marketplace developer program in Partner Center is eligible for co-sell-ready status if the co-sell-ready requirements are met, but names one exclusion: "Apps and add-ins for Microsoft Office (for Microsoft Teams, Microsoft Outlook, and Microsoft Excel, for example) aren't eligible for co-sell-ready status." Separately, offers that are merely *in-market* — live in the marketplace but not co-sell ready — are still eligible to be attached as solutions inside co-sell deals, so an in-market offer is not useless; it just cannot be the solution that unlocks deal creation or IP co-sell benefits.

## What are the four extra requirements for Azure IP co-sell eligible status?

After achieving co-sell-ready, Microsoft names four further requirements. **Requirement 1 — revenue threshold**: at the organization level, generate at least **USD 100,000 of Azure Consumed Revenue or Marketplace Billed Sales over the trailing 12-month period**; Azure credits or ACO do not count toward this. **Requirement 2 — technical validation**: the solution must meet Microsoft Marketplace certification policies, be primarily platformed in Azure, and pass the Azure platformed technical validation process. **Requirement 3 — reference architecture diagram**: upload one with your co-sell documents for review; this is not mandatory for Azure App, Container or VM offer types. **Requirement 4 — transactability**: effective **11 July 2023**, new offers must be transactable on Microsoft Marketplace, a condition that applies only to obtaining IP co-sell eligible status.

## Which offer types can achieve Azure IP co-sell eligibility?

The status applies to four offer types only — **Azure Application**, **Azure Container**, **Azure Virtual Machine**, and **Software as a Service (SaaS)** — and Microsoft adds a second gate: the solution type for the offer must be **"IP"** to be eligible. Microsoft also notes that the offer must be *actively* platformed on Microsoft Azure at the time of the transaction, stating this "ensures alignment between the offer's certified deployment environment and the actual transaction, maintaining the integrity of the certification process." A partner cannot certify an Azure-platformed offer and then transact a deployment somewhere else. Microsoft is also explicit that Azure IP co-sell eligible status is a prerequisite for MACC eligibility of an offer.

## How do I configure a solution for co-sell in Partner Center?

You need the **Co-sell solution admin** role, and the offer must already be live in Microsoft Marketplace. Sign in to Partner Center, select **Referrals**, then **Co-sell → Solutions**, open the solution and use **Add/Edit** next to the **Listing** header. On the Listing page you select one or more **Microsoft platforms** your solution is built with, extends or integrates with; a **solution type** (Device/hardware, IP/application, Managed service, or Service); and up to five **solution areas**, each with at least one and up to five **sales plays**. When all required fields are complete you select **Submit for review**, and Microsoft reviews the solution to determine which co-sell status it can be given. Microsoft notes you no longer need to contact anyone to nominate a solution.

## Which documents do I have to upload to get co-sell status?

Two documents are mandatory for co-sell-ready and a third for IP co-sell eligibility. The **solution one-pager** is a customer-ready description of your value proposition — Microsoft provides templates for Azure, Dynamics 365, Microsoft 365 and Windows, and says the document should not exceed 10 pages. The **solution pitch deck** pitches your solution and its value proposition, should reference the reference architecture diagram, and should cover what the solution does, how it helps customers, which industries it suits and how it compares with competitors. The **reference architecture diagram** is required for SaaS offers and shows your solution's relationship with Microsoft cloud services. Optional but recommended: a customer case study, verifiable customer wins, a channel pitch deck, and up to five other documents or videos.

## Why do I need to provide a sales contact for every geography?

Because Microsoft's sellers and channel resellers use those contacts to reach the right person in your organisation, and coverage gaps directly cost you deals. A contact is required for each geography in which your solution is available, and Microsoft says contact information is available to all Microsoft sales teams — and, if you make your solution available in the CSP program, to channel resellers as well. Four contact roles exist: **Partner marketing** (the main point of contact for marketing engagements and listing content), **Partner sales** (Microsoft says include at least one per region in which you want your solution to be co-sell-ready, and one contact can cover multiple regions), **Partner technical sales**, and **Partner customer success manager**. Microsoft's own emphasis: "It is critical that you keep your contact information up to date."

## How do I create a co-sell opportunity?

You need at least one **co-sell-ready** solution before you can create a new co-sell deal — Microsoft notes that once you have one, you can also co-sell with in-market solutions. In Partner Center go to **Referrals → Co-sell opportunities** and select **New Deal**. The flow walks through selecting a customer, adding deal details, capturing marketplace intent, adding team members, adding solutions, and choosing the deal type. If the **New Deal** button is greyed out, the FAQ gives the three causes: you lack the **Referral admin** role, or your solution is not published in Microsoft Marketplace, or your solution does not have **co-sell ready** status.

## Why does it matter whether the customer is "Microsoft Managed"?

Because it decides whether Microsoft can help and whether you can register the deal. When you search for a customer, results appear on three tabs. **Microsoft Managed** accounts are managed by Microsoft — pick from here and the deal is eligible for Azure IP co-sell deal registration if it meets the other criteria. **Microsoft Unmanaged** accounts are not managed by Microsoft, and a deal on one of these is **not** eligible for IP co-sell deal registration. **Other** shows accounts from the Moody's database, where managed status cannot be confirmed at creation time; the referral then goes through an internal Microsoft account matching process and registration is possible only if it matches to a Microsoft-managed account. For federal accounts, Microsoft says to select from the Moody's list only.

## Why does Microsoft push so hard on picking from the Managed or Unmanaged tabs?

Microsoft gives three concrete reasons and they are all about avoiding pain later. First, you know immediately whether deal registration is possible — pick from **Other** or enter manually and you will not know until later, and you will not be able to change the account without involving support, which causes delays. Second, the response from Microsoft is faster, because a Microsoft-matched account skips the internal account matching process and lands directly with the Microsoft seller if you are asking for help. Third, Microsoft can collapse duplicate entries for the same customer into a single row, whereas the Other tab is an external database and may show duplicates. If you cannot find an account, try name variations, parent or merged entities, and the **Search more** option.

## What has to be on a co-sell deal before Microsoft will look at it?

Required fields are deal name, location (the partner program location scope, which cannot be edited after creation), estimated value, estimated close date, and — for both IP co-sell and Services co-sell — **Solution Area** and **Solution Play**. Microsoft made Solution Area and Solution Play mandatory for all new IP co-sell deals created through the Partner Center portal and bulk upload, stating the change is "for faster seller identification in Microsoft sales ecosystem." You must add at least one employee with full contact details, and at least one solution for an IP co-sell or Solution Assessment deal. Optional fields are CRM ID and marketing campaign ID, both purely for your own tracking. Partner Center shows passive warnings for very high or very low deal values and for close dates within seven days.

## What kinds of help can I request from Microsoft on a co-sell deal?

Selecting any option other than "No help required at this point of time" converts the deal into a co-sell opportunity. The help types Microsoft offers are: **workload-specific value proposition**, **customer technical architecture**, **proof of concept / demo**, **quotes and licensing**, **post-sales customer success**, and **general or other**. Microsoft's guidance is to be as detailed and specific as possible in the notes, articulating the customer's business problem statement and the specific help required, because that is what lets a Microsoft seller evaluate the opportunity and align expertise and resources. Partner Center now uses generative AI to help draft these notes from the metadata you have already entered — you can select, edit or override the suggestions.

## How long does Microsoft take to decide whether to join a co-sell deal?

**14 days.** Microsoft states that in a co-sell, "a Microsoft seller can potentially help closing the deal, although their participation isn't guaranteed. Microsoft sales representatives have a 14-day window to decide whether they'll participate in the deal." Participation is genuinely optional on Microsoft's side — inviting Microsoft is a request, not an assignment. You can see the outcome by opening the deal and looking at the **Microsoft** status section; if your request was declined, Microsoft seller details are not displayed. If a Microsoft seller declined by accident, the documented remedy is to re-create the opportunity and invite Microsoft again.

## Why would Microsoft decline my co-sell deal?

Microsoft publishes two decline reasons and what they mean. *"No corresponding Microsoft customer record could be found"* means Microsoft's sales team cannot assist because Microsoft does not have a relationship with the customer. *"No Microsoft seller directly manages this customer"* means Microsoft cannot assist because Microsoft does not manage the customer. Both are account-coverage problems, not solution-quality problems. Microsoft also notes two cases where you will not see a decline reason at all: the deal expired before Microsoft could respond, or the deal value is less than USD 25,000. For an SMB-focused partner this is the structural constraint to understand — most genuinely small customers are not Microsoft-managed, so co-sell with an active Microsoft seller will not be available on those deals.

## What are the sales stages on a co-sell deal, and do I have to fill them in?

Partner Center maps your progress to seven standard stages with fixed percentages: **Created** and **Accepted** at 10%, **Qualified** at 20%, **Developed** at 40%, **Proposed** at 60%, **Negotiated** at 80%, and **Won** at 100%. Microsoft maps these to MCEM stages — Created, Accepted and Qualified sit in *Listen and Consult* with an exit criterion of a qualified opportunity; Developed sits in *Inspire and Design* exiting on customer alignment to solution and business case; Proposed and Negotiated sit in *Empower and Achieve* exiting on customer agreement in place; and Won sits in *Realize Value*. Providing these details is optional in general but **required** for solution assessment deals — and the **Won** button is only enabled once all previous stages are marked complete.

## How does Services co-sell differ from IP co-sell?

Services co-sell exists so that skilled services partners can co-sell without publishing marketplace IP. Microsoft states that qualified partners holding **Solutions Partner designations, Specializations, or Azure Expert Managed Services Provider (MSP)** can co-sell with Microsoft sellers "without having to publish a Microsoft Marketplace Consulting services offer." Adding a solution is required for IP co-sell but **optional** for Services co-sell — a partner can attach a consulting services offer, but no longer has to. What Services co-sell requires instead is a **Partner role** describing what the partner will do, plus Solution area and Solution play. Microsoft also notes that opportunities under the Microsoft Commerce Incentive (MCI) program are tracked as co-sell opportunities.

## What partner roles can I claim on a Services co-sell deal?

Microsoft lists eight roles a services partner can play, and the role is used both when you create an outbound opportunity and when a Microsoft seller sends you an inbound one: **Presales envisioning**, **Solution design**, **Proof of concept**, **Business strategy**, **Deployment services**, **Adoption and change management**, **Transaction**, and **Managed services**. Microsoft sellers evaluate services partners for co-selling based on the business profile published in Partner Center, which is why the profile is a prerequisite rather than a nicety. Inbound services co-sell opportunities arrive on the **Inbound** tab of the Co-sell opportunities page and carry the Partner role and Solution area/play fields so you can qualify them quickly.

## What are Solution Areas and Solution Plays and why are they mandatory?

They are the standard taxonomy Microsoft's sellers use to describe what a customer is trying to achieve, and Partner Center now requires them so opportunities route to the right seller. Microsoft's documented FY26 Solution Areas are **AI Business Solutions**, **Cloud and AI Platforms**, **Microsoft Unified**, and **Security**, each with a set of Solution Plays — for example *Copilot and Agents at Work*, *Migrate and Modernize Your Estate*, *Unify Your Data Platform*, *Modern SecOps with Unified Platform*, *Innovate with Azure AI Apps and Agents*, and *Secure AI Productivity*. Microsoft's stated purpose is that this "helps Microsoft Sellers identify the customer's objectives in standard terminology" and makes partner-seller interactions seamless.

## Who registers the deal in a partner-to-partner co-sell?

Only one party should, and Microsoft names which. "In partner-to-partner deals, only the partner who owns the end-customer contract with the IP solutions included should register a deal if the referral is eligible for Azure IP co-sell deal registration." Getting this wrong creates duplicate registrations, which is one of the reasons Microsoft routes registrations to manual review — a registration where multiple partners are involved in the same referral is excluded from automatic approval. In a P2P deal the inviting partner sets the invited partner's estimated close date and estimated value at creation and cannot change them afterwards, though the invited partner can change both after accepting, and can modify the solutions and add their own employees.

## What happens if I made a mistake on a deal — can I delete it?

No. Microsoft states that opportunities cannot be deleted in Partner Center, including those marked closed in error, in order to preserve historical data integrity. What you can do instead is **Close as Error** at any point in the workflow, choosing a reason such as duplicate deal or incorrect customer selected, which moves the deal into the Error stage. Referrals closed as error are excluded from the insights widgets and data downloads, so they will not distort your analytics. Outbound opportunities can also be **cloned** with a single click, which Microsoft positions as the way to recreate an expired or declined opportunity — before cloning, verify the customer details, solution area and play, budget and timelines, contacts and products.

## Currency warning

Co-sell eligibility rules carry hard numbers that Microsoft revises: the **USD 100,000** trailing-12-month Azure Consumed Revenue or Marketplace Billed Sales threshold for IP co-sell eligibility, the **11 July 2023** transactability effective date, the **14-day** Microsoft seller decision window, the **USD 25,000** floor below which decline reasons are not shown, the five-solution-area and five-sales-play caps, the 10-page one-pager limit, and the 30-day delay on the Microsoft preferred solutions badge. Solution Areas and Solution Plays are explicitly labelled **FY26** and will change with the fiscal year, as will the ISV Success and Marketplace Rewards requirements referenced here.
