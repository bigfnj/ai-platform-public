# MACC — how a partner's marketplace offer draws down a customer's Azure commitment

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/azure-consumption-commitment-enrollment · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/marketplace/azure-consumption-commitment-benefit · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/benefits/macc/track-consumption-commitment · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-requirements
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is a Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment (MACC)?

A MACC is a contractual agreement in which a customer's organisation commits to spending a specified amount on Azure over a defined period. Microsoft describes it from both sides: to publishers, "MACC is a contractual commitment customers make with Microsoft to spend a specific amount on Azure over time," and to customers, an agreement where "eligible Microsoft Marketplace purchases automatically count toward fulfilling this commitment." MACCs sit under a **Microsoft Customer Agreement** or an **Enterprise Agreement** billing account. The commercially important consequence for partners is that a customer with an unmet MACC has budget that is already committed to Microsoft — and a MACC-eligible marketplace offer lets that budget buy a partner's software.

## Why does MACC matter so much in a partner sales conversation?

Because it removes the budget objection. A customer under a MACC has already contractually committed the money to Microsoft; if a partner's solution is MACC-eligible and purchased correctly, the purchase draws down that commitment instead of requiring net-new spend. Microsoft frames it in its marketplace value proposition as helping customers "apply purchases toward their Azure consumption commitment" and positioning the solution as part of their broader cloud and innovation strategy. Microsoft's own consequence for the customer is real money: if a customer does not meet the MACC amount by its end date, a shortfall charge is applied for the remaining balance in the form of an Azure prepayment credit. Customers under-consuming a MACC are actively looking for eligible spend.

## How much of a marketplace purchase counts toward the customer's MACC?

**100% of the pretax purchase amount.** Microsoft's customer-facing documentation states: "when you purchase Azure benefit-eligible offers from Microsoft partners through Marketplace, 100% of the pretax purchase amount also contributes toward your commitment." Microsoft also notes that Azure services as defined in the Product Terms and billed through a Marketplace deployment inherently contribute toward the commitment. This is the number that makes the motion work — a customer buying a partner's eligible SaaS through the marketplace is not diverting budget away from their Microsoft commitment, they are fulfilling it.

## How does a partner get an offer enrolled in MACC?

There is no separate MACC application form. Microsoft's rule is: "For an offer to be enrolled in MACC, it must meet all **Azure IP Co-sell eligibility requirements**. Once these requirements are met, you can expect your offer to be enrolled in MACC within 1 week." MACC eligibility is therefore entirely downstream of Azure IP co-sell eligibility, which itself requires co-sell-ready status plus the four IP co-sell requirements — the USD 100,000 trailing-12-month Azure Consumed Revenue or Marketplace Billed Sales threshold at organization level, Microsoft technical validation as an Azure-platformed solution, a reference architecture diagram, and offer transactability on Microsoft Marketplace. Microsoft states directly that "Azure IP co-sell eligible status is a prerequisite for MACC eligibility of an offer."

## Does MACC enrolment apply to my product or to each individual offer?

To each individual offer, and this is the mistake that costs partners deals. Microsoft is explicit: "MACC enrollment applies at the offer level. If you create multiple Marketplace offers, even for the same product, each offer must be MACC-eligible for the purchase to contribute to your customer's MACC." A publisher with a flagship SaaS offer that is enrolled and a second, newer offer for the same product that is not will find the second purchase does nothing for the customer's commitment. Before promising a MACC benefit on a specific deal, check the specific offer the customer will actually buy.

## How do I check whether my offer is enrolled in MACC?

Partner Center shows enrolment status per offer. Sign in to Partner Center, select the **Marketplace offers** tile on the Home page, select the offer you want to check, and on the **Offer overview** page look at the **Marketplace programs** section — the **Microsoft Azure consumption commitment** status reads either *Enrolled* or *Not Enrolled*. Microsoft's guidance is to do this before the customer buys: "To make sure your customer receives MACC credit for their purchase of your product, please validate the offer they are purchasing is *Enrolled* in the Microsoft Azure consumption commitment program." Partner Center also shows solution MACC eligibility while you are searching for solutions to attach to a co-sell deal.

## How does the customer find MACC-eligible solutions?

By the **Azure benefit eligible** badge. Microsoft tells customers to look for that badge in Microsoft Marketplace to identify solutions that contribute to their MACC. In the Azure portal there is an **Azure benefit eligible** filter customers can apply to narrow the catalogue to qualifying solutions. Microsoft's recommended path for customers is to sign in to the Azure portal using a tenant associated with their organisation, filter to Azure benefit eligible solutions, and purchase using an Azure subscription tied to the organisation's agreement. For a partner, the badge is also a marketing asset — it is the visible signal on your listing page that a committed customer's budget can reach you.

## Where the customer buys from changes whether MACC is credited — how?

This is the trap that silently kills the benefit. Microsoft states that while Microsoft Marketplace displays the "Azure benefit eligible" badge, **purchases made directly on Microsoft Marketplace via credit card do not contribute to MACC**. To ensure the purchase counts, the customer must complete the purchase through Marketplace within the Azure portal by selecting the **"Get it in Azure portal"** button during checkout, using an Azure subscription tied to the organisation's agreement. A partner who has done everything right on the publishing side can still lose the customer's MACC credit at the checkout step, so the guidance to give a customer is specific: buy it in the Azure portal, on a subscription under your agreement.

## What is excluded from the MACC benefit?

Microsoft names several exclusions. **Marketplace purchases using Azure prepayment** (previously called monetary commitments) are not eligible for this benefit. **Exclusions might apply to Azure CtC agreements signed before this marketplace benefit existed**, and Microsoft directs eligibility questions to the customer's Microsoft account executive. Most consequentially for licensing conversations, **the MACC benefit only applies to licences used exclusively in Azure** — if the licence is deployed in a hybrid environment or on-premises, the purchased licence is not eligible to contribute to the commitment. On the publisher side, Microsoft states that **free and BYOL offers are not considered transactable** and therefore do not meet IP co-sell eligibility or MACC requirements.

## How do Azure credits and prepayment interact with a MACC?

They reduce what counts. Microsoft states that if an organisation receives Azure credits from Microsoft, consumption or purchases covered by those credits do not contribute to the MACC. If the organisation purchased Azure prepayment, consumption or purchases covered by that prepayment do not contribute either — although the **purchase of the prepayment itself does decrement the MACC**. Microsoft's worked example: Contoso commits USD 50,000 in May, buys a USD 10,000 Azure prepayment in June which decrements the MACC to USD 40,000, then consumes USD 10,000 of prepayment-eligible services in June which are covered by the prepayment and do *not* decrement the MACC. Once the prepayment is fully used, consumption and eligible purchases decrement again. Microsoft also notes that consumption covered by shortfall credits does not accrue toward the MACC.

## How does a customer see their MACC balance?

Through the Azure portal, though with an access caveat that matters when a partner is asked to help. MACC functionality in the Azure portal is available only to **direct** Microsoft Customer Agreement and **direct** Enterprise Agreement customers — a direct agreement is one signed directly between Microsoft and the customer, whereas an indirect agreement involves the customer signing through a Microsoft partner. For an Enterprise Agreement the viewer needs the Enterprise Administrator role; for a Microsoft Customer Agreement they need Owner, Contributor or Reader on the billing account. The MACC view shows remaining commitment, plus details including ID, purchase date, start and end dates, commitment amount and status, and an **Events** section listing the invoiced spending that decremented the commitment.

## What MACC statuses exist and what do they signal for a deal?

Four statuses, and each one changes the sales conversation. **Active** means the MACC is in effect and eligible Azure spending contributes toward fulfilling it — this is the buying window. **Complete** means the customer fully met the commitment and no further action is required, so the MACC argument no longer applies. **Expired** means the end date passed without the commitment being fully met. **Canceled** means the MACC was terminated before the end date and new Azure spending no longer contributes. For both Expired and Canceled, Microsoft directs the customer to contact their Microsoft account team. A partner should establish which status applies before building a proposal around commitment drawdown.

## What are MACC milestones and why do they create urgency?

Milestones are predefined financial targets within the MACC framework that a customer must meet within a specific timeframe, each carrying a due date and a milestone commitment amount. Not every MACC includes them; where configured, they appear in the Azure portal when the customer selects an event. The consequence of missing one is financial: "If you don't meet a milestone by its due date, a shortfall charge is applied to your account to fulfill the milestone commitment," taking the form of an Azure prepayment credit. Microsoft emails billing account admins at **90, 60 and 30 days** before both commitment expiry and milestone end dates. Those alert windows are, in practice, the highest-intent moments in a MACC-backed customer's year.

## How do I use MACC eligibility when creating a co-sell deal?

Partner Center surfaces MACC on both sides of the deal. When you select a customer, Microsoft notes the partner "can see if the selected customer account has a Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment (MACC) contract with Microsoft." When you add solutions, you can see solution MACC eligibility while searching. Microsoft then gives a direct instruction: "If you select a MACC-eligible customer, make sure you select a MACC-eligible solution from Microsoft Marketplace catalog." The same section adds a parallel rule for marketplace intent — if you mark marketplace intent as Yes, select an incentivized solution from the catalogue. Matching an eligible customer to an eligible solution is the whole point of the pairing.

## Currency warning

MACC rules are commercial terms Microsoft revises, and several of the figures here are load-bearing: the **100%** pretax contribution rate for Azure benefit-eligible partner offers, the **one-week** enrolment timeline after IP co-sell eligibility is met, the **90/60/30-day** alerting intervals, and the underlying **USD 100,000** trailing-12-month revenue threshold inherited from Azure IP co-sell eligibility. The exclusion set — Azure prepayment purchases, pre-existing CtC agreements, hybrid and on-premises deployment, free and BYOL offers — is equally subject to change. Note also that individual customer agreements can carry bespoke terms, which is why Microsoft routes eligibility questions to the customer's own account executive. This reflects Microsoft Learn as retrieved on 14 August 2026, with the MACC enrolment article carrying a Microsoft date of 2025-01-21 and the customer benefit article 2024-10-27. Never assert a MACC outcome for a specific customer without confirming the offer's *Enrolled* status in Partner Center and the customer's agreement terms.
