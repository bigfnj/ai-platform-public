# Referrals and leads — how Microsoft sends a partner business, and the clock on responding

## What are referrals in Partner Center?

Referrals is the Partner Center workspace through which Microsoft passes sales opportunities to partners. Microsoft's own framing is that many customers are looking for qualified experts to implement Microsoft technology, and that Microsoft "often gets direct requests for help from customers through our marketing activities and from our internal Microsoft sales agents." Customers also search directly using the **Connect with partners** directory on Microsoft Marketplace. Those requests land in the Referrals workspace, which contains two distinct things: **Leads**, which are expressions of customer interest, and **Co-sell opportunities**, which are active deals where a Microsoft seller or another partner is engaged. Microsoft states it sends millions of leads to partners every year.

## How do I start receiving leads from Microsoft?

The single most important prerequisite is a published business profile. Microsoft says the most important step you can take to start getting sales leads "is to create a business profile that showcases your company's offerings, solutions, and expertise," and that your profile is then listed in all the places customers and internal Microsoft sales agents search for qualified partners. Without a profile you are invisible to both the customer-facing directory and Microsoft's internal partner search. Creating one requires the **Business profile admin** role, though a **Referrals admin** can also manage a modern business profile. After publishing, updates appear in Microsoft Marketplace within 24 hours.

## What goes into a business profile and how do I make it findable?

You create a profile per region in which you are authorised to do business, choosing a **profile type** (CSP, or Services/ISV), a **default Partner ID** — note that Microsoft Marketplace leads are routed to this Partner ID — plus publishing locations, selling markets, company information and expertise tags. The **Find a Partner** search tool in the Microsoft Marketplace partner directory relies on expertise tags, not on your free-text description; Microsoft explicitly says "no need to stuff the description with search keywords" because the search tools analyse the Partner Expertise section instead. Tags cover industry focus, Microsoft products, service type, and solutions/skills/capabilities. Attained Solutions Partner designations, Azure Expert MSP and Specializations also feed the search, though these badges can take up to 72 hours to appear in Microsoft Marketplace.

## Should I set a target customer size on my profile?

Yes, and the choice cuts both ways. The profile includes a **customer organization size** setting that lets you exclude leads from businesses that do not match your preferred customer size, so you can spend time on businesses that fit your ideal customer profile. But Microsoft offers a specific piece of advice for partners new to co-sell: "If you're just starting to co-sell with Microsoft, having a smaller target size/larger range might help increase the number of opportunities applicable to you as you gain a proven record of accomplishment with Microsoft co-selling." For an SMB-focused partner this is the setting that determines whether the Microsoft-sourced pipeline is a trickle or a stream.

## What types of lead does Microsoft actually send?

The Leads section is split into tabs, and the tab tells you where the lead came from. **Marketplace leads** are customer inquiries originating from business profiles or from offer description pages on Microsoft Marketplace, including the Marketplace within the Azure portal — a customer finds you, fills out a form stating their need, and provides contact details. **Sales & Marketing leads** are leads sent from Microsoft CRM and cover Qualified leads, SMB Opportunities and Co-Marketing leads. **Joint Planning leads** come from Microsoft SMC-Corporate sellers and are intended to align customer territory planning and support co-selling success. A **Favorites** tab collects anything you have hearted from the other tabs.

## What is the difference between an SMB Opportunity lead and a co-sell opportunity?

This distinction matters and Microsoft calls it out directly. **SMB Opportunities** are leads from Microsoft sellers who have gathered requirements from a customer and are helping them find the right partner to solve the problem — the seller hands it over. Microsoft states that "SMB Opportunities leads aren't the same as co-sell opportunities, in which a Microsoft sales representative is actively engaged with the partner and customer until the deal closes." A **Qualified lead** is similar in nature: it comes from a Microsoft employee who received a customer request through a marketing channel. Both are handoffs. A co-sell opportunity is a joint motion. Treat an SMB Opportunity as yours to run, and a co-sell opportunity as one where you can ask Microsoft for specific help.

## What is an offer lead versus a profile lead?

There are two lead types in the Marketplace leads tab. **Offer leads** are generated for offers you publish through Microsoft Marketplace — a customer browses the storefront, the Marketplace within the Azure portal, or the AI Apps and Agents category, expresses intent to acquire your offer, and a lead is created. **Profile leads** are generated from your published business profile — all requests originating from a profile listing on Microsoft Marketplace arrive in the Leads section of the Referrals workspace. The practical difference is that offer leads require you to have published an offer, while profile leads only require a published business profile, which is why a services partner with no marketplace offer can still receive Microsoft-sourced leads.

## Do all leads require a response?

No, and knowing which ones do saves wasted effort. Microsoft states that marketplace offer leads generated when a customer selects a call-to-action such as **Get it now** or **Free trial** "don't require any action from partners" — these are informational, intended to help partners reach out for cross-sell and up-sell. They remain available for **six months**, after which they are archived. The leads that do carry an obligation are the ones with a lifecycle: profile leads and *Contact me* leads in the offer leads space carry Received / Accepted / Archived statuses, and Microsoft's marketplace guidance says customers submitting Contact me leads expect a swift response and recommends following up **within 24 hours**.

## What is the lead lifecycle in Partner Center?

Every actionable lead moves through three stages. In the **Received** stage a new lead has arrived from a customer or a Microsoft employee; you review the detail, may contact the customer, and take one of two actions — accept or decline. Accepting means naming the deal, giving the estimated value, team members and estimated purchase time frame, adding notes about what the customer is looking for, optionally recording a marketing campaign ID and your own CRM ID, and adding contacts from your company. Declining means selecting a reason and adding notes, after which the lead is archived as **Declined** and the customer is notified to choose a different partner. **Archived** is the terminal stage, showing one of Won, Lost, Declined or Expired; no action is possible on archived leads.

## How long do I have to respond to a lead before it expires?

**14 days.** Microsoft states that if you don't respond to a lead within 14 days it gets archived as **Expired**, and either Microsoft or the partner who sent you the opportunity is notified. Expired leads do not display contact information, so the commercial value is gone as well as the record. The same 14-day window applies to inbound co-sell opportunities. This is the hardest SLA in the referrals system and it is the one most partners breach, usually because notifications route to an inbox nobody watches — which is why keeping the Referrals admin contact and preferred email current is an operational priority rather than an administrative detail.

## What does the flame icon on a referral mean?

It means the customer has explicitly asked for interested partners to contact them directly, and it comes with an informal clock. Microsoft's guidance: when this happens an alert with a **flame** icon appears at the top of the page, and "we strongly recommend that you contact the customer as quickly as possible to improve your chances of winning the deal." After **72 hours** the referral remains active but the icon and message change — you should still contact the customer if you are interested. Treat the flame as the highest-intent signal in the workspace: the customer has raised their hand and asked to be called, and the 72-hour mark is Microsoft telling you the window of advantage has closed even though the lead has not.

## What are the qualification and evaluation substages?

Microsoft introduced mandatory substages between accepting a lead and closing it, and they gate the close. Once a lead is accepted you **qualify** it if the customer has responded, and **evaluate** it if the BANT criteria (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) have been identified. Microsoft states that "completing these stages is mandatory; without them, the **Won** and **Convert to Opportunity** buttons remain disabled." After completing the substages you mark the lead **Won** or **Lost** so it can be archived accordingly. The stated purpose is to keep Microsoft sellers informed and help partners engage with customers — practically, it means a partner cannot report a win without having recorded that they actually worked the lead.

## How do I know a lead's contact details are real?

Microsoft validates the email addresses and phone numbers on Marketplace offer leads and surfaces the result. Leads carry **Email validity** and **Phone validity** indicators with values of Valid, Invalid, Spam or Unknown for email and Valid, Invalid or Unknown for phone, and you can filter on both. Email addresses and domain names known to generate spam are marked in the list and again in the lead detail. There is also a **Lead quality** filter where *High* denotes a higher chance of the lead converting to a subscription and *Low* denotes a potential spam lead. Microsoft's caveat is important: it verifies only that the individual or company can be contacted as described, does not verify identity or legitimacy of interest, and a referral is not a Microsoft endorsement — safeguarding against fraud is the partner's responsibility.

## Who at my company gets notified when a lead arrives?

Notification routing differs by lead type and it is a frequent source of missed leads. **Offer leads**, **profile leads** and **Joint Planning leads** go by email to the **Referral admin**, with the *preferred email* taking precedence over the listed Referral admin email for offer and joint planning leads. **Qualified leads** and **SMB Opportunities** go to the contact a Microsoft seller added on the lead in MSX at the time it was created — so for those, the routing is decided on Microsoft's side, not yours. Microsoft also notes that new Modern Business Profiles do not account for a preferred contact and instead send leads to *all* Referral Admins. Notifications additionally appear as recommended actions in the Action center panel.

## Will I get an email for every single lead?

Not if you are getting a lot of them. Microsoft's rule is volume-based: leads are delivered as individual emails if you have a low volume, but if you receive **more than 10 leads in a day** they are delivered in a digest format instead. This is worth knowing because a partner scaling up marketplace visibility can suddenly find the per-lead emails stop and assume something has broken. The reliable pattern is not to depend on email at all — review leads in the Referrals workspace directly, filter them, and use the **Download** button to export to CSV for import into your CRM. If filters are applied, the exported file contains only the filtered list, and the export includes a `LeadCreationDateTime` field.

## Can marketplace leads be routed straight into my CRM?

Yes — Microsoft supports sending an offer's leads to your CRM system so they can be managed alongside your existing sales workflows, and notes that connecting a CRM is optional rather than required. If you are consuming lead data outside Partner Center, the field to read first is `LeadSource`, formatted as **Source-Action | Offer**. Source values are `AzureMarketplace`, `AzurePortal` and `AppSource (SPZA)`. Action values are `INS` for installation (customer acquired your product), `PLT` for partner-led trial (customer selected Contact me), `DNC` for do not contact (a cross-listed partner was requested, and no contact is needed), and `Create` inside the Azure portal when a customer purchases your offer. Customer information arrives as separate first name, last name, email, phone, country, company and title fields, though not all fields are always populated.

## How do I get more and better referrals from Microsoft?

Microsoft publishes the behaviours it rewards, and they are consistent across the leads and co-sell documentation. **Respond quickly** — when you respond in a timely fashion, Microsoft increases your visibility in future customer and partner search results. **Be choosy about which deals you accept** — Microsoft monitors what you accept and decline and uses it to find similar deals, so accepting poor-fit deals does not improve your search position and can degrade the quality of what you are sent. **Report outcomes honestly**, including estimated deal size, closing date and final won/lost status, because that data feeds referral quality. And **tune your profile**: choose keywords that represent your genuine expertise, remove tags that generate unwanted referrals, and keep contact information current for each location.

## Currency warning

The response windows on this page are live program rules — the 14-day lead expiry, the 72-hour flame-icon window, the 24-hour recommended Contact-me follow-up, the six-month retention of marketplace offer leads, the 10-leads-per-day digest threshold, the 24-hour marketplace profile publication delay and the 72-hour badge visibility delay. Lead types and tab names also change: Joint Planning leads and the Modern Business Profile experience are both recent additions, and Microsoft notes the legacy business profile behaves differently for contact routing. Re-verify SLAs before quoting them to a partner, and confirm notification routing in the partner's own tenant rather than assuming the default.
