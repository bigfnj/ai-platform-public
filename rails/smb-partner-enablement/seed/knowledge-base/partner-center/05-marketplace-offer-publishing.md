# Publishing to Microsoft Marketplace — listing-only versus transactable, and the offer types

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/determine-your-listing-type · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/publisher-guide-by-offer-type · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/marketplace-commercial-transaction-capabilities-and-considerations · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/gtm-your-marketplace-benefits
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is Microsoft Marketplace?

Microsoft Marketplace is Microsoft's commercial storefront and catalogue for partner solutions — Microsoft describes it as "a trusted source for cloud solutions, AI apps, and agents," delivered both as a central digital storefront at `marketplace.microsoft.com` and as in-product experiences customers reach in their flow of work. Those in-product surfaces include Microsoft 365, Dynamics 365, Microsoft Power Platform and Azure; Azure customers reach it through **Create a resource** in the Azure portal, and Power BI users reach add-ins from the Power BI **File** menu. What was previously described as two separate storefronts — Azure Marketplace and Microsoft AppSource — is now presented by Microsoft as a single Microsoft Marketplace with those surfaces as entry points.

## Why would a partner publish to Microsoft Marketplace at all?

Microsoft's stated case has three parts and they are worth repeating to an ISV weighing the effort. First, **deal velocity**: publishing streamlines procurement through Microsoft's buying platform, reduces customer onboarding time and procurement complexity, and lets customers purchase through their existing Microsoft agreements so approvals are simpler. Second, **alignment to cloud investment**: customers can apply purchases toward their Azure consumption commitment, the solution positions inside their broader cloud strategy, and the partner can co-sell with Microsoft account executives. Third, **reach**: Microsoft states partners can sell to customers across **141 geographies** and simplify taxation when selling in dozens of Microsoft-managed markets.

## What is the difference between a listing-only offer and a transactable offer?

The distinction is who handles the money. A **transactable** offer is one where, in Microsoft's words, "Microsoft facilitates the exchange of money for a software license on the publisher's behalf" — transact offers bill against an existing Microsoft subscription or credit card, so Microsoft hosts the transaction and is responsible for billing and collections. A **listing-only** offer, which covers free and bring-your-own-licence scenarios, just presents the solution; the publisher handles the commercial transaction outside Microsoft. This single choice cascades: transactability is a requirement for Azure IP co-sell eligibility on new offers, it is a prerequisite for MACC enrolment, and it determines which Marketplace Rewards benefits you unlock.

## What are the three listing options and what do they do?

Every offer picks from three listing options, which become the buttons on the offer's listing page. **Contact Me** creates a simple listing where customers request direct communication with you and can write notes describing their needs — this is a lead-generation listing, not a sale. **Free Trial** enhances discoverability and automates provisioning of a trial experience, letting prospects use your SaaS, IaaS or in-app experience free for a limited time. **Get It Now** is the acquisition button, and it covers several models: Get It Now (Free), Bring Your Own License (BYOL), Subscription, and Usage-based pricing. Only the transactable variants of Get It Now involve Microsoft in the money.

## Which listing options are available for which offer type?

Availability varies significantly by offer type, so this constrains the go-to-market before anything else. **Software as a service** and the Dynamics 365 offer types (Business Central, apps on Dataverse and Power Apps, Operations Apps) support all three — Free Trial, Contact Me and Get It Now. **Azure Virtual Machine** supports Free Trial and Get It Now. **Azure Application** (both managed app and solution template), **Azure Container**, **Managed Service** and **Power BI App** support Get It Now only. **Professional service** supports Contact Me, and Microsoft notes professional services can be sold through Private Offers. Free trial durations also differ: SaaS transactable through Microsoft runs 1 to 180 days, Azure virtual machine runs 1, 3 or 6 months, and Dynamics 365 Business Central, Dynamics 365 apps on Dataverse and Power Apps, and Power BI visuals run one month.

## What offer types exist in Microsoft Marketplace?

Microsoft documents nine offer types and the choice is the critical first decision, because the offer type defines the metadata shown to customers, the technical deployment method, the pricing and transaction models, and the integration with Microsoft products. **Azure Application** covers solutions needing multiple Azure resources, in two plan kinds — solution templates and managed applications. **Azure Container** is for Docker container images provisioned as Kubernetes-based Azure container services. **Azure Virtual Machine** is for virtual appliances and software images. **Professional service** covers assessments, briefings, implementations, proofs of concept, workshops, migrations and support. **Dynamics 365**, **Microsoft 365**, **Power BI app** and **Managed service** extend the respective Microsoft products, with managed service using Azure Lighthouse. **Software as a Service** is for cloud-based subscription software.

## Can I change my offer type later?

No — and this is the single most consequential irreversible decision in the publishing flow. Microsoft states it twice on the same page: "After you select an offer type, you can't change the offer to another type. To create a different offer type, you need to create a new offer." Changing the *transaction* type is more forgiving but still uneven. Going from Contact Me to Trial requires only republishing the offer. Going from Contact Me to Transact, or from Trial to Transact, requires only an update and republish **for SaaS offers**, but for **all other offer types it requires creating a new offer**. Microsoft points partners at the App Advisor tool for an interactive walkthrough of choosing the right offer type before committing.

## Which offer types can actually transact through Microsoft?

Microsoft lists the transact-capable offer types with their billing options. **Azure Application (managed application)** bills monthly, supports metered billing, and uses usage-based and flat-rate pricing. **Azure Container** bills monthly with usage-based or BYOL pricing. **Azure Virtual Machine** supports one-time upfront, monthly or a flexible schedule, with usage-based or BYOL pricing. **SaaS** supports monthly, annual or one-time upfront payment options, supports metered billing, and offers flat rate, per-user and usage-based pricing. **Dynamics 365 apps on Dataverse and Power Apps** and **Dynamics 365 Business Central** bill monthly and annually on per-user pricing. **Power BI visual** bills monthly and annually per user. **Professional Services** support one-time upfront, monthly, annual or flexible schedules with flat-rate pricing, and are only available to transact through a private offer.

## What does Microsoft charge me to transact on the marketplace?

Microsoft charges a **3% standard store service fee** when a customer purchases a transactable offer from Microsoft Marketplace, and deducts it at the time of publisher payout. Microsoft's own worked examples make the mechanics clear: on a SaaS subscription priced at USD 100 per month, Microsoft bills the customer USD 100, pays the publisher USD 97 and keeps USD 3. On a usage-based VM where your licence cost is USD 1.00 per hour and Azure usage is USD 0.14 per hour, Microsoft bills USD 1.14, pays you USD 0.97, keeps USD 0.03 of your licence cost and keeps 100% of the Azure usage cost. On BYOL, Microsoft keeps 0% of your licence cost because the licence was negotiated and billed by you. Microsoft also notes a **50% discount** on the existing Marketplace transaction fee for private offer customer renewals.

## What are the technical prerequisites for transacting?

Two apply universally: a Microsoft account with financial information, and mandatory financial information meaning a **payout account and tax profile**. Beyond that, requirements are offer-type specific. A **SaaS application** must be a multitenant solution, must use Microsoft Entra ID for authentication, and must integrate with the SaaS Fulfillment APIs — and because Azure infrastructure usage is billed directly to the publisher for SaaS, you must account for infrastructure and licensing as a single cost item presented as a flat fee. For **virtual machines, containers and Azure applications**, the customer's Azure subscription is billed for infrastructure separately from your licence fees. **Dynamics 365 Dataverse apps and Power Apps** require per-user pricing to be sellable, and **Power BI visuals** require selecting "Managing license and selling with Microsoft."

## What pricing models can I use?

Three broad models are available depending on offer type. **Subscription pricing** presents licence fees as a recurring flat rate, flat rate plus usage, or per-licence charge — SaaS plans support monthly, one-year, two-year and three-year billing terms with monthly, annual or one-time-upfront payment options; VM plans support hourly usage billed monthly plus multiyear reservation pricing for up to five years; managed application plans are billed monthly. **Usage-based pricing** applies to VM and container offers, where customers pay an hourly marketplace fee set by the publisher, with partial hours charged per minute. **Metered pricing** uses the Marketplace metering service for Azure Application and SaaS offers to bill on custom dimensions such as bandwidth, tickets or emails processed — publishers can define up to 30 metered dimensions per offer and must report usage events within an hour of occurrence.

## What is a private offer and when would I use one?

A private offer is negotiated, deal-specific pricing or configuration made available to named customers. Microsoft also supports **private plans**, which you make available to one or more customers by listing their Azure subscription at plan level, and which can price higher or lower than the public plan. Private offers are how professional services are transacted at all, and they carry the most flexible contract terms: for SaaS and Professional Services private offers created with absolute pricing, publishers can define **custom contract term lengths from 1 to 120 months or 1 to 10 years**, including non-standard durations like 18 or 45 months. Marketplace supports contract durations up to five years generally, but the maximum for **private plans** is three years.

## Can customers request a private offer from my listing page?

Yes, but you have to turn it on. The **Request Private offer** selection during offer setup lets customers request customised pricing or terms directly from the listing page, and Microsoft notes the feature is **disabled by default**. To use it you turn on the **Request Private Offer** toggle for an eligible transactable offer. Customer requests are then captured as marketplace leads in the Referrals workspace, so your sales team can engage the buyer and build a tailored private offer. For a partner running an enterprise-style motion through a marketplace listing, this is the mechanism that turns a self-service storefront into a negotiated sale without leaving Microsoft's commerce rails.

## What do I get back from Microsoft for publishing — Marketplace Rewards?

Marketplace Rewards is the sales and marketing benefit set that accrues based on your marketplace engagement and sales, visible on the **Benefits → Marketing benefits** page in Partner Center once your offer is live. Microsoft has folded Marketplace Rewards into **ISV Success**, a pathway within the AI Cloud Partner Program available at no cost to all participants. The minimum requirement to publish is a Partner ID, and Microsoft states these benefits are available to all partners regardless of competency, Solutions Partner designation, Action Pack status or partner type. To activate them you must first assign a company marketing contact, using an email address different from the one associated with the Partner Center account; a Rewards team member contacts them within a week.

## How do Marketplace Rewards differ for listing-only versus transactable offers?

Benefits are differentiated by whether the offer is **List, Trial, or Transact**. List, Trial and Consulting offers receive benefits once every 12 months at the partner level. **Transactable offers are eligible for evergreen benefit engagement based on trailing-12-month performance** — as you grow, you unlock greater benefits by tier of Marketplace Billed Sales, Business Applications solution value, or Teams App monthly active users. Microsoft also gives transacting partners a dedicated **Engagement Manager** to help prioritise and maximise benefits. Two categories are explicitly excluded from transactable-offer benefits: **Get It Now (Free)** offers are categorised as *Trial* because there is no transaction through the storefront, and **BYOL** offers are categorised as *List* for the same reason.

## Does ISV Success cover professional services offers?

Only conditionally, and this catches services-led partners out. Microsoft states that ISV Success supports software development companies offering transactable professional services "only if it's attached to a transactable app owned by the same software development company," and that publishing a professional service on its own does not qualify for enrolment or renewal. For Marketplace Rewards, Marketplace Billed Sales from professional services do count — but only for software development companies that also have transactable apps. Microsoft is explicit that **service-only partners aren't eligible, and sponsorship for services-only deals isn't provided**. A pure services partner should therefore look to Services co-sell and Solutions Partner designations rather than to marketplace publishing for Microsoft-side support.

## What is the sequence for getting an offer live and co-sell ready?

The dependency chain runs in one direction and skipping a step blocks the next. First, create a **Microsoft Marketplace account in Partner Center** and ensure the account is enrolled in the Microsoft Marketplace program, including completing the verification process. Second, decide the **publishing option** (listing option) and then the **offer type**, remembering the offer type is permanent. Third, build and publish the offer so it is **live** in Microsoft Marketplace — co-sell cannot be configured on an offer that is not live. Fourth, complete the **Co-sell → Solutions** page with listing details, the required documents and a sales contact for each geography, then submit for review. Fifth, if you are pursuing Azure IP co-sell eligibility, the offer must additionally be transactable and meet the revenue and technical validation requirements.

## Currency warning

Marketplace commercials and mechanics change: the **3% store service fee**, the **50%** private-offer renewal fee discount, the **141 geographies** figure, free trial durations, the **30 metered dimensions per offer** cap, contract duration limits (five years generally, three years for private plans, 1–120 months for SaaS and Professional Services private offers with absolute pricing), and the Marketplace Rewards tier structure are all program terms Microsoft revises. Storefront branding has also shifted — Azure Marketplace and AppSource are now presented as Microsoft Marketplace, and older partner material will use the previous names.
