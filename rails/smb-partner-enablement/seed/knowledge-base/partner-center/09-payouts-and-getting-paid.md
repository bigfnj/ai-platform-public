# Payouts — how a Microsoft partner actually gets paid

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/marketplace-get-paid · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/payout-policy-details · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/payment-thresholds-methods-timeframes · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/earnings/earnings · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/earnings/earnings-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/marketplace-commercial-transaction-capabilities-and-considerations

## Where does a partner see what they have earned and been paid?

In the **Earnings** workspace, which Microsoft describes as providing insights and detailed reports on earnings and payments, reconciliation details for all incentive programs plus Microsoft Marketplace and store programs, and revenue and usage eligibility outcomes for Microsoft commerce incentives. It replaces the older separate transaction history and payment pages with a single view. The roles required differ by program: for **Incentives** you need Incentives admin or Incentives user; for **Microsoft Marketplace and Store** you need Owner or Financial contributor. The workspace has three pages — **Earnings** for the combined earnings and payments view, **Revenue** for MCI eligibility outcomes, and **Reports** as the central download hub.

## What do I have to set up before Microsoft can pay me?

Three things, and a missing one is the most common cause of a payment never arriving. First, you must **accept the Microsoft Publisher Agreement (MPA)** — Microsoft states you must accept it before you can be paid, and it is the agreement that also defines the store fee Microsoft charges on every sale. Second, you must **set up your payout account**, providing your preferred payment method and bank details. Third, you must **complete your tax profile**, providing tax withholding information. Microsoft's phrasing is unambiguous: "Before getting paid the first time, you must set up your payout account and complete the bank and tax forms." For transactable marketplace offers, a payout account and tax profile are listed as mandatory financial information.

## When does Microsoft send payments?

Monthly. Microsoft's general rule is that "payments happen monthly," that payments due are "typically sent by the 15th day of the month," and that they then take a further 3 to 10 business days to reach the payout account. Payment timing is affected by customer payment terms — if a customer has non-standard terms such as Net 45 or Net 60, payout times are impacted accordingly. Microsoft also notes that all publishers who transact through Microsoft Marketplace are contracting with **Microsoft Corporation, a US entity**, and that payments on global marketplace sales are made by Microsoft Corporation from its US legal entity address, which is why Forms W-8 and W-9 determine applicable withholding.

## How long after a customer buys do I get paid — Enterprise Agreement?

For Enterprise Agreement customers, Microsoft pays out before it collects from the customer, which makes the cycle short. For **orders and subscriptions**: the customer buys in Month 1 and the transaction is reported in Month 1 with paymentStatus *Unprocessed*; the payout is prepared in the first week of Month 2 and moves to *Upcoming*; and the payout is sent in Month 2, no later than the 15th, moving to *Sent*. Microsoft then collects from the customer across Months 3 to 12. For **usage-based** EA transactions there is one extra month: usage occurs in Month 1, Microsoft calculates the billing amount and reports the transaction in Month 2, prepares the payout in the first week of Month 3, and sends it in Month 3 no later than the 15th.

## How long after a customer buys do I get paid — Microsoft Customer Agreement or CSP?

Longer, because for MCA and CSP transactions Microsoft pays only after collecting from the customer. Microsoft states that these transactions "become eligible for payment once Microsoft collects payment from the customer." For **orders and subscriptions**, Microsoft's worked example runs: customer buys 1 January, invoice sent 2 January, transaction recorded as *Uncollected* 3 January, customer pays 2 February, transaction reported as *Unprocessed* 4 February, payout prepared 6 February, payout sent 8–14 February. For **usage**, the same example shifts a month later — usage in January, billing calculated 4 February, invoice sent 5 February, customer pays 6 March, payout sent 12–18 March. MCA credit card purchases run a few days later again in both cases.

## What is the payment threshold?

For Microsoft Marketplace, Microsoft states the threshold explicitly in its payout-by-region article: "The payment threshold in all cases is **USD $50**." Earnings below that carry over rather than being paid. For **incentives**, the documented rule is stated as a forfeiture condition rather than a rolling threshold: Microsoft states that "if partner doesn't accumulate more than **$200** within a program year, earnings are forfeited." These are different mechanisms and worth keeping straight — the marketplace $50 is a minimum disbursement amount, while the incentives $200 is a program-year floor below which the earning is lost rather than carried indefinitely.

## How long does the money take to actually arrive?

It depends on the payment method, and Microsoft publishes approximate intervals. **PayPal** takes one business day. **ACH/SEPA** takes two to three business days. **Wire transfer** takes seven to 10 business days. These are counted from when the payment is dispatched, not from when the earning appeared. Microsoft notes the intervals are approximate and payments might take more or less time. Method availability is country-dependent: Microsoft pays by ACH/SEPA or wire transfer in any given country or region, and only some countries and regions additionally support PayPal — Microsoft publishes a per-country table of which methods and which storefronts apply.

## What does Microsoft deduct before paying me?

For transactable marketplace offers, a **3% store service fee**. Microsoft states that "any software licensing fees that Microsoft collects as an agent are subject to a 3% store service fee unless otherwise specified" and deducts it at the time of publisher payout. The worked example is that on a USD 100 per month SaaS licence, Microsoft bills the customer USD 100, pays the publisher USD 97 and keeps USD 3. For usage-based VM offers, Microsoft keeps 3% of your licence cost and 100% of the Azure usage cost. For BYOL offers Microsoft keeps 0% of your licence cost, because you negotiated and billed the licence yourself. There is also a documented **50% discount** to the existing Marketplace transaction fee for private offer customer renewals.

## What do the payment statuses mean?

An earning progresses **Unprocessed → Upcoming → Sent**, and anything else signals a problem or a special case. **Unprocessed** is the accrual and escrow period before a payment order is created. **Upcoming** means the payment order exists; Microsoft says it takes 5–10 days from this state for the payment to be internally reviewed and dispatched, and all earnings in the same payment share a payment ID. **Uncollected** means Microsoft has invoiced the customer but the customer has not yet paid. **In progress** means the order is being dispatched from Microsoft's bank. **Sent** means it left Microsoft's bank — after which it can take days to weeks to reach your bank depending on country, and delays beyond that should be raised with your bank rather than Microsoft.

## What do the problem payment statuses mean?

Several statuses need no action and a few need urgent action. **Incorrect payment** means payment recouping is in progress — no partner action required. **Failed** and **Reprocessing** both indicate a Microsoft system error, usually auto-corrected, with no partner action required. **Reversed** means your bank reversed the payment and it will be sent again next cycle. **Rejected** means your bank rejected it — contact your bank. **Rejected during review** means the payment order failed criteria to release payment. The two that genuinely require you to act are **Pending tax invoice** and **Tax invoice rejected**. Microsoft summarises it directly: "partner action is only required when the payment order is in **Pending tax invoice** or **Tax invoice rejected**," plus when the incentive program is not in the Enrolled state or bank and tax details need updating.

## Why would I be asked for a tax invoice?

Because of local tax regulation in specific countries. Microsoft states that "due to local tax regulations, partners from the following countries/regions: **India, China, Italy, Korea, Mexico, and Taiwan**, are required to submit an invoice to Microsoft to receive their incentive payments through Partner Center." Until the invoice is submitted the payment order sits in **Pending tax invoice**. Once picked up it moves to **Tax invoice under review**, and if the review succeeds the payment moves to **Sent**. If requirements are not met it moves to **Tax invoice rejected**, **all pending payments are held until the review completes**, and Microsoft contacts the partner with the exact corrections required. Partners in these six markets should treat tax invoice submission as part of the payment process rather than an exception.

## Can I lose earnings I have already accrued?

Yes, in two documented ways, both tied to missing or invalid payment details. **Forfeited** applies to **non-US incentives partners**: when Microsoft does not have valid bank or tax instruments to process a payment, it holds the payment and continues attempting to process for up to **180 days** after the initial failure — the typical SLA is 45 days — and if valid instruments still are not provided, Microsoft forfeits the earnings. The same status also applies where a partner does not accumulate more than **$200** within a program year. **Escheated** applies to **US-based incentives partners**: if there are no earnings for 18 consecutive months, earnings that could not be processed for more than 45–180 days after the original earning period are sent to the Unclaimed Property team based in Fargo.

## What happens if Microsoft's customer never pays?

Microsoft may claw the money back from you, because on Enterprise Agreement transactions it often pays the publisher before collecting. Microsoft describes a collections process taking approximately four months with persistent customer communication, after which unrecovered funds are written off as non-collectable. Recoupment then happens one of two ways: Microsoft may subtract the unpaid amounts from future payouts — Microsoft's example is that if USD 1,000 is written off, your future payouts are withheld until the USD 1,000 is recovered — or Microsoft may request a refund or invoice you. In the published example timeline, a payout sent 15 October leads to a customer termination notice on 1 February, write-off on 15 February, a negative transaction visible on 1 March, and payouts withheld from 15 March until the balance clears.

## Can I pause my payments?

Yes. Microsoft sends payments monthly but you can put them on hold: go to **Settings → Account settings → Payout and tax profile assignment**, find the program, and select the **Hold my Payment** check box. Earnings continue to display on the Payouts page but no payments are sent until you remove the hold. You can change hold status at any time, and the change affects the *next* monthly payment — Microsoft's example is that to start holding in April you set the hold before the end of March, and to resume in June you clear it before the end of May. One important detail: **holds are applied per program individually** — advertising, Microsoft Store and Microsoft Marketplace are separate — so holding all payments means setting the hold on each program.

## How do I reconcile a specific payment back to the deals that generated it?

Use the **payment ID**, which appears both in Partner Center and on your bank statement. Microsoft creates one payment order per enrolled participant ID, program and earning type, so earnings for the same program, enrolment identity, earning type and maturity period are usually lumped into a single payment. Selecting the payment ID filters the whole Earnings page — including the *by customer* and *by program/engagement/lever* summaries — down to that payment. For payments older than six months, set the time filter to 36 months before applying the payment ID filter. One exception: **claim payments cannot be tied back to co-op earnings**, because co-op earnings move from unprocessed to upcoming and are then consumed through the claim process rather than maturing into a payment.

## Which reports show the payout detail?

Five detailed downloads sit on the Earnings page: **Earnings-Default**, **Earnings-Growth**, **Earnings-Marketplace**, **Earnings-Store** and **Earnings-Payments**. The Earnings report is returned as an Excel spreadsheet and includes, per transaction, the earning and participant IDs, program name, transaction ID, currency, date, exchange rate and amount, the **lever**, the **earningRate** (97 in Microsoft's marketplace sample, reflecting the 3% store fee), earning type and amount, payment ID and **paymentStatus** with a description, customer and product identifiers, **storeFee**, tax fields, and whether the transaction was a private offer. Payment-level documents also attach in the Payment summary — a **payment remittance letter** for incentives partners, a **service fee tax attachment** for Marketplace and Store partners where applicable, and a **credit note link** where the payment method is Credit Note.

## Is there an API for payouts?

Yes. Microsoft documents a **Partner Payouts API** that lets you systematically pull payout reports rather than downloading them by hand, alongside the ISV Revenue API and the marketplace analytics APIs for revenue-stream management. Microsoft positions these as the way to manage transactional history, forecasting and other publishing needs — including assessing the creditworthiness of customers you continue selling to, which matters given the recoupment risk on non-payment. Partner Center also exposes the **Revenue dashboard** for tracing invoice created dates and due dates, the **Downloads hub** for CSV extraction, and the Usage and Orders dashboards, all of which Microsoft's payout timing tables reference at specific steps in the payment lifecycle.

## Currency warning

Payout terms are commercial and Microsoft revises them: the **3%** store service fee, the **50%** private-offer renewal fee discount, the **USD $50** marketplace payment threshold, the **$200** incentives program-year floor, the **by the 15th** monthly payment target, the **5–10 day** Upcoming-to-dispatch window, the PayPal/ACH-SEPA/wire arrival ranges of **1 / 2–3 / 7–10** business days, the **180-day** forfeiture period with its 45-day typical SLA, the **18-month** escheatment condition for US incentives partners, and the roughly **four-month** customer collections process. The list of tax-invoice countries (India, China, Italy, Korea, Mexico, Taiwan) is also subject to change with local regulation. This reflects Microsoft Learn as retrieved on 14 August 2026, with the payout schedules article carrying a Microsoft date of 2025-10-22. Payment method availability is country-specific — always check Microsoft's per-region payout table for the partner's own market rather than assuming.
