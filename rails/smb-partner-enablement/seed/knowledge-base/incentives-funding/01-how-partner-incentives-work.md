# How Microsoft partner incentives work: earning, eligibility and payout

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/incentives-get-started-intro · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/incentives-enroll · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/incentives-determined-your-program-eligibility · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/earnings/earnings
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Currency warning

**Incentive rates, eligibility thresholds and program structures change every Microsoft fiscal year, and the specific rates are partner-confidential.** Nothing in this file states an incentive rate or margin percentage, because Microsoft does not publish them openly. The rates that apply to your organization live in your incentive program guide, reachable from the Microsoft investments and incentives page and from the Resources column on the Partner Center Incentives enrollment status page, and on each MCI engagement's Summary page in Partner Center. Verify against those sources before quoting any number to a customer or building a margin model. Everything below describes *mechanics*, which are stable, rather than rates, which are not.

## What are Microsoft partner incentives?

Incentives are programs that reward a partner in the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program for delivering services to customers, and Microsoft positions them as a way to expand your business rather than as a pure rebate. They are not automatic: a partner has to be eligible for a given program, be enrolled in it, have complete and valid bank and tax profiles attached to the enrolled location, and then meet the earning conditions of the specific engagement or lever. Miss any one of those four and the earning either never calculates or calculates but never pays. A partner who is "not getting their incentives" is almost always failing one of those four checks rather than being underpaid.

## Who inside a partner organization can work with incentives?

Two roles govern everything. The **Incentives admin** can enroll in incentive programs and enter and edit bank and tax information for incentive payments. The **Incentives user** can view incentives earnings, payment details and reports, but cannot edit bank or tax details. Only an Incentives admin can enroll — if a location has no Incentives admin assigned, the Account admin must assign one, and incentive roles must be assigned per Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program location, under each incentive program. To find who holds the relevant admin role, go to Partner Center Settings > Account settings > User Management and filter on Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin; a partner admin or Account admin can then assign the Incentives admin role.

## How does a partner get enrolled in an incentive program?

Enrollment is a two-step process and it is invitation-driven. Step one is user management: the Account admin assigns Incentives admins in Partner Center. Step two is the enrollment itself — once your organization is eligible, Microsoft sends an invitation to the Incentives admin of the eligible LocationID, from Microsoft Partner Center with the subject "Partner Incentive Enrollment Invitation." The admin opens it and selects Get Started, or can complete the process directly by signing into Partner Center, opening the Incentives workspace, selecting Enroll and completing the payout and tax profile for the program. An enrollment is only considered complete once Microsoft has validated the payout and tax profile you provided. Note that some incentive programs have no eligibility criteria and are open to all partners; for those, invitations appear on the incentives overview page but Microsoft does not send an email.

## What do the incentives enrollment statuses mean?

Six statuses appear in the Status column of the Incentives workspace. **Invited** means a new enrollment invitation was sent but you haven't started. **Enrolling** means the enrollment is in an Action required or Validating enrollment state and the current user is not an Incentives admin. **Action required** means you accepted an invitation but may need to update bank or tax information — check the adjacent Actions required column for links. **Validating enrollment** means you submitted bank and tax details and Microsoft is validating them, which can take up to 48 hours. **Enrolled** means all tax and bank information is validated and no further action is needed. **Inactive / Ineligible** means the program isn't open for enrollment, or you don't meet current eligibility — selecting the "See eligibility requirements" link shows the requirements and which you have met. **Discontinued** means the program is no longer offered.

## Does incentives enrollment need to be renewed each year?

Enrollment technically expires at the end of the fiscal year, but Microsoft rolls it forward automatically. As long as you remain an eligible partner with an active agreement, Microsoft continues your incentives enrollment into the next fiscal year and no partner action is required — provided the payout and tax information required for that program remains complete under the program's rules. The failure mode to watch for is an expired tax profile: if the tax form behind your enrollment lapses, the enrollment stops being valid for payment even though nothing about your eligibility changed. Check the Expiration Date column on the tax profile page proactively rather than after a payment fails.

## How is eligibility for a specific incentive program determined?

Each incentives program has its own eligibility criteria, and by meeting them you ensure you have the appropriate agreements signed, Solutions Partner designation earned, revenue forecast and other prerequisites completed. The authoritative source for a given program's criteria is the program guide, available from the Microsoft partner incentives page. For some programs the requirements can also be looked up on the Overview page of the Incentives workspace in Partner Center. For Microsoft Commerce Incentives specifically, eligibility is shown per engagement on the MCI Engagements page — a green icon in the Eligibility column indicates one or more earning locations for that engagement, with the count of earning locations out of enrolled locations shown alongside; no green icon means none of your enrolled locations are earning on it.

## What is the difference between an earning and a payment?

An earning is the calculated entitlement; a payment is money leaving Microsoft's bank. Partner Center tracks them separately on the Earnings page, with distinct cards for Earnings, Payments and Next estimated payments. Earnings carry a payment status that tracks their progress toward becoming a payment. Earnings are aggregated into payment orders, and one payment order is created uniquely per enrolled participant ID, program and earning type — which is why a single bank credit may cover many customers' earnings and why the payment ID shown in Partner Center is the same reference that appears on your bank statement. Earning types you will see include Rebate, Fee, Co-op, Indirect Rebate and Claim.

## What are the payment statuses and what do they actually mean?

An earning starts as **Unprocessed** during the accrual and escrow period — if earnings generate mid-month on a monthly payment cadence, they stay unprocessed until Microsoft creates the payment order at month end. It then moves to **Upcoming** when the payment order is created; from this state it takes 5–10 days for the payment to be internally reviewed and dispatched. **In progress** means it is being dispatched from the Microsoft bank; **Sent** means it has left, after which it can take days to several weeks for your bank to receive it depending on country. **Uncollected** means Microsoft invoiced the customer but the customer hasn't paid yet. **Failed**, **Reprocessing** and **Incorrect payment** are Microsoft-side conditions that usually auto-correct with no partner action. **Reversed** means your bank reversed the payment and it will be resent next cycle; **Rejected** means your bank rejected it and you should contact them.

## Which payment statuses actually require partner action?

Only two, and Microsoft says so explicitly: **Pending tax invoice** and **Tax invoice rejected**. Due to local tax regulations, partners in **India, China, Italy, Korea, Mexico and Taiwan** must submit an invoice to Microsoft to receive incentive payments through Partner Center, and the payment order sits in Pending tax invoice until they do. It moves to **Tax invoice under review** when picked up, then to Sent if the review succeeds. If requirements aren't met it moves to **Tax invoice rejected**, all pending payments are held until the review completes, and Microsoft reaches out with the exact corrections needed. Beyond those two statuses, partner action is also required whenever the incentive program is not in the Enrolled state, or bank and tax details are pending update.

## When are incentive earnings forfeited?

Two mechanisms exist and both are worth knowing before money disappears. For **non-US partners**, if Microsoft doesn't have valid payment (bank) or tax instruments to process a payment, it holds the payment and continues attempting to process for up to **six months (180 days)** after the initial failure — the typical SLA being 45 days. If valid instruments still aren't in place, Microsoft **forfeits** the earnings. Separately, Microsoft states that if a partner doesn't accumulate more than **$200** within a program year, earnings are forfeited. For **US-based partners** (location Partner ID registered to a US address), if there are no earnings for 18 consecutive months, earnings that couldn't be processed for more than 45–180 days after the original earning period are **escheated** to the Unclaimed Property team. The defence against both is simple: keep bank and tax profiles current.

## Where does a partner see and download their incentives data?

The Earnings page in Partner Center is the hub. It carries filters for Enrollment ID/Location, Program Name, Solution Area, Partner Role, Engagement name, Lever, Earning Type, Status and Payment ID, plus a time filter offering 3, 6, 12 or 36 months or custom dates. The Earnings summary tab shows the top 50 customers and the top 50 programs, engagements or levers by earnings. The Payments summary tab shows sent and pending payments, with remittance letters, credit note links and service fee tax documents attached where applicable. Downloadable reports include Earnings-Default, Earnings-Growth, Earnings-Marketplace, Earnings-Store and Earnings-Payments. To see total unpaid earnings, multi-select all payment statuses except Sent — with the caveat that co-op earnings never progress past Upcoming and must be tracked through the claims experience instead.

## What are the four partner roles that incentives are organised around?

Microsoft Commerce Incentives categorises earning opportunities by the role the partner plays in the customer's journey, and the same taxonomy appears as a filter on the Earnings page. **Build intent** (split into Partner activities, Advisor and Build with) covers encouraging customers to pursue Microsoft solutions and proving the solution's value. **Transact** covers facilitating the customer's purchase. **Use or consume** covers integrating solutions into customer environments and increasing consumption. Understanding which role an engagement pays on tells you what evidence you will have to produce: transact engagements measure revenue, while build-intent engagements typically require a claim with proof of execution rather than a revenue trigger.
