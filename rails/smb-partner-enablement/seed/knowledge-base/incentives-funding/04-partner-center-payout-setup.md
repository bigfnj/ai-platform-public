# Partner Center payout setup: bank profile, tax profile and what blocks a payment

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/incentives-create-and-manage-your-payout-and-tax-profiles · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/incentives-enroll · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/earnings/earnings
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Currency warning

**Payout mechanics, supported payment methods and the country lists attached to them change over time, and tax requirements are jurisdiction-specific.** The country lists, timelines and thresholds below are what Microsoft published on Microsoft Learn as of the retrieval date. Payment method availability depends on the rules of the individual incentive program, and disbursement is affected by an exchange rate Microsoft sets monthly — so the amount that lands in your account is not fixed at the time the earning is calculated. Confirm current requirements in Partner Center under Account settings > Payout and tax, and take local tax advice rather than relying on this summary.

## Why does payout setup matter more than partners expect?

Because eligibility to earn and ability to be paid are separate gates, and the second one fails silently. Before you can receive payment for an incentive program for a particular Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program location, you must complete enrollment by associating a **valid payout and tax profile** with that program and that location — Microsoft uses that pairing to issue payments. An enrollment is only considered complete once Microsoft has validated the payout and tax profile you provided. A partner can be fully qualified for an engagement, earn against it, and still watch the money sit in a pending state indefinitely because a tax form expired or a bank profile was never validated.

## Who is allowed to set up bank and tax details?

Only the **Incentives admin**. Microsoft is explicit: you must be an Incentives admin to enter bank and tax information for incentive payments, and Incentives *users* can view earnings, payment details and reports but cannot edit bank and tax details. Incentive roles must be assigned to each Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program location under each incentive program. To request the role, contact your Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin — you can find who that is via Partner Center Settings > Account settings > User Management, filtering on "Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin." A partner admin or Account admin can assign the Incentives admin role to themselves or a colleague from the same User Management page.

## Where do you find the payout and tax section in Partner Center?

Sign into Partner Center with your Microsoft Entra (company) account — choosing **Work or school account** if prompted — then select the **Settings** (gear) icon, choose **Account settings**, and select **Payout and tax**. Two sub-areas matter: **Payout and tax profiles**, where the profiles themselves are created and managed, and **Payout and tax profile assignment**, where profiles are attached to individual programs. A sign-in wrinkle worth knowing: multiple domains can be registered within one Microsoft Entra account, so if you can only sign in with the `@onmicrosoft.com` domain and need others, your Account admin has to add the extra domains to the Entra account.

## How do you create a bank profile?

Bank profiles are created at **company level**, which lets one bank profile be assigned across multiple Partner IDs and incentive programs — although exceptions can apply when applying a banking profile across different countries because banking and tax rules differ. Creation runs across three pages. On **Details** you set a unique profile name, the bank account location (the country/region where your company's bank sits) and the payment method — Microsoft states the preferred payment method for Partner Center is **electronic bank transfer**. On **Bank account** you enter your account information; the fields shown vary by country/region. On **Beneficiary** you name the person in your company the bank should contact if they need to discuss the account. You then select Finish and Confirm.

## How long does bank profile validation take and what are the outcomes?

After you create a bank profile its status is **Pending Microsoft validation**, and **verification can take up to 48 hours**. When validation completes the status becomes either **Approved** or **Action required**; if it is Action required you repeat the creation steps to supply the missing information. At the enrollment level the equivalent status is **Validating enrollment** during the same window, resolving to **Enrolled** on success or **Action Required – Update Payment and/or tax details** on failure. One thing to plan around: **Microsoft does not support deleting existing payout and tax profiles.** You can create new ones and reassign, but you cannot clean up old ones, so name them clearly from the start.

## What is a default bank profile and why set one up?

A default bank profile is a bank-profile-plus-currency pairing attached to specific Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program locations, which Microsoft then uses automatically for subsequent enrollments for those locations. Set one up under Account settings > Payout and tax > Payout and tax profiles > **Manage default profiles** > **Add a default bank profile**, selecting an existing bank profile, the currency, and the list of locations it should apply to. The behaviour to know: the same bank and currency pairing can be applied to multiple locations, but once a location has been assigned a default profile-and-currency combination it disappears from the location dropdown for future default assignments — and reappears if you delete that default. When you later accept an enrollment invitation for a location that already has a default bank profile, Partner Center presents it and, if Microsoft has everything it needs, you simply select Submit.

## How do you create a tax profile?

Through Account settings > Payout and tax, following a dynamic flow whose pages vary by country or region. You complete a **Business Profile** page; a **Setup** page where you indicate whether your company is incorporated in the United States only (or the profile is for an individual) or incorporated outside the United States, selecting the country in the latter case; a **Tax status** page whose fields vary by country/region; and an **Additional documentation** page where you upload any documents your country requires. Microsoft gives one emphatic instruction: **never enter personal information — only enter information about your company.** For US partner companies needing help with the IRS forms, Microsoft links the instructions for **form W-8** (`irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iw8.pdf`) and **form W-9** (`irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iw9.pdf`).

## What is the most common silent payout blocker?

An **expired tax profile**. Tax profiles carry an expiration date, and once expired the enrollment stops being payable even though nothing about your eligibility changed. To fix it, go to Settings > Account settings > Payout and tax > Payout and tax profile > Tax profile, check the **Expiration Date** column, find the profile that has expired or is about to, select **Edit**, and update the tax forms with new details. This is worth putting on a calendar rather than discovering through a missing payment. A related gotcha: Microsoft pays out incentives from different locations depending on the incentive type, and those differing locations may require more tax information under the program's rules — so an existing tax profile that worked for one program may need updating before it can be used with a new one.

## How do you assign a payout and tax profile to a specific program?

Go to Settings > Account settings > **Payout and tax section** > **Payout and tax profile assignment**, which lists your programs; select the arrow next to a program to see its profile details. In the **Tax Profile** dropdown choose an existing profile or create a new one. Then choose the **Payment method**. If you select **Electronic bank transfer**, pick the payment profile or create one. If you select **Credit Note**, complete the verification step, which confirms the referenced SAP number belongs to your organization. Then select the currency and submit. Two details catch people out: if multiple Microsoft business entities are listed, you must select a payment profile **for each entity**; and payment method availability depends on the rules of the incentive program, so not every method is offered everywhere.

## What is the LRD credit note payment method and where is it available?

If your Partner Location ID is paid by a local Microsoft subsidiary for a particular incentive program and that program allows an LRD (limited risk distributor) credit memo as the payment method, your payment profile is pre-populated with the **LRD Credit Note** payment method, showing a status of either **Confirmed** or **Verification Needed**. Selecting Verification needed opens a Credit Note Details dialog where you verify the CSP Tenant ID associated with the location — choose carefully if more than one tenant ID is presented, because that is the tenant that will receive the payments — then select Confirm. Microsoft states this method is currently available for the Microsoft Commerce Incentive program and the Campaigns Incentive program in: **Australia, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, India, Italy, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland and the United Kingdom.** New incentive credit notes are applied to your newest invoice every month. If you are a direct-bill partner or indirect provider in one of these countries and don't see the option, confirm that your tenant ID is associated with the relevant partner location account.

## How does currency work for incentive payouts?

Incentive payments are made **in the currency you selected when you set up your payment profile**, and payments are calculated using an **exchange rate set monthly by Microsoft**. Microsoft states plainly that **you are responsible for any changes in value due to the currency selected** — the FX risk between earning and payment sits with the partner, not Microsoft. Earnings themselves are displayed and totalled in USD on the Earnings page, with separate columns in the payments view for "Earnings (USD)," "Earnings in payment currency" and "Payment in payment currency," so you can see where conversion moved the number. If your company is enrolled in multiple incentive programs you can use the same payment account for all of them, or use different payment accounts for different programs.

## What blocks or delays a payout in practice?

Microsoft's own summary of the pending payments view is the best checklist: a payment sitting in any status other than Sent, Unprocessed or Upcoming means **either the minimum threshold isn't met, the money is escheated, bank or tax information isn't up to date, or the tax invoice is rejected**. Beyond those, partner action is required when the incentive program is not in the **Enrolled** state for the location you are enrolled for, and when bank and tax details are pending update on the Incentives overview page. Two payout-status outcomes are worth pre-empting: for **non-US partners**, if valid bank or tax instruments are missing Microsoft holds the payment and keeps retrying for up to 180 days before **forfeiting** the earnings, and earnings under **$200** accumulated within a program year are forfeited; for **US partners**, 18 consecutive months with no earnings can lead to unprocessable earnings being **escheated** to the Unclaimed Property team.

## Which countries require a tax invoice before incentives are paid?

Due to local tax regulations, partners in **India, China, Italy, Korea, Mexico and Taiwan** must submit an invoice to Microsoft to receive their incentive payments through Partner Center. Until that invoice is submitted the payment order sits in **Pending tax invoice**. Once picked up it moves to **Tax invoice under review**, and to **Sent** if the review succeeds. If it fails, the order moves to **Tax invoice rejected**, **all pending payments are held until the review completes**, and Microsoft contacts you with the exact corrections required. Partners operating in these markets should treat tax invoice submission as a standing operational task tied to the payment cycle rather than an exception process.
