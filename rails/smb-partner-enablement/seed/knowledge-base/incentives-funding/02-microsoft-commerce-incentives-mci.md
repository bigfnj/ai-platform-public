# Microsoft Commerce Incentives (MCI) and the CSP incentive programs

## Currency warning

**Every rate, multiplier, tier and threshold in Microsoft Commerce Incentives is set per fiscal year and is published in partner-confidential material, not in public documentation.** This file deliberately states no incentive rate, margin percentage or earning multiplier. The only place to read the rate that applies to you is the **Summary page of the specific MCI engagement in Partner Center**, which carries the rates alongside partner qualification criteria, customer qualification criteria and proof-of-execution requirements — and the **MCI program guide** at `aka.ms/incentivesguide`, which contains the product eligibility list in each engagement's product addendum. Engagement names change over time as they align to program refresh periods, and lever names can repeat across engagements, so re-verify each fiscal year rather than reusing last year's figures.

## What is Microsoft Commerce Incentives (MCI)?

MCI is the consolidated container model Microsoft moved to for commercial incentives. Historically, every new incentive offer was set up as a separate program or as a lever inside an existing program. Since **1 October 2021**, any commercial incentive offer that is new or undergoes rule changes launches as an **engagement** under Microsoft Commerce Incentives, or as a new **lever** under an existing engagement — Microsoft's own description is to "think of engagements as containers." Critically for a CSP partner: **any transaction on new commerce is only incentivized through a Microsoft Commerce Incentive engagement.** OEM is the stated exception and is not yet covered by this model.

## How are MCI engagements structured in Partner Center?

Find them by signing into Partner Center, opening the **Incentives** workspace and selecting **MCI Engagements**. The Engagements page lists all earning opportunities separated by solution area, and you can pick a solution area to see its engagements or filter by status and role. Each row shows the engagement name, your partner role, eligibility details and the engagement's status. Importantly, the list only displays engagements for which your partner account has at least one eligible location — so an engagement you have heard about but cannot see is one you are not eligible for. Selecting an engagement opens its Summary page, from which you can reach its Customers page and Eligibility page.

## What is on an MCI engagement Summary page?

The Summary page is the single most useful artefact for a partner planning against an incentive, because it is engagement-specific and current. It gives a high-level description of the earning opportunity plus four things you need before committing effort: the **partner qualification criteria** (what your organization must be or have), the **customer qualification criteria** (which customers count), the **proof of execution requirements** (what evidence you will have to produce to get paid), and the **rates**. Resource links sit alongside. If someone asks "what will we earn on this," the Summary page is the answer — not a remembered percentage.

## What are the MCI solution areas?

MCI engagements are organised by solution area, and the Earnings page carries a matching Solution Area filter. The solution areas that appear across Microsoft's documentation and screenshots are **Azure**, **Modern Work and Security** (sometimes split as Modern Work and Security), and **Business Applications**. This matters for an SMB partner because it determines where your earnings show up and how you slice reporting — for example, to see how much you are earning through Modern Workplace and Security workshops you set the Solution Area filter to Modern Workplace and Security and the Partner Role filter to Build Intent.

## What is the Eligibility page for an engagement and why does it matter?

The Eligibility page for an engagement lists every location enrolled in the MCI program, that location's eligibility status, and that location's **payee profile status**. A green check mark next to both indicates the location is eligible to earn on that engagement. Selecting the eligibility status shows the qualification criteria still to be met. The payee profile status carries a direct link to update the bank or tax profile if needed. This two-part check is the fastest diagnosis of "why aren't we earning" — the location may be qualified but unpayable, or payable but unqualified, and the page tells you which.

## Why might a partner not be eligible for an MCI engagement?

Work through three layers in order. First, check the engagement listing itself — a green tick means eligible, an empty cell means not, and for some engagements partner eligibility is **custom controlled**, which means if the engagement doesn't appear on your screen at all you are not eligible for it. Second, if you *are* eligible for the engagement, check the **Revenue page** in the Earnings workspace for customer-level or transaction-level ineligibility; Microsoft publishes a set of ineligibility reason codes there with next steps, and notes that the list of reason codes is expanded on an ongoing basis. Third, **product eligibility is not shown on either page** — it lives in the product addendum section of each engagement inside the MCI program guide, so a product that quietly falls outside the addendum will produce earnings you were expecting and never received.

## What are customer claims in MCI and what do the statuses mean?

Some MCI engagements pay on claims rather than on transacted revenue, and the Customer claims page gives an all-up view across all earning opportunities, tabbed by solution area. The lifecycle runs: **Customer claimed** (you claimed the customer but haven't sent the consent request — this starts the SLA countdown), **Awaiting customer consent response**, then **Customer consent received** or **Consent rejected** / **Customer consent declined** (the customer didn't respond in the required timeframe). Once consent is received the claim goes to **Draft**, where you conduct the engagement, upload proof of execution and complete the customer attestation form. From there it moves to **Submitted**, **Under review**, and finally **Approved**, **Partner action required**, or **Rejected**. A rejected claim can be resubmitted; a **Disputed** claim that you fail to resubmit in the allotted time becomes **Disputed workshop expired**, and **Rejected final** is exactly what it sounds like — the dispute response was inadequate and the decision is final.

## What are the claim expiry traps in MCI?

Three separate expiry states can kill a claim, and all three are deadline-driven rather than merit-driven. A claim left in **Draft** past the deadline specified in the MCI program guide will **expire**. A claim where the partner never completes the first attempt at submittal within the program-guide deadline becomes **Not submitted workshop expired**. And a claim that was submitted, sent back to "Partner action required" by the proof-of-execution review team, and not resolved and resubmitted before the deadline becomes **Post-submission workshop expired**. The deadlines themselves are engagement-specific and live in the program guide — but the operational lesson is that a claim in Draft or Partner action required is a clock running down, not a parked item.

## How do you add a customer to a claim?

From the Customer claims page, select **+Add customer**, then choose the claim type. **Partner activities** associates partner-activity-type claims. **Use or consume** associates Claiming Partner of Record (CPOR) claims. You then enter the required customer, engagement and contact information to complete the process. From this same page you can export claim data, create new claims in the tab belonging to the relevant solution area, open individual claims, filter using the search bar and sort by column headers. Note the separate route for the Online Services Advisory (OSA) Sell, Online Services Usage (OSU) – Microsoft 365 and OSU – Business Applications programs: co-op claims don't apply to those, and you submit a claim by creating a customer association using the CPOR model.

## What incentive programs exist for a CSP partner specifically?

Microsoft's published co-op threshold table names three CSP incentive program participants, which is the clearest public confirmation of who earns what kind of incentive in CSP: **Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) Direct Bill Partner**, **Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) Indirect Provider** and **Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) Indirect Reseller**. All three carry a co-op threshold of **USD 10,000**. Adjacent programs in the same table — with a lower USD 1,500 co-op threshold — include Authorized Distributor (EduWins and Software), Hosting, IoT Channel Incentive, Mixed Reality, OEM Reseller Software, and Surface PC and Surface Hub Authorized Device Distributor. Note that the threshold is a co-op mechanic, not an incentive rate; it does not tell you what you earn.

## What is the relationship between MCI and partner earned credit?

They are separate mechanisms and should not be conflated in a margin model. **Partner earned credit (PEC)** is a billing adjustment on Azure plan consumption — it reduces the effective unit price you are charged on eligible Azure resources when you hold qualifying management access, and it appears in your adjusted net charges on the Microsoft invoice, not as an incentive payment. **MCI incentives** are earnings calculated against engagements and paid out through the payout profile as rebates, fees, co-op or claims. A partner can earn both on the same Azure customer, but PEC lands as a lower cost of goods while MCI lands as a payment, and they are reported and reconciled in completely different places in Partner Center.

## Where do partners find current MCI offers and program guides?

Three sources, in decreasing order of specificity. The **engagement Summary page** in Partner Center is the most specific and carries the actual rates for the engagement you are looking at. The **MCI program guide** at `aka.ms/incentivesguide` carries the product eligibility lists in each engagement's product addendum and the claim deadlines. The **Microsoft investments and incentives page** at `partner.microsoft.com/membership/partner-incentives` is where Microsoft publishes existing and new incentive offers and the individual program guides, including the payout cadence and rebate/co-op percentage split for your program. All three require partner sign-in. Do not substitute a third-party blog post or distributor summary for these — those are frequently out of date by a fiscal year.
