# How incentives interact with deal registration and co-sell

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/register-deals · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/co-sell-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/mci-engagements · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/earnings/earnings
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Currency warning

**The deal value thresholds, timing windows and eligibility rules below are what Microsoft published on Microsoft Learn as of the retrieval date, and they are reset or revised across fiscal years.** The USD figures quoted here are stated openly in Microsoft's deal registration documentation and are attributed as such — but the *incentive* attached to a registered deal is not published anywhere public, and depends on the engagement, solution area, partner role and fiscal year. Do not build a compensation model on any earning rate for a co-sell or registered deal without reading the relevant MCI engagement Summary page in Partner Center. Currency conversion for the thresholds is based on monthly exchange rates.

## What is co-sell and how does it differ from deal registration?

Co-sell is the collaborative selling motion; deal registration is the administrative act of recording a deal you won so Microsoft can validate and credit it. Microsoft defines co-selling as "any collaborative engagement between Microsoft and its partner ecosystem," covering building demand, sales planning, sharing sales leads, accelerating partner-to-partner selling and delivering marketplace-led commerce. Deal registration sits at the *end* of that motion: you register deals you win by providing more information about the contract, which helps Microsoft provide you with more opportunities in future. A deal can be co-sold without being registrable, and only certain deals qualify for registration at all.

## What kinds of co-sell opportunity exist?

Microsoft names four categories. **Co-sell with Microsoft sales teams** means working with one or more Microsoft sales teams to fulfil customer needs — which can include selling your offers, Microsoft's offers, or both, with either side able to identify and share opportunities. **Partner to Partner (P2P)** means working with another Microsoft partner to solve a customer problem together. **Private deal** means sharing what you are independently working on with Microsoft so it appears in Microsoft's reporting for analysis and forecasting, without inviting Microsoft into the deal. **Solution Assessment (SA)** means working with partners vetted by the solution assessments business team to assess the technology needs of customers using or planning to use Microsoft technologies. Note that Solution Assessment deals are explicitly **not eligible for deal registration**.

## What are the co-sell statuses a solution can hold?

Co-sell statuses apply to solutions (offers) rather than to partners, and they differ by marketplace category. For **Azure** the statuses are **In-market**, **Co-sell ready** and **Azure IP co-sell eligible**. For **business applications** they are **Business Applications Co-sell Incentive Standard** and **Business Applications Co-sell Incentive Premium**. The benefits differ meaningfully: co-sell-ready status exposes your solutions to Microsoft sales teams; Azure IP co-sell eligibility additionally allows you to submit co-sell referrals and lets your offers contribute toward customers' Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitments (MACC); and the Business Applications co-sell incentive statuses incentivize *Microsoft sellers* to sell your offer. To achieve IP co-sell eligibility you must first achieve co-sell-ready status.

## Which offer types can achieve Azure IP co-sell eligibility?

Azure IP co-sell eligibility applies only to IP solution types, specifically: **Azure Application, Azure Container, Azure Virtual Machine and Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)** offers. Business Applications co-sell incentive status (Standard and Premium) applies to **Dynamics 365 apps on Dataverse and Power Apps**, and **Dynamics 365 Operations Apps**. This is the point at which many SMB services partners discover the ceiling: co-sell status attaches to *intellectual property* you publish, so a pure reseller or managed services partner with no published offer cannot achieve IP co-sell eligibility and cannot register an Azure IP co-sell deal. Services co-sell exists as a separate motion for services-led partners.

## What are the eligibility criteria for co-sell deal registration?

A referral must meet **all** of the following. The deal type must be either **co-sell or partner-led** (meaning you chose to allow Microsoft sellers to view the deal). The deal value must be **greater than or equal to USD 25,000**, with currency conversion based on monthly exchange rates. The customer account selected for the deal must be **Microsoft-managed** — a company the Microsoft sales team has an existing relationship with. There must be **at least one Azure IP co-sell eligible solution** in the deal. The deal status must be **won**. And if the deal type is co-sell, Microsoft must either have accepted the invitation or marked the deal as won. Eligibility is always shown below the deal progress bar; if the registration banner doesn't appear, the deal doesn't meet all the requirements.

## What are the timing rules for registering a deal?

Two windows matter and both are easy to blow. First, **wait 72 hours after creating an eligible deal before marking it Won** — if you mark it won earlier, the registration might be rejected. Second, you have **60 days from the date the contract is signed to register a co-sell deal**; all dates are in UTC. Within the registration form itself the date fields carry their own validations: the **contract signed date** can be at most 60 days in the past and cannot be in the future, and must be on or after the referral creation date or the Microsoft invitation date, whichever is latest; the **contract start date** can be at most 90 days in the past and may precede the signed date. Get the sequence wrong — deal created, won same day, registered three months later — and the deal simply fails validation.

## What information does the deal registration form require?

Eight fields, several with validation. **Is marketplace transaction?** — answering Yes enables the simplified Marketplace deal registration path and requires an **estimated marketplace transaction date**. **Will the solution be deployed on Azure?** — registration only applies to Azure-deployed solutions; answering "Others" prompts you to close the deal without registration. **Is this contract a perpetual contract?** — if yes, the total contract value is spread over a six-year period to derive an annual contract value, and registration is only allowed if that annual value exceeds USD 25,000. **Total contract value** — must include software and services but **not hardware**. **Solution value** — the total contract value less any non-recurring implementation fee, and must be less than or equal to the total contract value. **Contract signed date**, **contract start date** and **contract end date** (not required for perpetual contracts, where the end date is deemed six years from the start date). **Tenant type** — whether the solution is deployed on the customer or partner tenant. **Pricing model** — whether or not it is pay-as-you-go.

## How does a pay-as-you-go deal qualify and register?

Pay-as-you-go deals qualify for Azure IP co-sell eligibility if the same customer's payments to the same partner, for the same solution, **equal or exceed USD 25,000 in one year**. To register one, indicate pay-as-you-go in the pricing model — which moves the deal to **Review Pending**, because pay-as-you-go deals don't qualify for automated validation. Four operational rules apply: the deal must be **shared before the first contract or invoice date**; on registration you set the sign date and start date as the date of the first invoice and the end date as the date of the last or most recent invoice; the contract signing date can be up to **one year** after the referral creation date or Microsoft invitation date, whichever is latest; and you should only request a one-time update to a previously registered pay-as-you-go deal, within the same fiscal year, when total contract value exceeds **USD 1 million** — revalidations across fiscal years are not accepted, and a previously approved deal not identified as pay-as-you-go cannot be revalidated to a higher TCV.

## Which deals get approved automatically and which get manually reviewed?

Most deals are eligible for **automatic approval**. Microsoft names three exclusions that push a registration into the **Review(Pending)** state for manual review: the **annual contract value is greater than USD 1 million**; **multiple partners are involved in the same referral**; or the **pricing type is pay-as-you-go**. Even automatically approved registrations can be selected for further review, in which case you are notified by email and by a banner in Partner Center, with no action required unless the review team sends it back and moves the state to **Action required**. The registration state machine runs Registration(Pending) → Approved or Review(Pending) → Review(Passed)/Review(Failed) → Closed, with a parallel Marketplace Validation track for marketplace transactions.

## What is the deal registration exception process and its deadline?

If a deal is rejected — either because a programmatic check failed and it was never eligible, or because validation failed during review — a partner can request an exception **within 60 days from the date of initial registration**. A **Request Exception** link appears in the bottom right of the screen, visible only for deals in the **Closed** state (any substatus: Won, Lost or Error). You get up to **5,000 characters** to explain the case, and the exception council can take up to **14 working days** to decide. The rules are strict and worth internalising before you rely on this route: **only one exception request per referral is allowed**, the decision is **final and binding**, support cannot override the council, and Microsoft asks partners not to create support requests for rejected exceptions. Fields eligible for change via exception include perpetual-contract status, total contract value, the three contract dates and (within 90 days, and only if genuinely done by mistake with evidence) tenant type. Fields that are **not** eligible — and will be rejected — include Azure deployment status, solution value, marketplace transaction status and pricing model; each of those requires creating a new referral.

## What is the ISV Connect deal registration path and its threshold?

ISV Connect registration is a separate path for solutions carrying an ISV Connect **Standard** or **Premium** tag, and it applies to Dynamics 365 Dataverse / Power Apps or Dynamics 365 Operations published solutions. **The only criterion for registering an ISV Connect deal is that the deal value must be greater than USD 5,000** — a far lower bar than the USD 25,000 co-sell threshold. There are two routes: register directly using the Deal Registration tab via **+ Report Closed ISV Connect deal**, which is the method for deals that didn't originate from a co-sell referral, opportunity or lead; or register via the Register button in the co-sell deal progress indicator after a co-sell referral is won. If a solution has both IP co-sell eligibility and a Biz Apps Standard or Premium incentive, you register both — and it is worth completing the co-sell registration first, because the ISV Connect details are then prefilled.

## How does deal registration connect back to incentives and earnings?

Indirectly but materially. Registered and co-sold deals feed the Microsoft Commerce Incentives machinery through the **Partner Role** dimension: MCI engagements categorise earning opportunities as Build intent (including Advisor and Build with), Transact, or Use or consume, and the Earnings page carries a Partner Role filter that Microsoft says is applicable for Microsoft Commerce Incentives only. Co-sell and build-intent activity therefore shows up in a different slice of your earnings than pure transactional CSP revenue. Microsoft's worked example of using this: to see how much you are earning through Modern Workplace and Security workshops, set the Solution Area filter to Modern Workplace and Security and the Partner Role filter to **Build Intent**. **What a registered deal actually earns is not published publicly** — read the relevant engagement's Summary page in Partner Center.

## What should an SMB partner realistically expect from deal registration?

Be candid about the fit. Deal registration as documented is built around Azure IP co-sell — it requires an Azure IP co-sell eligible solution in the deal, a Microsoft-managed customer account, and a minimum deal value of USD 25,000. A typical SMB reseller selling Microsoft 365 Business Premium to a 40-seat customer meets none of those conditions, and registration is simply not the mechanism through which they get paid; CSP transactional incentives and co-op are. Deal registration becomes relevant when the partner has **published IP** (an Azure application, container, VM or SaaS offer, or a Dynamics 365 solution), is selling into **Microsoft-managed accounts**, and is running deals at or above the thresholds. Microsoft also encourages partners not transacting on Microsoft Marketplace today to keep registering deals and using Partner Center to share referrals and collaborate with Microsoft sales teams, and to attain a Solutions Partner designation with certified software for Azure deals.
