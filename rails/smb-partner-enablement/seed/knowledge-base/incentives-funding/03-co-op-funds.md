# Co-op funds: what they are, what you can spend them on, and how to claim

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/claims-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/claims-threshold · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/core-requirements · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/incentives/incentives-create-your-plan · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/earnings/earnings
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Currency warning

**The rebate-versus-co-op split, the eligible activity list and the expense caps are set per program and per fiscal year, and they change.** The figures quoted below are the ones Microsoft publishes openly on Microsoft Learn as of the retrieval date, and each is attributed. The percentage split example (60% rebate / 40% co-op) is explicitly an *illustration* in Microsoft's documentation, not your split — your actual split and payout cadence are in your incentive program guide. Expense caps and eligible activity definitions are in the Partner Incentives Co-op Guidebook, which requires partner sign-in. Re-verify every figure here against your program guide and the current Co-op Guidebook before committing marketing spend.

## What are co-op funds?

Cooperative (co-op) marketing funds are funds you earn through Microsoft Incentive Programs by claiming reimbursement for activities that support Microsoft product awareness. Microsoft's stated purpose is that co-op funds help you conduct marketing activities that increase brand awareness and drive growth of your sales pipeline. They are not restricted to marketing in the narrow sense — Microsoft states you can invest co-op funds in activities that facilitate readiness, develop specializations, or attain Microsoft certifications, describing these as activities that "foster focused solution practices and Centers of Excellence." In other words, co-op can fund both demand generation and your own team's skilling.

## How are co-op funds calculated and how do they differ from a rebate?

Microsoft calculates eligible incentive earnings using a percentage split between rebate and co-op. The **rebate portion** is paid to you at the existing payout cadence for your program — it is money. The **co-op portion** is accrued by Microsoft and can only be released to you by submitting a claim for a qualifying activity with proof of execution. Microsoft's documentation illustrates the split as "such as 60% rebate, 40% co-op," which is an example rather than a published rate; the actual split and payout cadence for your program are in your incentive program guide. The practical implication is that a partner who never files co-op claims is leaving a fixed proportion of their earned incentive permanently unclaimed.

## What is the co-op threshold and what happens if you fall below it?

If your co-op earnings are below the threshold in a co-op earning period, you don't qualify to *use* co-op funds — instead Microsoft converts the co-op earnings to a rebate and pays them out at the end of the semester. This is a protection, not a penalty: small accruals get paid rather than trapped. Microsoft publishes the thresholds openly, and for all three CSP roles — **Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) Direct Bill Partner, CSP Indirect Provider and CSP Indirect Reseller — the co-op threshold is USD 10,000**. Most other programs in the same table (Authorized Distributor Software and EduWins, Hosting, IoT Channel Incentive, Mixed Reality, OEM Reseller Software, Surface ADD) sit at USD 1,500. A smaller CSP partner should therefore expect co-op to convert to rebate until their accrual clears USD 10,000 in a half.

## What are the H1 and H2 co-op periods?

The co-op timeline runs on two six-month halves of the Microsoft fiscal year. **H1 is 1 July to 31 December**; **H2 is 1 January to 30 June**. Three distinct periods then apply to the same money. You **accrue earnings** during a six-month *earning period*. You must **use** your reimbursement during the *following* six-month *usage period*. And you can **claim** reimbursement during a six-month *claiming period* that overlaps and extends past the usage period. Unless otherwise noted, periods run from the first day of the first month to the last day of the last month.

## What are the actual co-op earning, usage and claiming dates?

Microsoft's published examples make the shape concrete. Earnings accrued **July–December** (an H1 earning period) must be **used January–June** of the following calendar year, and can be **claimed 16 February to 15 August**. Earnings accrued **January–June** (an H2 earning period) must be **used July–December**, and can be **claimed 16 August to 15 February** of the following year. So the claiming window opens roughly six weeks into the usage period and closes six weeks after it ends. The critical consequence: **if you don't use your earnings by the end of the usage period, you forfeit them.** Co-op is use-it-or-lose-it on a six-month clock, which is why it needs to be in a marketing plan rather than treated as a windfall.

## What documentation must a partner keep for a co-op claim?

Proof of execution, and for longer than you might expect. Microsoft reserves the right to request original proof-of-execution documents, and states that **you should keep all of your original documents for 24 months after the activity date**. The specific proof required varies by activity type but generally combines three elements: a **third-party invoice** or certification statement and report where the activity was outsourced, the **communication material** itself (a copy of or link to the final advertising piece, script, event invitation, screenshot or landing page URL), and a **metric** such as estimated customer reach, number of registered guests, number of calls made, or a click-through summary. Two activity types additionally require Microsoft pre-approval before execution: TV and radio advertising requires completion of pre-approval and receipt of final review and approval before the activity runs.

## What demand-generation activities can co-op funds be spent on?

Microsoft's published core requirements list six demand-generation categories. **TV/radio** (eligible expenses: third-party media placement, content development, agency fees — requires pre-approval and final approval before execution). **Print advertising** (third-party media placement, printed material, agency fees; must carry company brand and contact information plus Microsoft content). **Digital advertising** (third-party media placement and agency fees; proof is a screenshot of the ad plus the landing page URL, with a click-through report as the metric). **Direct mail, email and SMS** (database acquisition, printed material, postage, agency fees; proof of distribution required). **Partner website and search engine** including SEO (agency fees, programming and development fees, SEO optimization and maintenance fees; a statement of work is required for SEO projects). **Multitouch digital campaign**, which combines several of the above and requires all the corresponding proof of execution. **Microsoft syndicated content** is also eligible, capped at **actual costs up to USD 18,750 per usage period**.

## What market-development activities can co-op funds be spent on?

Microsoft lists these separately from demand generation. **Telemarketing and expositions** (agency fees, third-party call centre charges, database acquisition; requires the telesales script with company name and Microsoft content, campaign dates and call durations). **Customer seminars and boot camps** and **tradeshows and exhibitions** (signage, displays and printed materials, giveaways, agency fees, registration fees, external speaker fees, facility and equipment rental including third-party-owned Surface devices, and catering — **excluding alcohol**). **Proof of concept**, which is unusually broad and covers white papers, case study development, market research, Customer Immersion Experience, programming and development including API system development, and labour services by the partner or a third party, plus setup of a technology demo centre and deployment services such as installation, configuration, data migration and testing. **Employee purchase web setup for customers** is also eligible, though not for Surface ADD.

## What partner-readiness activities can co-op funds be spent on?

Three categories, and this is where co-op quietly funds your own team. **Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program participation** covers annual enrollment and registration fees, the membership plaque and first set of DVDs, and the Additional Benefit Toolkit — proof required is the original Microsoft invoice. **Microsoft exams and tuition** covers Microsoft exam fees for certifications and competencies, Microsoft tuition fees including on-demand training, third-party ISV solution training where the solution demonstrably uses or resides on Microsoft platforms, and Microsoft Certified Trainer renewal fees; proof is a third-party invoice carrying the course or exam code and completion date, with an attendee list and per-person cost as the metric. **Internal training and floor days** covers training, tuition, registration, digital training platform or formatting, signage, giveaways, agency fees, external speakers, facility and equipment rental and catering (excluding alcohol) — requiring 100% Microsoft content for the timeframe being claimed.

## What co-op expense caps does Microsoft publish?

Several caps are stated openly and are worth planning around. **Customer offers** and **internal incentives and SPIFFs** are limited to actual costs up to **25% of total earned co-op funds per usage period** (Surface partners can claim up to 100%). **On-site Champ** services are capped at **50% of earned co-op funds per usage period** for all programs other than Surface, which is 100%; the services must be focused 100% on Microsoft products. **Microsoft syndicated content** is capped at **USD 18,750 per usage period**. **Microsoft-hosted conferences** cap airfare/train fare and hotel at **USD 5,000 per person per conference, limited to three attendees, with a total cap of USD 45,000 per period per program** (Hosting may have different allowances); hotel covers room rate and applicable taxes only. **Product seeding and demo units** are capped at **10% of total accrued co-op funds or USD 3,000, whichever is greater**, per program period — Surface PC and Hub partners have no claims limit here.

## How does a partner submit a co-op claim?

Claims are created and managed in the Incentives workspace in Partner Center, under **Customer claims** or the co-op claims experience, and require the Incentives admin or Incentives user role. The sequence is to run the qualifying activity, gather the proof of execution required for that activity type, and submit the claim with the invoices, statements and communication materials attached. Microsoft's own guidance on submitting a successful claim is about organising the documentation before you start — the proper invoices, statements and proof of execution — because incomplete claims come back as "Partner action required" and then run into the expiry deadlines. Note that co-op claims do **not** apply to the Online Services Advisory (OSA) Sell, OSU – Microsoft 365 or OSU – Business Applications programs; for those you create a customer association using the Claiming Partner of Record (CPOR) model instead.

## What is a co-op claims plan and when must it be submitted?

Some incentive programs require a **plan** before claims can be made — a series of marketing campaigns, activities and details describing how you will make the program a success, submitted to Microsoft for approval. You create it under **Plan Management** on the Incentives page: create a plan for the relevant market (setting the currency and the reviewer email, which should be your Microsoft account manager's address), then add campaigns, then add activities under each campaign, then submit. Only **one plan can be created per PartnerID, fund, program and period**. Timing is strict: you must create and submit your plan **by the last day of the program year**, and while you can edit and resubmit for a further **90 days** after that, the plan must have been created and initially submitted during the program year. Once submitted, a plan can't be edited — only resubmitted through the Action required flow. Plan statuses are Submitted, Under review, Action required, Editing, Approved and Rejected. If you don't see Plan Management, you are probably not enrolled in a program that uses plans.

## How and when do co-op payments actually arrive?

Where a plan applies, Microsoft pays every month based on your earnings and the limits of your plan, on the **15th day of the following month**. Microsoft's worked example: with a plan approved for $10,000, earnings of $5,000 in April pay $5,000 on 15 May; earnings of $15,000 in April pay only $10,000 on 15 May because $10,000 was the plan limit — to receive more you must raise the plan limit for the next month. Separately, in the Earnings page, **co-op earnings move from Unprocessed to Upcoming and stop there** — they don't automatically mature into payments. Once you use the funds and the claims are approved, they reappear with an earning type of **Claim**. This is why total unpaid earnings calculated by filtering payment statuses will misrepresent co-op: use the claim management experience to see pending versus claimed co-op instead.
