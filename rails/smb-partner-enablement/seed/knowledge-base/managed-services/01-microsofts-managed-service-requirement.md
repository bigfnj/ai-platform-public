# What Microsoft requires as a "managed service" — and what it declines to define

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/csp-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/direct-partner-new-requirements · https://partner.microsoft.com/en-us/partnership/azure-expert-msp
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Where does Microsoft actually require a managed service?

At the CSP direct-bill gate. Among the direct-bill model requirements, Microsoft states you must
"demonstrate that you provide at least one **managed service, IP service, or customer solution
application**." It sits alongside the two hard commercial bars: at least **USD 1 million eligible
CSP revenue as an indirect reseller at the PGA level over the preceding 12 months**, and
transacting **as an indirect reseller for at least 12 months** before applying.

Microsoft's framing of why is in the same document: a direct-bill partner should "own the
end-to-end customer lifecycle," and is therefore "well positioned to meet customer demand for
managed services." Since October 2025, partners are **reassessed annually** to confirm the
requirements continue to be met, and failing that reassessment eventually restricts direct-bill
capabilities — a partner can lose the ability to make new purchases for customers.

## How does Microsoft define a managed service?

It effectively doesn't, and this is worth knowing before you go looking. The CSP overview links
to `partner.microsoft.com/solutions/managed-services` for the definition, and **that page has
been retired** — the URL now redirects through two hops to a generic cloud solution-areas page.
Microsoft Learn contains no definition of "IP service" or "customer solution application"
either; the three terms appear only as the alternatives in that one requirement sentence.

The nearest thing to a current statement is in the direct-bill capabilities assessment, under
*Sell*: "**Managed services: A well-established practice to complement Microsoft cloud
products.**" That is the bar as written — a practice, not a product, and one that complements
rather than resells.

Practically, this means the assessment is judged rather than checklist-scored. A partner should
be prepared to describe an actual repeatable service with defined scope, delivery and pricing,
not point at a definition and claim compliance.

## What does Microsoft's formal MSP credential demand?

The **Azure Expert MSP** designation is the visible ceiling, and its requirements are a useful
read on what Microsoft considers a serious managed-services capability even if you never pursue
it. Microsoft states that Azure Expert MSPs "must pass a time and cost-intensive auditing
process" — "a third-party auditor who will conduct an intensive two-day audit against the latest
published checklist" — and warns that "depending on your business, the process can take more
than **300 hours** of effort."

The prerequisites stack: active Solutions Partner designations in Data & AI, Digital & App
Innovation *and* Infrastructure; an active CSP contract; an MSP offer listed in Microsoft
Marketplace; the offering promoted on your own website; **four customer references including one
public case study**; and Advanced or Premier Support for Partners covering Azure.

For an SMB-focused partner this is almost certainly out of reach and not the right target. Its
value here is as a definition by example: Microsoft's idea of a managed service involves audited
delivery capability, published offers and referenceable customers — not a support contract.

## Currency warning

The CSP direct-bill thresholds, the annual reassessment cadence and the Azure Expert MSP
prerequisites are all program rules that change with Microsoft's fiscal year. The USD 1 million
threshold in particular was raised in recent years and is reported to have moved again in channel
coverage. Confirm current requirements in Partner Center before advising a partner on a
direct-bill application, and note that Microsoft's own link for the managed-services definition
was broken as of August 2026 — so a partner searching for it will find nothing.
