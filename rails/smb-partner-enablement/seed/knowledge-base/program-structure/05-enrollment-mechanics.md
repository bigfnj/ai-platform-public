# Enrolling in the partner program: accounts, IDs, tenants and verification

## How do I enrol my company in the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program?

Microsoft documents enrolment as four steps, and the whole path requires a Global admin. **Step one** is to initiate enrolment from the Partner Center enrolment page, selecting *Partner*. **Step two** is to sign in with an existing work account or create a new one. **Step three** is to submit the enrolment request — selecting the billing account or legal entity, reading the MAICPP agreement, and clicking *Enroll*; some countries and regions additionally require a Registration ID. **Step four** is verification, which Microsoft begins once the application is submitted. Microsoft's caution is worth quoting: enrolment "requires acknowledgement of the MAICPP agreement. Ensure the enrollee has the authority to sign agreements on the organization's behalf and can produce legal documents during the verification process."

## When does my enrolment actually become active?

Not when you submit it — only when vetting completes. Microsoft states that "enrollment is **Active** only once vetting is **Authorized**," and directs partners to check the current vetting status under **Account Settings → Legal info**. This distinction matters operationally: until the account is Authorized, Microsoft notes that "some Partner Center capabilities are limited," and a business "might not be able to purchase certain offers, publish content, or manage key account settings." A partner who has submitted enrolment but cannot buy a benefits package is almost always sitting in pending verification rather than hitting a bug.

## Why can't I use my personal Microsoft account or an Azure trial to enrol?

Because Partner Center enrolment is bound to an organisational identity, not a consumer one. Microsoft's enrolment guidance is explicit about what not to use: "Don't use Personal Azure free or trial accounts, Azure Government tenants, Legacy EA-only constructs, or Microsoft Accounts (MSA, Hotmail, or Outlook.com)." A work account is one associated with a Microsoft Entra ID tenant, formatted either as `user@yourcompany.com` or as an Azure-assigned `user@yourcompany.onmicrosoft.com`. Microsoft adds specific rules for the enrolling email address: it must be a business work address that is valid and reachable for a verification code, and it must not be a generic role-based mailbox such as info@, admin@, email@ or marketing@, nor a disposable address, nor a personal one.

## Should I enrol on my existing company tenant or create a new one?

Microsoft's guidance leans toward separation, primarily on identity-isolation grounds. Creating a new work account "creates a new tenant and a billing account," and Microsoft recommends following identity-isolation best practice to "ensure there are separate tenants used for Microsoft AI cloud partner program (MAICPP) and Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) enrollments," and to "create a tenant for just the users who do need access, keeping the identities used for your Partner Center instance separate from your company work accounts." One hard constraint shapes day-to-day use: "Partner Center currently supports logging in with only one tenant context at any given time. Multi-tenancy isn't supported."

## What is the difference between my MPN ID, my Partner ID and my tenant ID?

These are three different identifiers that partners routinely conflate. The **Partner ID** — formerly called the MPN ID — identifies the partner organisation or one of its locations within the partner program, and Microsoft's Identifiers documentation refers to "your PLA ID (formerly MPN ID)." The **tenant ID** is the Microsoft Entra directory identifier; Microsoft notes that "the Microsoft ID shown in this section is the same as your Microsoft Entra tenant ID." They are not interchangeable: the tenant is the identity boundary you sign in through, while the Partner ID is what gets attached to transactions, incentives and program enrolments. All of them are visible in **Account Settings → Identifiers**.

## What is a Partner Global Account (PGA) and a Partner Location Account (PLA)?

Enrolling creates two partner identities at once, and they do different jobs. Microsoft describes a two-level structure: the **partner global account (PGA)** at the top, and **partner location accounts (PLA)** beneath it. The PGA "contains the legal business address and the primary contact for your company," identifies the equivalent of headquarters, and is used for membership, benefits and tracking support requests. Critically, Microsoft warns that the PGA "isn't used to associate for Incentives or Cloud Solution Provider programs. Use locations for those types of associations." Each PLA identifies a subsidiary, carries its own Partner ID, may have its own legal address and primary contact, and is what you use "for transactional business and enrollment in incentive programs" — incentive payouts are tied to specific locations.

## When should I create additional partner locations?

Sparingly, and only when a genuine transactional or legal need exists — because locations are far easier to create than to remove. Microsoft's own advice is to "create more location accounts only when needed," and notes that locations can only be used for Marketplace or CSP "once vetting is authorized." There is a one-to-one relationship between a CSP tenant and a partner program location ID. When creating a location you either reuse an existing legal entity or create a new one, and Microsoft warns that "multiple locations can be created using the same Legal Entity. If a company name changes, that change applies to all locations sharing the same Legal Entity." Deleting is deliberately hard: in the legacy experience it requires a support request plus a written acknowledgement that deleted locations cannot be retrieved and that program enrolments, co-sell solutions and incentives tied to that Partner ID will stop being recognised.

## What does Microsoft actually verify before my account is authorised?

Verification is not one check but several independent ones, and the account is not fully verified until all required checks pass. Microsoft lists five. **Email verification** confirms the primary contact's business email is active and reachable. **Identity verification** confirms the identity of at least one user, matched against a government-issued document. **Employment verification** confirms the primary contact is an employee of the business, evidenced by current domain documentation from an authorised registrar showing business name, address, domains and purchase/expiry dates. **Business verification** confirms the business is legally registered at the stated address, evidenced by formation documents such as articles or certificate of incorporation, business licence or registration. **Additional verification** is a discretionary trustworthiness check that may require a questionnaire or mitigating steps.

## How long does verification take, and what usually holds it up?

Microsoft states that "in most cases, the process takes three to five business days to complete," while noting some checks take longer if they need additional review. The most common delays are self-inflicted and avoidable. Microsoft's guidance is to "ensure your business name and address match official registration records exactly, without spelling errors or abbreviations," to use an employee business email rather than a personal or free account, to add microsoft.com as a safe sender and check junk folders for the verification email, and to ensure the user's name on the account matches the name on the government-issued identity document. Only the **primary contact** receives verification emails and requests for more information — other users can complete the steps, but will not be notified — so a stale or unmonitored primary contact address stalls the whole process silently.

## What do the verification statuses mean?

Partner Center surfaces both an overall status and per-check detail, and there are two interface generations in circulation. The overall status on the Legal info page is **Authorized** (all required checks complete, no action needed), **Pending** (still in progress), or **Rejected** (couldn't be completed; an appeal may be available). In the **legacy** Verification Summary, where steps display horizontally, the per-check statuses are Complete, In Progress, Pending Partner Action, and Rejected. In the **updated** experience, where steps stack vertically, they are Verified, In Progress – Microsoft Action, User Action Needed or Pending User Action, and Not Verified. Microsoft cautions that status changes take time to propagate across Partner Center.

## What happens if I change my legal business name or address later?

Any change to legal details re-triggers verification, which is why profile edits are not cosmetic. Microsoft states that on submitting a change, "your legal identity is reassessed. We only reassess information that you changed," and points partners to the verification troubleshooting path if it fails. Who may make the change depends on the program: for MAICPP accounts, both the Global admin and the Account admin can update the legal company name, in Authorized, pending or rejected status; for CSP indirect reseller accounts, only the Global admin can. Direct-bill partners and indirect providers cannot change their legal name at all once verification status is Authorized — that requires a support request. CSP partners additionally cannot change the country/region of their legal address, because it is tied to the tenant, services and billing currency.

## Currency warning

Enrolment flow, verification checks, status vocabulary and role requirements are actively changing — Microsoft is currently running both a legacy and an "updated experience" for verification and both a legacy and a "new experience" for account management, so screenshots and menu paths in any guide date quickly. The three-to-five business day verification estimate is Microsoft's typical case, not a commitment.
