# Program history: MPN → MCPP → MAICPP, and why the old names still bite

> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Partner_Network · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/introduction-to-pcs · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/silver-gold-membership-competency · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/benefits/benefits-faq-new · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/isv-success · https://rcpmag.com/articles/2023/07/18/microsoft-relaunches-partner-program-for-ai-era.aspx · https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2023/07/18/microsoft-inspire-accelerating-ai-transformation-through-partnership/
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Why do I see three different names for Microsoft's partner program?

Because the same program has been renamed twice in under a year, and all three names remain in circulation. It ran as the **Microsoft Partner Network (MPN)** from its launch in late 2010; became the **Microsoft Cloud Partner Program (MCPP)** in October 2022; and became the **Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program (MAICPP)** in July 2023. Microsoft's own current documentation confirms the lineage in passing — the ISV Success article refers to "the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program (formerly called the Microsoft Partner Network)." Older blog posts, third-party guides, contracts and even Microsoft URLs still carry the earlier names, so treat MPN, MCPP and MAICPP as the same program at different points in time unless a source says otherwise.

## What came before the Microsoft Partner Network?

The partner program has a long pre-cloud history that occasionally surfaces in legacy terminology. Wikipedia's history of the program records a Microsoft Certified Solution Provider program launched in 1992, a Microsoft Certified Partner program launched in 2000, and a major 2003 restructuring that centralised OEMs, ISVs, VARs, integrators and consultants under one program. The Microsoft Partner Network itself launched in late 2010, superseding the older Microsoft Partner Program. This matters mainly when reading very old partner marketing: "Microsoft Certified Partner" is a retired construct from the 2000s, not a current status.

## What changed in October 2022 when MPN became the Microsoft Cloud Partner Program?

October 2022 was the structural break, not just a rename. Wikipedia records that the program was "rebranded as Microsoft Cloud Partner Program, replacing 'Silver' and 'Gold' competencies with six Solutions Partner designations" in October 2022. The old model was roughly eighteen competencies, each of which could be held at a Silver or a Gold tier. The new model collapsed that into six Solutions Partner designations aligned to Microsoft's cloud solution areas — Data & AI (Azure), Infrastructure (Azure), Digital & App Innovation (Azure), Business Applications, Modern Work, and Security — with a single attainment level rather than two tiers, measured by a points-based partner capability score. Note that secondary sources cite the go-live as 3 October 2022; the exact day could not be verified directly against a Microsoft-hosted page during this research.

## When did the program become the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program?

July 2023 — specifically, it was announced by Satya Nadella at Microsoft Inspire on 18 July 2023. Wikipedia dates the rebrand to July 2023, and contemporaneous trade coverage of Inspire 2023 reports the AI Cloud Partner Program replacing the Microsoft Cloud Partner Program "only nine months after its launch," effective immediately, with existing MCPP members transitioned automatically and retaining their designations and benefits without action on their part. **A caution on the date:** MAICPP is sometimes described as a 2024 change. The evidence found in this research points to July 2023 for the announcement and rename; if a document says 2024, it is most likely referring to a later benefits or criteria update rather than the rename itself.

## Are Silver and Gold partner levels still a thing?

No, and Microsoft asks partners to stop referencing them. Microsoft's Solutions Partner documentation states plainly: "Microsoft no longer offers Silver and gold memberships. Partners should stop using references to Silver and gold membership in the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program." The wind-down ran in two stages. Microsoft's benefits FAQ says gold and silver memberships "were retired on September 30, 2022," but partners who held them and did not qualify for a Solutions Partner designation could continue repurchasing the legacy benefits package. That grandfathering ended on 22 January 2025, after which legacy Silver and legacy Gold could no longer be purchased or renewed at all — existing purchases simply ran to their end date.

## Why does the naming history matter when I read older documentation?

Because a document's vocabulary tells you how old its mechanics are, and the mechanics changed underneath the names. If a source talks about *competencies*, *Silver*, *Gold*, or *Microsoft Action Pack*, it predates the October 2022 restructure or describes something now retired, and its requirements almost certainly no longer apply. If it talks about *Microsoft Cloud Partner Program* without "AI," it sits between October 2022 and July 2023 and its designation structure is broadly still valid but its benefit contents and prices are not. Only sources using *Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program* are describing the current program — and even those need a date check, because the benefits catalogue was refreshed on 22 January 2025 and again effective 13 February 2026.

## What older names still appear inside Partner Center and Microsoft URLs today?

Legacy naming survives in the plumbing even where the user-facing labels were updated, which is a routine source of confusion. Microsoft Learn article URLs still contain `mpn-` prefixes — `mpn-overview` is the current membership overview, and `manage-your-partner-network-benefits` is the current benefits page. Partner Center's Identifiers page describes the location-level partner ID as "your PLA ID (formerly MPN ID)." Elsewhere Microsoft's own documentation occasionally transposes the acronym to "Microsoft Cloud AI partner program." None of these indicate a different program; they are residue from two renames applied unevenly across a large documentation estate.

## Currency warning

The dates, retirements and transition rules in this file are historical and therefore stable, but the program mechanics they point to are not — offer names, prices, benefit contents and designation criteria reset with Microsoft's fiscal year and have also changed mid-year. This file reflects sources retrieved on 14 August 2026. The 3 October 2022 MCPP go-live date and any claim that MAICPP dates to 2024 could not be confirmed against a Microsoft-hosted page in this research and should be re-verified before being asserted to a partner.
