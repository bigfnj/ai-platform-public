# Solutions Partner designations and the partner capability score

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/introduction-to-pcs · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/partner-capability-score · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/mpn-overview
> As of: 2026-08 (designation article dated 2026-08; capability score article dated 2025-01) · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is a Solutions Partner designation?

A Solutions Partner designation is the current replacement for the retired Silver and Gold competency tiers — an earned, measured status aligned to a Microsoft solution area, which a partner then pays to enrol in. Microsoft describes designations as aligning "to Microsoft solution areas" and including "incremental benefits, so you can stand out from the competition and increase your customer reach," backed by "easily identifiable badges." Two things distinguish it from the old model: there is only one attainment level rather than two tiers, and eligibility is calculated automatically from data Microsoft already holds in Partner Center rather than being assembled and submitted by the partner.

## What are the three badges and the six designation pathways?

Microsoft currently operates a two-layer structure: six designation pathways that roll up into three externally visible badges. The badges are **Solutions Partner for Cloud & AI Platforms**, fed by the Data and AI (Azure), Digital and App Innovation (Azure), and Infrastructure (Azure) pathways; **Solutions Partner for AI Business Solutions**, fed by the Business Applications and Modern Work pathways; and **Solutions Partner for Security**, fed by the Security pathway. Microsoft is explicit that this is a presentation change only: "The way you qualify, enrol, earn benefits, and are scored for each designation is unchanged. You continue to see and work with the six individual designations in Partner Center; the badge is an external representation that rolls up the designations you earned." Qualifying in any one pathway earns the corresponding badge.

## How do I qualify for a Solutions Partner designation?

Qualification is a points test with a floor on every component, and it only has to be met momentarily rather than continuously. Microsoft states three conditions: a partner capability score for the solution area of **at least 70 points** out of a maximum of 100; **every metric in that solution area greater than zero points**; and meeting both of those "on any day during the qualification eligibility window." The second condition is the one that trips partners — a strong score concentrated in one or two metrics does not qualify if any single metric sits at zero. Once qualified, status changes to *Qualified* and the partner can pay to enrol, at which point status becomes *Enrolled*.

## What goes into the partner capability score?

The score is a composite of three categories, each containing weighted metrics with thresholds. **Performance** measures the partner's ability to expand the customer base, through a single metric, *net customer adds*, counting eligible customers added over the trailing 12 months with lost customers subtracted. **Skilling** measures certified people in the organisation, split into *intermediate certifications* and *advanced certifications*, credited from certifications earned by learners linked to the organisation. **Customer success** measures the partner's ability to drive adoption, through *usage growth* (growth in customer use of Microsoft products over the past year) and *deployments* (expansion of Microsoft workloads across the customer portfolio). Microsoft's rule for each metric is that reaching the threshold awards the maximum weighted points, with partial points for partial progress.

## Why do two partners with the same numbers get different scores?

Because several solution areas evaluate partners on two different attainment tracks with different thresholds. Microsoft operates **Enterprise** and **small and medium business (SMB)** paths in Data & AI, Digital & App Innovation, Infrastructure, Modern Work and Business Applications, "each with its own thresholds and qualifying criteria." The mechanics differ by area: in Modern Work and Security, Microsoft evaluates the partner on both paths and takes the higher of the two scores; in Data & AI, Digital & App Innovation, Infrastructure and Business Applications, the organisation is "automatically categorised into one of the two tracks, depending on the revenue and customer base you tend to serve." Once enrolled, the track is fixed for a year until renewal.

## What is the qualification eligibility window?

It is the rolling look-back period inside which the qualifying day has to fall, and it differs depending on whether the partner is already enrolled. For a partner **not yet enrolled** in a solution area, the window is the current month plus the previous five full calendar months, rolling forward at the start of each month — so in August, the window runs from 1 March to the present. For a partner **already enrolled**, the window covers the month of the anniversary date plus the previous five months, and also extends through the 30-day renewal window that follows the anniversary. Microsoft's practical framing is that "all partners must qualify on at least one day in their qualification window in order to enroll or renew."

## What are specializations, and what do I need before I can get one?

Specializations are narrower, deeper credentials that sit on top of a designation and cannot be held without one. Microsoft states the prerequisite directly: "To enrol into a Specialization, you need to already be enrolled into the aligned Solutions Partner designation pathway." They carry no additional fee — Microsoft lists them as "no extra cost. You need the Solutions Partner designation program as a base" — but the extra benefits they unlock are subject to caps by solution area: a maximum of **5** specializations or expert offerings earning benefits under Azure, and **3** each under Business Applications, Modern Work and Security. Specialization benefits align to the Solutions Partner designation enrolment anniversary date rather than having their own.

## What is Azure Expert Managed Service Provider (MSP)?

Azure Expert MSP is the highest Azure-side offering in the program, gated behind holding all three Azure designations. Microsoft describes it as available "after you earn Solutions Partner designation in all three Azure-related solution areas (Data and AI, Digital and App Innovation, and Infrastructure)," at which point the partner can "gain the most comprehensive Azure Expert MSP support." Like specializations it is listed at no extra cost with the Solutions Partner designation program as its base, and its benefits count against the same Azure category cap of five specializations or expert offerings.

## How often does my score update, and where do I watch it?

Score data refreshes on two different cadences, which is why a certification earned last week may already show while a new customer may not. Microsoft states that *performance* and *customer success* subcategories "are typically refreshed by the 20th of every month," with possible minor refreshes in between, while *skilling* subcategories are "typically refreshed within 10 days after certification is completed." Progress is visible in the Membership workspace under Solutions Partner, which requires the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin role. The dashboard shows current status, valid-till date, enrolment track, renewal window, qualification eligibility window, a six-month scores history, and a Recommendations panel flagging where a score is close to a threshold or at risk of dropping — for example when a certification is nearing expiry.

## Currency warning

Designation names, badge groupings, scoring weights, thresholds and the specialization catalogue change with Microsoft's fiscal year, and Microsoft states outright that "required certifications are expected to change." The 70-point threshold and the six-month qualification window reflect the Microsoft Learn capability score article as retrieved on 14 August 2026; the individual metric weights and per-solution-area thresholds are not published as fixed numbers in that article and must be read from the partner's own Partner Center dashboard. The three-badge rollup is a recent change (article dated August 2026) — confirm it is still current before describing it to a partner.
