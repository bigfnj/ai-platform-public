# Renewal, anniversary dates, and what causes loss of status

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/renew-mpn-offers · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/mpn-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/partner-capability-score · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/introduction-to-pcs · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/benefits/benefits-faq-new · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/csp-overview
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is my anniversary date and when does my renewal window open?

Every membership offer has its own anniversary date, and the renewal window is a tight 30 days hanging off it. Microsoft states that "your renewal window opens **the day after your anniversary date** and closes **30 days after** your anniversary date." Microsoft's worked example: if the anniversary date of the membership offer is 1 September 2021, the 30-day renewal period begins on 2 September 2021. The anniversary is derived from purchase — membership lasts 365 days from the purchase date — so different offers bought at different times produce different anniversaries within the same company. Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admins receive the renewal communications leading up to the anniversary and for the 30 days after.

## What happens to my benefits during the 30-day renewal window?

They keep working, which is the point of the window. Microsoft states plainly that "your benefits continue throughout this 30-day renewal window," and elsewhere that the benefits end date is the membership end date plus 30 days, so "your benefits don't expire within this renewal window." The governing principle from the program agreement is that "you keep access to program benefits as long as your offer is active. Once a membership expires, access to benefits is lost." The window is therefore a grace period for paying, not a grace period for the underlying status — a partner who no longer qualifies for a designation is not saved by it.

## What happens if I miss the renewal window entirely?

The membership lapses and the benefits go with it, but the door is not permanently shut. Microsoft's answer to a partner who missed the window is that "your existing benefits expired. But you can still make a new purchase. After you complete the new purchase, you receive a new set of benefits," which then have to be redeemed. For a Solutions Partner designation specifically the consequence is sharper: "If you *don't* renew within your renewal window, you lose your benefits, Solutions Partner designations, and badge." A partner can re-enrol later — Microsoft notes that "if the partner meets the qualifications after the expiration of their renewal window, the partner will be able to reenroll in the designation" — but there is a gap in the interim during which the badge cannot be used.

## When does my new anniversary date get set?

At payment, not at the old anniversary — which quietly shifts the calendar every year a partner renews late in the window. Microsoft states: "When you renew during the renewal window, your new anniversary date is 365 days after the day when you make the payment and Microsoft receives it." A partner who habitually renews on day 29 of the window will see the anniversary drift roughly a month later each year. Microsoft's mpn-overview phrasing is consistent: "your renewal starts 365 days after the initial purchase, followed by a 30-day grace period."

## Why is my renew button greyed out?

Microsoft lists a specific set of conditions that all have to be true, and any one of them will disable the button. To renew or buy a new membership a partner must: link their Partner Center account with their Microsoft billing profile; have a verified account; be inside an open renewal window; and meet any applicable qualification criteria. For legacy Silver and legacy Gold the button is disabled permanently — those packages ceased to be renewable on 22 January 2025. Microsoft also flags a prerequisite that is easy to overlook: before renewing, "the Account admin for your company needs to review and update your business information," and extra documentation may be required to verify it.

## How does renewal work specifically for a Solutions Partner designation?

Designation renewal is conditional on re-qualifying, not just on paying. Microsoft states that during the renewal window "you can renew the subscription, **if you're qualified**," and that a partner "can renew only if they are qualified for at least one solution area." Requalification uses the same rule as initial attainment — at least 70 points in the solution area with every metric above zero — but assessed across the qualification eligibility window, which for an enrolled partner spans the anniversary month plus the previous five months and extends through the 30-day renewal window itself. Microsoft's practical relief here is that the partner only needs to have met the criteria on **at least one day** within that window, even if the score has since dropped.

## What is my "valid-till" date telling me?

It is the designation's outer expiry, and it deliberately bundles the renewal window into the number. Microsoft states that "the valid-till date is 13 months from the date of the Solutions Partner designation purchase, including a one-month renewal window," and gives the worked example: a designation purchased on 15 January 2025 has an anniversary date of 15 January 2026, with the renewal window running 16 January 2026 to 15 February 2026. This is why the designation's stated validity (13 months) is longer than the membership term (12 months) — the extra month is the grace period, not extra entitlement.

## What does "Renewal at risk" mean on my dashboard?

It is Microsoft's early warning that the partner has not yet re-qualified inside the current window. Microsoft defines three renewal statuses. **Not applicable** means the partner hasn't reached the qualification eligibility window yet and so can't renew. **Allowed to renew** means the partner qualified within the window and can successfully renew in the renewal period. **Renewal at risk** appears "because the partner hasn't yet qualified in the given qualification eligibility window" — and Microsoft notes it is recoverable: "if the partner qualifies later, the status changes to Allowed to renew." Seeing *Renewal at risk* is a prompt to work the score, not a verdict.

## What causes a partner to lose Solutions Partner status?

Three distinct failure modes, and they are worth separating because the remedies differ. **Failing to re-qualify** — not reaching 70 points, or dropping a metric to zero, on any day of the qualification eligibility window — makes the partner ineligible to renew. **Missing the renewal window** while qualified loses the benefits, designations and badge even though the capability was there. **Failing to pay** ends the membership at expiry regardless of score. A fourth, upstream failure mode applies to everything: losing verification status. Microsoft requires CSP partners to "pass business vetting annually" to remain eligible to transact, and until an account is Authorized, Partner Center restricts purchasing offers and managing key account settings.

## Does my score dropping mid-year cost me my designation?

No — enrolment is stable for the term regardless of what the score does. Microsoft's definition of the **Enrolled** status is explicit: "You're enrolled in the Solutions Partner designation for this solution area. You remain enrolled until your anniversary date regardless of increase or decrease in your score." Enrolment track is similarly fixed: "once your organization is enrolled in this solution area, the enrollment Track remains unchanged for one year until the next renewal." The score only becomes decisive again at the qualification eligibility window ahead of renewal. This also works in the partner's favour in the other direction — if a partner qualifies in a *new* solution area while already enrolled in one, Microsoft automatically enrols them in the additional area with no further purchase.

## Does renewing automatically restore my benefits?

No, and this is the most common post-renewal support issue Microsoft documents. Its FAQ addresses the exact symptom: "I renewed my offering by making a payment, but my Azure credits, Microsoft 365 license, or Visual Studio license stopped working. Why?" The answer is that "membership renewal doesn't automatically redeem benefits. You must manually redeem the new set of benefits awarded upon renewal." Cloud service benefits in particular need the new 5x5 licence keys fetched from the Cloud services section of the Benefits workspace and activated, otherwise the previous subscription simply reaches its own end date and stops — even though the membership behind it is current. Redeeming promptly after payment is the only way to avoid a service interruption.

## Can I get a refund if I buy or renew my own MAICPP membership offer by mistake?

This section is about **your own Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program membership purchase** — not about a subscription you sold to a customer. If you are asking how long you have to cancel a customer's Microsoft 365 or Azure subscription, that is the New Commerce cancellation policy and the window is **seven calendar days**, not thirty. Do not apply the rule below to a customer subscription.

For a membership offer there is a narrow refund path with two conditions. Microsoft states that to cancel a purchase you must "create a support ticket within 30 days of your purchase," and that "refunds are granted only if the provided benefits are unused." The two conditions interact badly with the redemption advice above: a partner who immediately redeems benefits after purchasing has, by doing so, given up the ability to reverse the purchase. Where a partner is uncertain whether they bought the right package, the sequencing decision — redeem now versus hold for a refund — should be made deliberately rather than by default.

## Currency warning

Renewal mechanics, grace periods, qualification windows and the conditions that disable renewal are program rules that reset with Microsoft's fiscal year, and the surrounding offer catalogue has already changed twice in recent memory (22 January 2025 and 13 February 2026). The 30-day renewal window, 365-day term, 13-month valid-till construct, 70-point qualification threshold and 30-day refund window all reflect Microsoft Learn documentation retrieved on 14 August 2026. Dates that are historical — the January 2025 legacy retirements — are stable; forward-looking mechanics are not. Re-verify against Partner Center's Membership offers page before advising a partner on a renewal decision, because the cost of getting this wrong is a lapsed designation.
