# Azure in CSP: the Azure plan, billing and partner earned credit

## What is the Azure plan in CSP?

The Azure plan is the new commerce vehicle for selling Azure through CSP. It gives partners access to Azure services at pay-as-you-go rates for customers under the Microsoft Customer Agreement, and it simplifies purchasing because a single Azure plan can contain multiple Azure subscriptions — you no longer submit a separate order per Azure subscription. Microsoft describes the new commerce experience for Azure as having "a single global pricing principle that enables Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) partners to offer Azure at the published prices." Note that Azure plan availability is narrower than Azure software in CSP; regional availability should be checked against the Purchase the Azure plan documentation before promising it in a given market.

## What has to be in place before a partner can sell the Azure plan?

The customer must have signed the Microsoft Customer Agreement. Microsoft states that all customers wanting to take advantage of the new commerce experience in CSP for Azure must sign the MCA, and that partners transacting under the Azure plan must confirm customer acceptance through Partner Center or the API before placing a new order. Separately, mandatory CSP security requirements apply: since 1 August 2019 all partners must enforce multifactor authentication for all users in their partner tenant, including service accounts. Microsoft's suggested routes are Microsoft Entra ID P1 or P2 with MFA enforced per user, Microsoft Entra security defaults, or a non-Microsoft solution that enforces MFA for every user.

## How is Azure consumption transacted and billed to a partner?

Azure plan billing runs on a single billing date and a calendar-monthly billing period. The billing period covers a full calendar month, first day to last day. The invoice is ready by the **eighth of each month**, and payments are due **within 60 days from the invoice date**. Charges on the invoice are shown net of partner earned credit — the invoice line is described as "adjusted net charges," and PEC does not appear as a separate credit line. Partners are billed in their own partner-location currency regardless of where the customer sits. Meters calculate subscription charges every day, so daily rated usage is the granular record; the invoice is a monthly roll-up of it.

## How does a partner reconcile an Azure plan invoice?

Two files in the Billing workspace do the work: the **daily rated usage reconciliation** CSV and the **reconciliation** CSV. To reconcile between them, compare the `ProductId`, `SkuId` and `AvailabilityId` fields for each `SubscriptionId`. Within a reconciliation line item, `UnitPrice` is the undiscounted price, `EffectiveUnitPrice` is the adjusted price after discounts, and `PriceAdjustmentDescription` explains what the adjustment was — it may be a partner earned credit percentage or a tier discount such as "100% Tier 1 Discount." When the pricing currency and billing currency are the same and no discounts or PEC apply, the effective unit price equals the unit price. To download, go to Billing > Billing overview (NCE), pick the year, select the invoice number, and download the reconciliation report from the side panel.

## How is the Azure resource hierarchy structured for billing?

Five levels, and understanding them makes reconciliation legible. The **Azure plan** is the top-level container — every unit of usage is linked to an Azure plan. Inside it sit **Azure subscriptions**, which allow allocation and billing to be split (for example a separate subscription for a development team's testing). Inside a subscription sit **resource groups**, which collect related resources such as everything belonging to one web application. Inside those sit the **resources** themselves — virtual machines, storage accounts, SQL databases, VPN gateways. Each resource may have one or more **meters** measuring a specific aspect of consumption, each with a unique meter ID. Not every resource has a billing meter — a virtual network, for instance, has none.

## What is partner earned credit (PEC)?

PEC for managed services is Microsoft's mechanism for recognising and rewarding partners who own IT operational control and management of some or all of a customer's Azure environment. Where a billing partner provides 24x7 cloud-operations management, that partner becomes eligible for a partner earned credit on the eligible Azure consumption. PEC is calculated and applied to eligible resources and subscriptions **daily**, and the credit shows up as a reduction in the `EffectiveUnitPrice` relative to `UnitPrice` in the reconciliation file, feeding through to the adjusted net charges on the invoice. PEC values in the reconciliation files are shown in USD, as indicated by the `PricingCurrency` column.

## What is the PEC rate?

**Do not quote a PEC percentage from memory.** Microsoft's own FAQ answers "How much is PEC?" with: "You earn PEC based on specific calculations… Find the rate on the price list page in Partner Center." The partner-facing documentation for the Azure plan likewise says the PEC calculations and the roles and permissions eligible to earn PEC "are available from your Partner Center price list (sign-in required)." Microsoft's billing documentation does use a **15% PEC** figure, but only inside worked calculation examples ("Example: A 15% PEC earned through managed services…"), which is illustrative arithmetic and not a published rate card. The correct answer to a partner asking what they will earn is: check the Azure plan price list page in Partner Center for your market.

## What does a partner have to do to actually earn PEC?

Access, correctly configured. To receive PEC a partner must maintain an active Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program agreement and hold an eligible Azure role-based access control (RBAC) role — not all RBAC roles qualify. There are three supported access permission types, each with a required partner association type. **Admin on behalf of (AOBO)** is the default when a partner provisions an Azure plan subscription and pairs with a reseller association, which is set up automatically for the first transacting partner. **Azure Lighthouse** suits partners managing many customer environments with granular role definitions, and requires a manually added Partner Admin Link. **Individual user accounts and service principals** also require a manually added Partner Admin Link. PEC can be earned at management group, subscription, resource group or resource scope; valid access at subscription or resource group level earns PEC for everything beneath it. Assignments at root scope alone do not qualify.

## When does a partner silently lose PEC?

Three scenarios, all of which Microsoft calls out. First, **after a partner-to-partner transfer** — when an Azure plan is transferred to a new partner, AOBO, Lighthouse and individual account access are not automatically re-established, and the association must be set up manually or PEC won't calculate. Second, **when a customer removes the partner's RBAC admin access** — the partner keeps the billing relationship and accountability with Microsoft but is no longer eligible for PEC on the invoice. Third, **for indirect resellers**, where PEC depends on the indirect provider enabling RBAC access for the reseller on the customer's Azure subscription. To detect loss early, review the daily rated usage file for the `PartnerEarnedCreditPercentage` field, and set up Azure Monitor activity log alerts to fire when RBAC access is removed from CSP subscriptions.

## What Azure purchases are not eligible for PEC?

PEC applies to all pay-as-you-go Azure offers in CSP under the Azure plan **except**: Azure reservations, Azure savings plans, third-party products marked "Third Party" in the `Tags` column of the Azure plan consumption price list, products in the Microsoft Marketplace (non-Microsoft) price list, Azure Spot Virtual Machines, and any overage charges for telco products. Beyond that exclusion list, PEC only applies to services listed in the Azure plan consumption pricing — only services explicitly included in that list qualify. The practical implication is that a customer estate weighted heavily toward reservations, savings plans and Marketplace SaaS will yield materially less PEC than the same spend on pay-as-you-go compute.

## How can a partner see how much PEC they received?

Three routes. The **daily rated usage file** shows where PEC was applied, or not applied, day by day — Microsoft's example shows a meter qualifying from 7/1–7/3, not qualifying 7/4–7/7, then qualifying again 7/8–7/31, with the effective unit price dropping when it qualifies. The **PEC API**, available through the Azure API toolset, allows programmatic access. And **Microsoft Cost Management** cost analysis exposes a `PartnerEarnedCreditApplied` pivot that is True when the resource or subscription met PEC eligibility and False otherwise, which you can group and filter on. Typical latency: usage appears in Cost Management within 8–24 hours, and PEC appears within 48 hours of access being in place.

## How do Azure reservations and savings plans work in CSP?

Both can be purchased under an Azure plan and paid either once (up-front) or monthly. Their prices are listed in **US Dollars**, and if the billing account uses a different currency Microsoft converts using the exchange rate for the billing month. The formula Microsoft publishes is `Cost (in billing currency) = USD unit price × Exchange rate × Quantity` — their example being a USD 100 unit price at a 0.92 USD-to-EUR rate giving €92 per unit, so two units cost €184. The exchange rate used appears in the invoice or daily usage reconciliation files, along with the original USD price and the final billed amount. Exchange rates can change monthly; for up-front payments the whole cost is converted and billed at once, while monthly payments are converted at each month's rate. If a reservation or savings plan is cancelled or transferred mid-term, charges and refunds are prorated. Remember that neither reservations nor savings plans earn PEC.

## Where does a partner get Azure pricing, and how often does it change?

Azure plan pricing — covering both Azure plan consumption services and Azure plan reservation pricing — is downloadable from the Pricing workspace in Partner Center, retrievable per supported market, and **refreshed every day**. There is no price list preview for usage-based services because they are dynamic; partners who need to track Azure consumption price changes should use the RateCard API, which returns that day's meter pricing. Foreign exchange rates used to convert between USD and the partner's local currency for Azure products are updated **monthly**. Some Azure plan consumption services use tiered pricing, where the unit price drops as usage increases within defined bands — to check whether a specific meter is tiered, search the Azure plan pricing by Meter ID and look for tier thresholds; if none are specified the price is flat.

## Can an indirect reseller download Azure pricing?

Not directly. Microsoft states that only CSP partners with the capability to transact can view and download price lists, and that indirect resellers should contact their CSP provider (distributor) to request pricing details. This is a recurring friction point for indirect resellers quoting Azure: the pricing conversation runs through the distributor, and the distributor's own margin sits between the Microsoft price list and what the reseller pays. Build the quoting process around that rather than assuming direct access to the Azure plan price list.
