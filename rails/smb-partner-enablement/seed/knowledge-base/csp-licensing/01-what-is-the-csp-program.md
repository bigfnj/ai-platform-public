# What the Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) program is

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/csp-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/regional-authorization-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/customers/azure-plan-get-started
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the Microsoft Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) program?

CSP is the Microsoft authorization that lets a partner buy Microsoft cloud services and resell them to end customers under the partner's own commercial relationship, rather than the customer buying direct from Microsoft. Microsoft describes the value of the authorization as engaging deeper with customers, increasing profit by adding deployment and support expertise, bundling industry-specific solutions with Microsoft products, and owning the end-to-end customer lifecycle to meet demand for managed services. In practice, CSP is the transactional plumbing underneath a managed-services or SMB reseller business: it is how you place the order, how you get billed, and how you get paid.

## Why does an SMB-focused partner join CSP instead of just referring customers to Microsoft?

The core reason is ownership of the billing relationship. Under CSP the partner (or the partner's distributor) is the one Microsoft invoices, and the partner is the one the customer pays. That gives the partner control of the renewal conversation, the ability to bundle Microsoft licences with its own managed service, and a recurring revenue line rather than a one-time referral fee. Microsoft's own framing is that CSP partners are "well positioned to meet customer demand for managed services" and that offering deployment and support expertise "opens up new revenue streams." A referral or advisor motion, by contrast, leaves Microsoft owning the invoice and the renewal.

## What responsibilities come with owning the CSP billing relationship?

Owning the invoice also means owning the credit risk and the first line of support. Microsoft is explicit that the partner remains responsible for paying Microsoft regardless of whether the end customer pays: if a customer refuses or is unable to pay because of insolvency, bankruptcy, a commercial disagreement, or dissolution of the business relationship, the partner still owes Microsoft the outstanding amount. Microsoft's suggested mitigations for a customer that looks like a payment-default risk are to put them on a monthly-term subscription that can be cancelled at the end of any month, or simply not to sell to them. Partners are also expected to provide first-level cloud product support to their customers.

## What eligibility requirements apply to every CSP partner regardless of model?

Microsoft applies three baseline requirements to all CSP partners, whether direct-bill or indirect. First, the partner must pass business verification, which includes email ownership, identity verification, employment verification and other due diligence. Second, the partner must maintain active verification status by passing business vetting annually — failing this removes eligibility to transact. Third, the partner must keep its legal business profile current in Partner Center, including legal company name, company address and primary contact details. Changes to primary contacts and address later on are themselves subject to re-verification.

## What can a partner actually sell through CSP?

CSP covers the full range of Microsoft cloud services plus a number of other offer types, including server and perpetual software, term-based software subscriptions, Azure consumption under the Azure plan, and third-party SaaS through Microsoft Marketplace. There is one notable restriction: a partner cannot sell online services to another CSP partner using its CSP partner tenant. If a CSP partner wants to buy CSP services from another CSP partner, the purchasing partner has to use a tenant that isn't associated with its own CSP authorization.

## Which countries can a CSP partner sell into?

A partner's market — the set of countries and regions it may sell CSP offers into — is determined by the partner's own company location, and the partner must hold an active Partner ID for the location it wants to sell in. Some markets are single-country: a partner located in Brazil can only sell to customers in Brazil, and the same is true for India, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and China. Others are regional: a partner in the United States can sell in the U.S. and the U.S. Minor Outlying Islands, while a partner in the Africa region can sell to customers across all countries in that region. Crucially, what matters is the country set in the *customer's tenant*, not where the company physically sits — a Brazil-based company whose tenant is set to the United States is out of territory for a Brazilian CSP.

## What agreement does the customer have to sign before a partner can order?

Before a partner can order CSP offers on behalf of a customer, the customer must accept the Microsoft Customer Agreement (MCA). The MCA is a perpetual agreement introduced in October 2019 that standardises the purchasing terms across buying channels through a fully digital process. Since February 2020, partner confirmation (attestation) of the customer's acceptance of the MCA is required for all offers — Microsoft 365, Dynamics 365 and Azure alike — and a CSP partner cannot place a new order for a customer without that attestation. The applicable version of the agreement varies by the customer's country and language.
