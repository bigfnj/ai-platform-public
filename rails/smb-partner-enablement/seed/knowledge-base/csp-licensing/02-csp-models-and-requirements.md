# CSP models: direct-bill partner, indirect reseller and distributor

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/csp-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/direct-partner-new-requirements · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/enroll-as-indirect-reseller
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What are the different CSP models a partner can operate under?

Microsoft describes two sales models for CSP authorization: the **indirect model** and the **direct-bill model**. The indirect model has two roles in it — the **indirect reseller**, who owns the customer relationship, and the **distributor** (formerly called the indirect provider), who buys from Microsoft and enables the reseller. So in practice a partner sees three positions: be an indirect reseller working through a distributor, be a direct-bill partner buying straight from Microsoft, or be a distributor yourself. Distributor is a specialist, high-volume role; for an SMB services business the real choice is between indirect reseller and direct-bill.

## What is a CSP indirect reseller and why do most small partners start there?

An indirect reseller sells Microsoft cloud services to end customers but transacts through a distributor rather than buying directly from Microsoft. Microsoft's framing is that this model lets you "accelerate your go-to-market with minimized operational complexity and investment." The distributor buys from Microsoft, handles billing with Microsoft, and can supply pricing, provisioning support, customer support, marketing help, technical training and credit terms — while the reseller keeps the customer relationship. Microsoft explicitly positions this as the entry point for partners who are ready to enter the market but don't want to manage multiple vendors, or who don't yet have end-to-end customer relationship management infrastructure. It is also mandatory as a stepping stone: direct-bill eligibility requires having transacted as an indirect reseller for the previous 12 months.

## What does a partner need to become a CSP indirect reseller?

The indirect model requirements are comparatively light. Microsoft lists three: an active membership in the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program (MAICPP) with a Partner Location Account (PLA) ID for the location you want to sell in; the authority to accept and sign legal agreements on behalf of your organization; and a partnership with an authorized distributor. Alongside these, enrollment prerequisites include a MAICPP enrollment that is Active with vetting Authorized, a location Partner ID of type *legal entity* whose country matches the country you intend to transact in, a monitored business email domain (personal, disposable, freehost, government and generic role-based addresses such as `info@` or `admin@` are not accepted), and a user holding the Global Admin role to initiate enrollment. Microsoft also recommends — but does not require — setting up the CSP enrollment in a separate tenant from the MAICPP tenant for security isolation.

## What are the steps to enroll as a CSP indirect reseller?

Microsoft describes a five-step authorization flow. Step one is to start enrollment at the CSP indirect reseller enrollment page and create a work account, which also creates a Billing Account (BA) ID acting as the legal entity. Step two is to provide business and support information: select or create the BA ID, associate your location Partner ID for the matching country, review the read-only legal business details, and supply customer support contact details (support email and, where applicable, support phone) — providing these confirms your ability to support customers directly, which is a key reseller requirement. Step three is Microsoft's verification of your legal details, after which status shows as Authorized. Step four is a Global Admin accepting the Microsoft Partner Agreement (MPA) in Partner Center; neither Microsoft support nor a distributor can accept it on your behalf, and enrollment only activates once it is signed. Step five is selecting and connecting with a distributor.

## Why can't an indirect reseller transact without a distributor relationship?

Authorization alone is not enough. Microsoft is explicit that an indirect reseller can't transact in CSP without an active distributor relationship — the distributor relationship is what enables ordering, billing and provisioning in Partner Center. The connection is established when the distributor sends a partnership request in Partner Center and the reseller accepts it; on acceptance the reseller's PLA ID is linked and the reseller becomes eligible to transact through that distributor. The reseller must have users with Admin Agent or equivalent permissions for this to work. Partners who don't already have a distributor can search Microsoft's official distributor directory, and Microsoft's advice is to evaluate providers on the support and services they offer, since these vary considerably.

## What does a CSP direct-bill partner do differently?

In the direct-bill model the partner purchases Microsoft products and subscriptions directly from Microsoft and sells them directly to customers through in-house sales staff. That means the partner must sell to, bill, manage and support its customers autonomously — no distributor sits in between to absorb billing, credit or first-line support. Microsoft frames this as the right choice for partners who already operate, or are willing to build, the appropriate sales, billing and support infrastructure. One caveat worth setting expectations on: direct-bill status does *not* guarantee that the partner is assigned a Microsoft account manager.

## What are the requirements to become a CSP direct-bill partner?

Microsoft's published minimum bar is substantial and covers seven areas. You need an active MAICPP membership with a PLA ID for the selling location; the authority to accept legal agreements; at least the prior 12 months transacting as an authorized indirect reseller; at least **USD one million** in CSP trailing-twelve-month transactional revenue at the Partner Global Account (PGA) level (calculated on cloud revenue and including perpetual software in CSP, evaluated independently per PGA, with indirect reseller revenue excluded); a passing score on a capabilities assessment; demonstration that you provide at least one managed service, IP service or customer solution application; at least one MAICPP Solutions Partner designation; completion of all mandatory Partner Center security score requirements; and purchase of either an Advanced Support for Partners (ASfP) or Premier Support for Partners (PSfP) plan. Partners can check their TTM revenue on the Partner Global CSP Transactional TTM revenue card in Partner Center Insights.

## What does the direct-bill capabilities assessment actually test?

Microsoft runs an automated online assessment scored against capabilities grouped under Sell, Secure and Support. Under **Sell**, it looks for allocated technical resources with skills and certifications backed by Solutions Partner designations, a structured sales force, automated billing and provisioning platforms integrated with Partner Center APIs, an established managed services practice, and solution expertise across core Microsoft solution areas. Under **Secure**, it looks for MFA across CSP tenants and users, a security incident monitoring and fraud detection system, a dedicated compliance function running risk assessments and staff training, customer business verification and credit-check processes, and the ability to obtain an End Customer Purchase Commitment. Under **Support**, it looks for a mature support practice with ticketing and escalation, certified support staff, and SLA tracking with continual improvement.

## What are the mandatory security requirements a direct-bill partner must meet?

There are three mandatory Partner Center security score requirements, validated annually during the anniversary month of the partner's original CSP onboarding: enable multifactor authentication for all administrative users on the CSP tenant, designate a security contact within Partner Center, and respond to security alerts within 24 hours or less on average. Assessment is at the PGA level, which is the part partners routinely miss — all production tenants under a given Partner Global Account are assessed, and if any single production tenant fails the mandatory requirements, the entire PGA is treated as at risk and may lose authorization, whether the PGA sits in one region or several.

## Does direct-bill authorization have to be renewed?

Yes. Since October 2025 Microsoft requires annual reassessment of existing direct-bill partners during their CSP onboarded month as a condition of continued direct-bill authorization. The reassessment questionnaire covers the same capabilities as the pre-enrollment assessment, and at the same time the partner must still hold active MAICPP membership and a PLA ID, have at least **USD 1 million** in trailing-twelve-month *direct bill* CSP transactional revenue at PGA level (aggregated across all direct-bill tenants under the PGA, with indirect reseller revenue excluded), renew the premium support plan contract, meet all mandatory security score requirements, and hold at least one Solutions Partner designation. Microsoft sends advance notification emails about incomplete requirements starting three months before the anniversary month.

## What happens if a direct-bill partner stops meeting the requirements?

A partner that fails one or more of the annual renewal requirements is required to transition to indirect reseller status in order to keep any authorization to transact in CSP. A transitioned partner must then wait one year before reapplying for direct-bill status. The same one-year waiting period applies to partners deauthorized for not meeting eligibility requirements, and to direct-bill applicants who were not approved for onboarding. Where a direct-bill partner has multiple authorized CSP PGAs, the onboarded anniversary month is calculated as the first month of the calendar year.

## How does a partner move from indirect reseller to direct bill?

There is no automatic "conversion." Microsoft treats indirect reseller and direct bill as separate authorization paths, and you apply for direct-bill authorization separately while continuing to operate as an indirect reseller if you choose. The sequence is: confirm you meet the direct-bill eligibility requirements for the active fiscal year; make sure your Partner Center organization profile and legal information are accurate so verification isn't delayed; confirm you can meet the support and operational readiness expectations; apply for direct-bill authorization through the application form; then, if approved, complete onboarding, billing and invoicing setup, and any financial, credit or tax steps. Important sequencing detail: do **not** buy a premium support plan or submit a Partner Center enrollment while the application is under review — wait until you receive the email confirming you passed credit check.

## What happens to existing customers when a partner adds direct-bill status?

Subscriptions typically are not moved automatically. A partner adding direct-bill authorization must decide whether to run a hybrid model — keeping some customers indirect through a distributor while transacting direct-bill for others — or to transition customers across. Transitioning may require new customer agreements and relationships and, depending on the offer and term, re-provisioning or other transition steps. Note also that being authorized as a direct-bill partner in one region does not automatically authorize you in another: you must complete the full application, assessment, enrollment, business verification and credit approval steps for each new regional tenant.

## Where can a partner get help deciding which CSP model to choose?

Microsoft's Technical Presales and Deployment (TPD) team is explicitly named in the CSP overview as a resource for authorization questions. TPD can advise on the most appropriate CSP model and partner tenant architecture, answer authorization requirement questions and risk scenarios, and guide the design of secure customer tenancies including best-practice access and administration models. For a partner weighing indirect versus direct — or planning tenant isolation between MAICPP and CSP operations — that is the sanctioned free channel rather than guessing.
