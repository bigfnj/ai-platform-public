# New Commerce Experience (NCE): terms, cancellation and renewal

> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the New Commerce Experience (NCE) and what changed?

New commerce is Microsoft's current platform for transacting license-based cloud services in CSP, paired with the Microsoft Customer Agreement introduced in 2019. Microsoft's own framing is that it is "less about *what* partners are purchasing and more about *how* they're purchasing and managing." The headline changes are more flexibility in managing services (controlling renewals, partial-licence upgrades to new SKUs, scheduling changes that take effect at end of term), new pricing and add-on options (programmatic pricing via API, add-ons no longer dependent on a base-offer transaction, new monthly term options), and simplified billing (consolidated in the partner's currency, with billing dates based on months rather than variable mid-month dates). As of **1 September 2024**, all license-based subscription purchases are on new commerce, except for six-year and five-year public sector legacy offers.

## What are the term options in new commerce?

New commerce supports monthly, annual and three-year terms for many subscriptions, and separately supports monthly, annual or up-front billing. Term and billing frequency are two different dials: an annual term can be billed monthly or annually. In the price list the term appears in the `termDuration` field as P1M (monthly), P1Y (annual) or P3Y (three-year). Not every product supports every term — Microsoft names Microsoft Intune, Microsoft Defender for Cloud, some Phone System and Calling Plan products, and various Exchange Online standalone products as examples that do not support a monthly term. Partners should filter the current price list by `termDuration` rather than assuming a SKU is available monthly.

## How do the prices differ between monthly, annual and three-year terms?

Each term and billing-frequency combination is priced separately in the Partner Center price list, and the price list is the only authoritative source. Do not quote a fixed uplift for the monthly term from memory — look it up per SKU, per market. Microsoft's documented mechanics are: list and estimated retail prices are shown for monthly billing frequency, and for annual billing frequency you multiply the monthly price by 12; annual-term (P1Y) prices reflect twelve months, so divide the total by twelve to get the monthly plan charge. Microsoft also notes that slight price differences exist for the same term with different billing frequencies (their worked example shows a UK GBP annual-term product at 3393.9 with annual billing versus 3393.96 with monthly billing), and that partners can always rely on being billed the amount reflected in the price list for the customer market and partner billing currency.

## What is the NCE cancellation window?

This is the window for a subscription you sold to a customer. It is **seven calendar days** — do not confuse it with the thirty-day refund window on your own MAICPP membership offer purchase, which is a different rule for a different transaction and does not extend a customer subscription.

For license-based (per-user) new commerce subscriptions, partners can cancel with a **prorated refund within the first seven calendar days of any term**, except where otherwise required by law. Microsoft's wording is "seven calendar days" and it publishes no hour figure — the "168 hours" and "167 hours" versions circulating in distributor material are third-party restatements. Do not plan a cancellation against an assumed full 7×24: partners have reported the window closing on an hour boundary earlier than a naive 168-hour count would predict. If a cancellation matters commercially, action it on day five or six, not in the final hours. If you cancel on the third day, the refund covers the remaining paid period minus those three days. After the seven-day window closes, cancellation is no longer available and the partner is billed for the full term even if the customer stops paying for or using the subscription — and that applies regardless of which billing plan was chosen. The window does not reopen until the subscription renews into a new term. One trap: a subscription created as the result of a partial upgrade inherits the cancellation window from its parent (source) subscription rather than getting a fresh one.

## Can a partner reduce seats mid-term in new commerce?

Only inside the same seven-day window. The number of licences on a subscription can be decreased only within the first seven days of when those licences were added, except where otherwise required by law — and this applies whether the licences were added at initial purchase, on renewal, or mid-term. If more than seven days have elapsed since the order was placed or the extra licences were added, the licence count cannot be decreased until the next cancellation window at renewal. For software subscriptions Partner Center does not allow licence reductions at all outside that seven-day window. Adding licences mid-term is allowed at any time; it is only reduction that is locked.

## What are the cancellation windows for the other CSP product types?

The windows differ by product type, so check which one you actually sold. **Perpetual software** gets a full refund within 30 calendar days of purchase. **Software subscriptions** get a prorated refund within seven calendar days of purchase. **License-based (per user)** subscriptions get a prorated refund within seven calendar days. For **Microsoft Marketplace** offers, SaaS gets a full refund excluding metered billing charges if cancelled within 72 hours of purchase — after 72 hours you must contact the publishing partner, who submits a ticket to process the refund — while Dynamics 365 Business Central requires cancellation within seven days of purchase or renewal. Free trials on Marketplace SaaS can be cancelled at any time during the trial, but once cancelled they can't be restarted.

## How does auto-renewal behave on a new commerce subscription?

New commerce subscriptions auto-renew by default, and the partner controls this. Auto-renew can be switched off from the subscription details page by clearing the **Auto-renew** checkbox in the Status section. Two behaviours catch partners out. First, suspending a new commerce subscription turns auto-renew off — if you later reactivate the subscription you must remember to turn auto-renew back on. Second, during a renewal event the auto-renew flag, scheduled next-term instructions and suspend/reactivate become read-only until the renewal completes; renewal events begin at 12:00 AM on the day of the event. Partners also get a fresh seven-day window to cancel or reduce licences after a renewal completes.

## What are scheduled changes and when should a partner use them?

Scheduled changes let a partner queue a change to take effect at the end of the current term rather than immediately — this is the mechanism for repricing or restructuring a customer without breaching the seven-day rule. You can schedule changes to quantity, SKU, term duration, billing frequency and coterminous date. Auto-renew must be on for a scheduled change to apply, and you use **Manage Renewal** to view, update or remove an existing scheduled change. Saved scheduled changes are deleted if you then make certain mid-term changes: cancelling the subscription, turning off auto-renew, changing a quantity, or converting a trial. If a scheduled SKU upgrade keeps the same licence quantity, user licence reassignment is automatic; otherwise reassignment has to be done manually.

## How do trials work in new commerce?

New commerce license-based trials last one month (ISO 8601 P1M) and have auto-renew turned on by default, so after that month they auto-renew into the paid equivalent subscription unless auto-renew is switched off. On conversion to a new subscription the term duration defaults to Annual and the billing cycle defaults to Monthly, both of which you can change at conversion. During a trial you cannot increase or reduce quantity. You can convert at any point during the trial, not just at the end, but a mid-term conversion cannot go below the default of 25 licences: converting to a new subscription requires at least 25 licences, and converting into an existing subscription adds 25 licences to it, inheriting that subscription's term and billing frequency. Promotions do not apply when converting from a trial to a paid subscription. If a subscription includes a 30-day free trial, the trial is applied automatically and a CSP partner cannot waive it.

## How do promotions work in new commerce, and do they survive renewal?

Promotions in new commerce are applied to the *partner price* (not the estimated retail price) and are, with very few exceptions, auto-applied when a transaction meets the promotion's constraints — so a partner does not usually opt in. Promotions carry constraints such as minimum and maximum seat counts, term, billing cycle and first-purchase rules. Promotional discounts run for the term of the purchase. A subscription keeps the promotional price at renewal only if the renewal date falls inside the promotion's duration date range; renewals outside that range go back to the standard price list price. Critically, promotions with a **FirstPurchase** constraint are never eligible at renewal — those subscriptions renew into standard, non-promotional pricing at their anniversary. Where two promotions overlap on the same SKU, the deeper discount is applied. Always check the review cart screen (or call the verify-eligibility API) before submitting, because if you don't see the promotion there it won't be applied.

## When does a price change actually hit a subscription?

Subscription price changes take effect at renewal, upgrade or term conversion. A partner's charge is based on the purchase price, or the price at the time the subscription was created, and if the price changes mid-term the new price applies only at renewal — unless the partner themselves makes a change such as updating the term or upgrading, in which case the current price applies. For license-based services Microsoft publishes a price list preview 30 days ahead of any changes, visible under Pricing and offers, with change markers ADD, CHG, DEL and UNC; the preview is only published if there are changes that month. Price changes from the preview go into effect on the first of the following month at midnight UTC. There is no preview for usage-based Azure services because they are dynamic.

## Can a partner upgrade a customer mid-term, and what happens to the term?

Yes, and it is often the cleanest way to move a customer up a SKU without waiting for renewal. Partner Center supports both full upgrades (all seats) and partial upgrades (some seats), performed immediately or scheduled at the end of the current term via renewal instructions. Cancellation windows are **not** applied to upgrades, so once an upgrade is submitted it cannot be undone — check the details before confirming. On term behaviour: upgrading within the same term duration keeps the original end date (upgrading annual E3 to annual E5 four months in leaves eight months of E5), whereas upgrading from a shorter to a longer term starts a whole new term (annual E3 to a three-year E5 four months in starts a full three years). Only scheduled changes allow coterminous end dates; mid-term upgrades do not. Starting a mid-term upgrade also removes any scheduled upgrades.

## What is the difference between new commerce and the traditional (legacy) CSP catalog?

Four differences matter operationally. **Catalog**: traditional offers were defined by an offer ID; new commerce offers are defined as product, SKU and availability (or term), surfaced to API users as a `catalogItemId`. **Pricing**: traditional prices were per currency; new commerce pricing is defined per *customer market*, so a partner needs the price list for the customer's market, not just their own currency. **Promotions**: traditional promotions were sometimes discounts and sometimes separate offers with "promo" in the name; new commerce promotions are simplified and applied to the partner price. **Subscription management**: cancelling and reducing licences works differently, and new commerce subscriptions can only be cancelled in the first seven days of their term. What has *not* changed includes creating customers, requesting customer relationships, and the purchase and manage flows for software subscriptions, perpetual software, Azure services and Marketplace offers.
