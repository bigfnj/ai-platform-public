# The SMB licensing families a CSP partner sells

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office365/servicedescriptions/office-365-platform-service-description/office-365-plan-options · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/business-premium/microsoft-365-business-faqs · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/frontline/flw-licensing-options
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What licensing families should an SMB partner know?

Microsoft groups its Microsoft 365 and Office 365 plans into service families, and only two of them matter for most SMB conversations. The **Business** family — Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Business Standard, Business Premium and Microsoft 365 Apps for business — is capped at a maximum of 300 users. The **Enterprise** family — Office 365 E1/E3/E5, Microsoft 365 E3/E5, Microsoft 365 Apps for enterprise, and the frontline plans Microsoft 365 F1, Microsoft 365 F3 and Office 365 F3 — has no user limit. There are also Education (A-series) and U.S. Government families, plus standalone services such as Exchange Online Plan 1/2, SharePoint Online, Project, Visio and Power BI that can be bought on their own or added to Business and Enterprise plans that don't already include them.

## What does Microsoft 365 Business Basic include and who is it for?

Business Basic is the entry point of the Business family and the natural landing spot for a customer moving off consumer email or a legacy on-premises mail server. It includes the Office 365 platform, Exchange Online (Plan 1), SharePoint Online, OneDrive, Microsoft 365 Apps for the web, Microsoft Teams, and core Viva Engage functionality. What it does not include is the **Microsoft 365 desktop client apps** — users get browser-based Office only. It also does not include Azure Information Protection. Business Basic suits customers whose staff work almost entirely in a browser, or who already own perpetual desktop Office and only need the cloud services. It is one of the eligible base licences for Microsoft 365 Copilot Business.

## What does Microsoft 365 Business Standard add over Business Basic?

Business Standard is Business Basic plus the installable desktop applications. Microsoft's description is that it "includes the apps and services that businesses need for remote work and collaboration" — Microsoft Teams, secure cloud storage, business email, and premium Office applications across devices. In the service comparison table the single differentiator from Business Basic is **Microsoft 365 desktop client apps: Yes**. Business Standard still does not include Azure Information Protection, and it does not include the Defender security stack. It is the productivity-complete but security-thin plan, which is exactly why it is the most common upgrade target for a Business Premium conversation.

## What does Microsoft 365 Business Premium include?

Business Premium is Microsoft's comprehensive cloud productivity and security solution designed and built for small and medium-sized businesses of 1–300 users. It includes everything in Microsoft 365 Business Standard plus two security products: **Microsoft Defender for Business** and **Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 1**. It also includes Azure Information Protection, which Business Basic and Business Standard do not. Microsoft characterises the added capabilities as advanced security protection, next-generation protection, endpoint detection and response, and threat and vulnerability management, describing it as "enterprise-grade technology built for businesses with fewer than 300 users."

## What is Microsoft 365 Apps for business and when is it the right answer?

Microsoft 365 Apps for business (formerly Office 365 Business) sits in the Business family but is a productivity-apps-only SKU. In the service table it provides Microsoft 365 Apps for the web, the Microsoft 365 desktop client apps and OneDrive — but no Office 365 platform services, no Exchange Online, no SharePoint Online and no Microsoft Teams. It is the right answer only for a customer who needs the Office applications and file storage but genuinely gets email and collaboration elsewhere. Note that it counts against the same 300-seat Business family cap, and it is an eligible base licence for Microsoft 365 Copilot Business.

## Where does Office 365 (E1/E3/E5) fit against Microsoft 365?

Office 365 plans are the productivity-and-collaboration layer without the Windows and Enterprise Mobility + Security components that make a plan "Microsoft 365." Office 365 E1 gives the platform, Exchange Online Plan 1, SharePoint Online, OneDrive, Office for the web and Teams — but no desktop client apps. Office 365 E3 and E5 add the desktop apps and progressively more compliance capability, with E5 adding Power BI, Phone System and Audio Conferencing, the Microsoft Purview suite and the Microsoft 365 Defender suite. For an SMB the practical read is that Office 365 E1/E3 are what you sell when a customer needs to exceed 300 seats or needs an enterprise-only feature, but doesn't need or won't pay for the Microsoft 365 security and device management stack.

## What is Microsoft 365 F3 and which customers is it for?

F3 is a frontline worker plan sold from the Enterprise family, so it carries no 300-user cap. Microsoft defines frontline workers as staff who often work on tablets or phones and work directly with customers or the general public — retail associates, healthcare clinicians and nursing staff, factory workers, hospitality staff — as opposed to information workers with desktops or laptops who work mostly on documents. Microsoft 365 F3 (which includes Office 365 F3) provides the Office 365 platform, Exchange Online **Kiosk**, SharePoint Online, OneDrive with **2 GB** of storage, Microsoft 365 Apps for the web, Microsoft Teams and core Viva Engage. It does **not** include the Microsoft 365 desktop client apps. Typical frontline use is Teams chat, Shifts for scheduling, Planner for task sign-off and Lists for tracking.

## What is Microsoft 365 F1 and what is the mailbox trap?

F1 is the lighter frontline plan, and its limitations are easy to mis-sell. It includes the Office 365 platform, SharePoint Online, OneDrive with 2 GB, Microsoft Teams and core Viva Engage — but **no Exchange Online**, and no Microsoft 365 desktop client apps. On Office for the web, F1 users can *read* files but do not have create, edit or save rights. The trap Microsoft calls out explicitly: F1 does not include rights to an Exchange mailbox, yet F1 licences may come with the Exchange Online K1 service plan enabled to allow a full Teams experience, which provisions a mailbox the user is *not entitled to use*. Microsoft's guidance is to disable Outlook on the web for those users and instruct them not to access the mailbox by any other means.

## How do you mix plans across one customer tenant?

Mixing is expected, not exceptional. Microsoft states you can combine Enterprise, Business and standalone plans (for example Exchange Online Plan 1) within a single account, and that Business Premium can be combined with plans and add-ons from Azure, Dynamics 365, Enterprise Mobility + Security and Office 365. The frontline guidance is explicitly a mixing pattern: Enterprise licences for information workers such as headquarters staff, and frontline licences for store managers, sales associates, shipping staff, stockers and pickers in the same tenant. You can also switch a customer between plans — within the same service family, from a standalone plan, or to a different service family — though moving to Business Premium may increase or decrease the monthly charge.

## Does every user in an organization need the same licence?

No. Microsoft is explicit that not everyone in an organization is required to have a Business Premium subscription, but adds an important caveat: the security and management benefits of Business Premium are only available to users whose devices are managed under a Business Premium subscription. Microsoft's own recommendation is to deploy Business Premium to *all* users for the best protection of sensitive business data and consistent collaboration experiences, while acknowledging that SMBs often update software in step with hardware refresh cycles over an extended period. That tension — partial coverage is permitted but leaves gaps — is the honest framing for a phased-rollout conversation.

## Which SMB plans include Microsoft 365 desktop apps and which do not?

This is the single most common source of customer surprise, so it is worth stating plainly. **Includes the desktop client apps**: Microsoft 365 Business Standard, Microsoft 365 Business Premium, Microsoft 365 Apps for business, Microsoft 365 Apps for enterprise, Microsoft 365 E3/E5 and Office 365 E3/E5. **Does not include the desktop client apps**: Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Office 365 E1, Microsoft 365 F3, Office 365 F3 and Microsoft 365 F1. A customer moving from Business Basic to Business Standard is, in practical terms, paying for installable Word, Excel, Outlook and PowerPoint on their machines.
