# Microsoft 365 Business Premium: the SMB security upsell

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/business-premium/microsoft-365-business-faqs · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office365/servicedescriptions/office-365-platform-service-description/office-365-plan-options · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/customers/create-a-new-subscription
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What exactly is Microsoft 365 Business Premium?

Business Premium is an integrated solution that brings together productivity tools, security and device management capabilities for small and medium-sized businesses. Microsoft describes it as "a comprehensive, cloud-based security solution that enables you to defend your business against advanced threats with sophisticated phishing and ransomware protection," helping safeguard data, devices and information. It is fully integrated with Microsoft 365 Apps, so administration, billing and 24x7 support all live in one place. Microsoft's own positioning line is the one to internalise for SMB selling: it "consists of enterprise-grade technology built for businesses with fewer than 300 users."

## What does Business Premium add over Business Standard?

Business Premium is Business Standard plus a security layer. Microsoft names two products specifically: **Microsoft Defender for Business** and **Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 1**. Together these deliver, in Microsoft's words, "more cybersecurity and productivity capabilities, including advanced security protection, next-generation protection, endpoint detection and response, and threat & vulnerability management." Business Premium also includes **Azure Information Protection**, which neither Business Basic nor Business Standard includes — Business Standard would have to buy AIP as a separate add-on to get the supported Information Rights Management features. Everything in Business Standard — Teams, business email, secure cloud storage, the premium Office desktop applications across devices — carries forward unchanged.

## Why is Business Premium the key SMB upsell rather than just a nicer plan?

Because it changes the *category* of what the partner is selling. Business Standard is a productivity plan; Business Premium is a productivity plan with a security and device-management platform attached, which is the technical foundation a managed security service actually runs on. Microsoft positions it as "designed and built for small and medium-sized businesses (1–300 users)," meaning an SMB customer gets endpoint detection and response, next-generation anti-malware, threat and vulnerability management, and advanced phishing and ransomware protection without having to buy an enterprise SKU or bolt on third-party point products. For a partner, the upgrade turns a licence resale into a recurring managed-service engagement with configuration, monitoring and remediation work attached.

## What is the honest limit of the Business Premium security story?

Two limits are worth stating so a partner doesn't over-promise. First, security and management benefits are only available to users whose devices are managed with a Business Premium subscription — a partial deployment leaves the unlicensed users unprotected, and Microsoft explicitly recommends deploying Business Premium to all users for the best protection of sensitive business data. Second, Business Premium does not launder non-genuine software: Microsoft states flatly that it "doesn't make an otherwise non-genuine version of Windows genuine." If a customer's estate includes non-genuine Windows installs, that has to be remediated separately.

## How does Business Premium help a customer with GDPR and compliance obligations?

Carefully — this is a scoping question, not a checkbox. Microsoft's framing is that the GDPR requires organizations controlling or processing personal data tied to EU residents to use only third-party data processors that meet GDPR requirements for personal data processing, and that since March 2017 Microsoft has made available contractual guarantees providing those assurances. Business Premium contributes technical capability toward compliance obligations — Azure Information Protection for classification and rights management, Defender for Office 365 Plan 1 for threat protection — but the compliance obligation itself remains the customer's. The correct partner posture is to position Business Premium as helping the customer meet obligations, and to point compliance questions at the Microsoft Trust Center rather than making a compliance claim on Microsoft's behalf.

## What are the seat limits on Business Premium?

Microsoft 365 Business base per-user plans are designed for organizations with **up to 300 users**, and the 300-seat cap applies across the whole Business family combined — Business Basic, Business Standard and Business Premium share it, not 300 each. Microsoft's worked example: an organization provisioned for 250 seats of Business Premium is eligible to provision only 50 more seats in total across the Business family. Microsoft reserves the right to enforce the tenant limit of 300 provisioned licences across the Business family, and states that organizations with more than 300 users should consider subscribing to Microsoft 365 for enterprise plans.

## Can a customer add telephony and other add-ons to Business Premium?

Yes. Microsoft states that all the add-ons that can be added to Microsoft 365 Business Standard can also be added to Business Premium. Telephony specifically is supported: Microsoft Teams Phone with Calling Plan is a telephony add-on subscription bundling Phone System, Audio Conferencing, a Domestic Calling Plan, voicemail, caller ID, call park, call forwarding, auto attendants and call queues. Availability is not universal — you must check country and region availability for Audio Conferencing and Calling Plans before quoting. Business Premium can also be combined with plans and add-ons from Azure, Dynamics 365, Enterprise Mobility + Security and Office 365.

## How does a customer move onto Business Premium, and can they trial it first?

A customer can switch from a qualifying Microsoft 365 plan to Business Premium, which may increase or decrease the monthly charge depending on where they are coming from. In CSP the mechanics are a Partner Center upgrade — Microsoft's own example of a mid-term upgrade is "upgrading from Microsoft 365 Business Standard to Microsoft 365 Business Premium mid-year," which can be done immediately or scheduled at end of term. A trial is available directly from the Business Premium product page. Business Premium is available to all partners and organizations in all locations where Office 365 is available.

## What should a partner check before deploying Business Premium?

Microsoft's recommendation is to plan the deployment with a Microsoft Partner rather than alone, on the grounds that partners have training and experience across a wide variety of customer scenarios and are best equipped to understand the environment. The practical pre-checks Microsoft points at are hardware and software compatibility against the Business Premium technical requirements, the Windows Compatibility Cookbook, and the Microsoft 365 system requirements. Windows for business — meaning Windows 11 Pro and Windows 11 Pro for Workstations — is the supported desktop baseline. Sales-side, Microsoft's guidance is to sell Business Premium with the same consultative methods used for Office 365, focused on understanding the customer's existing desktop environment, Active Directory reliance, mobility and security needs.
