# Recent changes to program mechanics — what moved in FY27

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-august
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Why this collection exists

Microsoft publishes a Partner Center announcements page every month, and it is the single most
reliable way to know whether something you believe about the program is still true. Designation
criteria, certification requirements, specialization structure and offer terms all change on a
monthly cadence, and older documentation is rarely retracted — it simply goes stale in place.

This file is a point-in-time snapshot of the changes that landed around July and August 2026. If
you are reading it more than a quarter later, treat it as a list of things to re-check rather
than as current fact.

## Security specializations moved to an audit-based model

As of 30 July 2026, all four Microsoft Security specializations — Cloud Security, Data Security,
Identity & Access Management, and Threat Protection — moved to an audit-based model. The audit
validates whether a partner can genuinely deliver Microsoft Security solutions in real customer
environments, rather than inferring capability from certifications alone.

Three details carry real commercial weight. The audit is conducted by an **independent
third-party auditor**, it is **funded by the partner**, and it recurs **every two years**.
Partners receive a Pass or No Pass result, including if they withdraw. Microsoft granted a
six-month extension to anniversary dates to allow preparation time.

For a smaller partner this materially raises the cost of holding a security specialization, and
it should be weighed as an ongoing expense rather than a one-off attainment effort.

## Three specialization mergers

As of 31 July 2026, three new specializations were created by merging existing ones, with
enrolled partners moved across automatically at their existing anniversary date.

**Analytics on Microsoft Azure** absorbed Data Warehouse Migration and Business Intelligence. Its
performance bar is USD 15,000 of Azure Consumed Revenue over the last three months across three
customers, drawn from Azure Databricks, Fabric F SKU, or the rest of Analytics.

**Agentic Business Solutions** merged Low Code Application Development with Intelligent
Automation. It offers two performance pathways — either two unique customers deploying Power
Automate and Power Apps in production, or two new Copilot Studio deployments at a minimum of USD
10,000 trailing twelve months each. It also requires a published consulting service offer on
Microsoft Marketplace.

**App Modernization on Microsoft Azure** merged Kubernetes on Azure with Migrate Enterprise
Applications to Azure, requiring USD 15,000 of ACR over three months from eligible app-platform
services.

## Certification changes that affect designation scoring

A set of certification retirements took effect on 30 July 2026 and apply to **both the Enterprise
and SMB tracks**. In Business Applications, the Dynamics 365 Customer Experience Analyst
Associate was replaced by Dynamics 365 Sales AI Consultant Associate, Power Platform Functional
Consultant Associate by Intelligent Applications Builder Associate, and Dynamics 365 Field
Service Functional Consultant Associate by Dynamics 365 Contact Center AI Engineer Associate. In
Digital & App Innovation, Azure Developer Associate was replaced by Azure AI Cloud Developer
Associate, with GitHub Certified: Agentic AI Developer added. In Security, Azure Security
Engineer Associate was replaced by Cloud and AI Security Engineer Associate.

The rule that protects you here: **skilling achieved before a certification retires remains valid
for one year.** That is a grace period, not a reprieve — a partner sitting near the 70-point
threshold on the strength of retiring certifications should be rebuilding that skilling now, or
the score will drop without any change in headcount.

## Benefits redemption changed, and expiry dates are moving

The benefits redemption process was replaced with a guided experience in Partner Center that
completes as a **0 USD purchase flow**, replacing key-based activation. Azure credits, including
GitHub Enterprise and GitHub Copilot Enterprise credits, now deposit directly onto a billing
profile rather than requiring a user assignment and a new Azure subscription.

One change deserves particular attention: **effective 1 November 2026, each benefit's expiration
aligns to the date the offer was purchased, not the date the benefit was first redeemed.** Under
the old model, delaying redemption effectively delayed the clock. Under the new one it does not —
an unredeemed benefit is simply worth less. Redeem early.

Note also that benefits can only be redeemed on tenants under the Microsoft Customer Agreement
billing account used for the MAICPP purchase, not on third-party tenants.

## Other changes worth knowing

The **MAICPP Agreement was updated effective 1 September 2026**, taking effect automatically with
no signature or acknowledgement required. **Windows 365 Frontline SKUs were renamed Windows 365
Flex** from 3 September 2026 — a pure branding change with no effect on entitlements, billing or
functionality, but one that will surface in customer-facing licensing views and confuse anyone
working from older documentation.

## Currency warning

This entire page is time-sensitive by design. Every item here has an effective date, and several
describe changes that were still "coming soon" when captured. Check the current month's Partner
Center announcements page at `learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/` before
treating any of it as current.
