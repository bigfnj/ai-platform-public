# MCEM Stage 3: Empower and achieve

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the purpose of MCEM Stage 3, Empower and achieve?

Stage 3 is where the agreed design has to survive contact with proof and with procurement. Microsoft describes it as the stage where "we blend our teams, technology, and subject matter expertise. To showcase the optimal solution and instill confidence in our customers." Two categories of activity are named: "Demonstrating how the proposed solutions meet their needs" and "Commencing any relevant technical training."

The stage carries a dual burden that partners frequently treat as one. Technical proof comes first — Microsoft lists "conducting proof of concept, pilot projects, developing prototypes, or designing demos to illustrate how the solution aligns with the customer's requirements." But Microsoft is explicit that this is not sufficient: "As the technical proof is developed, the customer will also want proof that the solution delivers the desired value to the business." Business proof is described as coming "through engagements with Microsoft, partner solution and industry experts, and other customers who have adopted similar solutions." Only once both are in hand does Microsoft say "we move to creating a proposal and agreeing on terms."

## What are the exit criteria for MCEM Stage 3?

The stage-3 exit is the least ambiguous of the five, and Microsoft states it as an absolute:

- **From the MCEM narrative:** "The **Empower and achieve** stage isn't complete until we have an agreement in place that has been signed by all parties."
- **From the Partner Center sales-stage mapping table**, the exit criterion for Empower and Achieve is: "Customer Agreement in place."

Note what this rules out. A verbal commitment, a budget allocation, a selected vendor, or a proposal the customer says they are happy with are all still stage 3. Signature by all parties is the gate. In Partner Center terms, two sales stages sit inside MCEM stage 3: **Proposed** at 60% ("making a formal proposal to the customer based on their requirements") and **Negotiated** at 80% ("negotiating the final terms based on the proposal to get to the final state – winning or losing the deal"). Both map to the same exit criterion, which is a useful reminder that moving from proposal to negotiation does not clear the stage.

## What artifacts does MCEM Stage 3 produce?

The artifacts fall into three groups. Proof artifacts are the proof of concept, pilot, prototype, or demo, plus any technical training that has begun. Value artifacts are the evidence that the solution delivers business outcomes — reference conversations with comparable customers, industry expert engagements, and the quantified case built on the stage-2 business case. Commercial artifacts are the proposal, the negotiated terms, and finally the signed agreement itself, which may be a statement of work, an order, or a purchase.

Microsoft describes agreement construction as a multi-party exercise: it "involves collaboration among various stakeholders, such as technology specialists, cloud solution architects, account executives, and industry solutions delivery and support personnel." There is a corresponding demand on the customer side — "the customer needs to identify the key players involved in the finalization of the agreement and terms." If the customer cannot name who signs, stage 3 is not going to close on the date in your forecast.

## What help can a partner request from Microsoft at MCEM Stage 3?

Stage 3 is the point in the methodology where asking Microsoft for help is most obviously well-timed, and Partner Center exposes a fixed list of help types on a co-sell deal. The published options are: workload-specific value proposition, customer technical architecture, proof of concept or demo, quotes and licensing, post-sales customer success, and general or other. Three of those — technical architecture, proof of concept or demo, and quotes and licensing — map directly onto stage-3 work.

Selecting any option other than "No help required at this point of time" converts the deal into a co-sell opportunity, at which point a Microsoft seller "can potentially help closing the deal, although their participation isn't guaranteed." Microsoft sales representatives have a 14-day window to decide whether to participate. Microsoft's guidance is to be specific in the notes about the customer's business problem and the exact assistance wanted, because that text is what the seller evaluates.

## What does the transition out of MCEM Stage 3 trigger?

Marking a deal **Won** in Partner Center is the act that carries it out of stage 3, and it has consequences beyond the forecast. Won is a terminal state — "deals can't be modified after they move into a terminal state" — so estimated value, solutions, and account should be correct before you mark it. For deals eligible for Azure IP co-sell deal registration, Microsoft requires "a gap of 72 hours between creating the deal and marking the deal *won*"; closing sooner can cause the registration to be rejected.

Deal registration itself, where applicable, requires the deal to be won, to include an Azure IP co-sell eligible solution, to be of type *partner-led* or *co-sell*, to carry a deal value of at least USD 25,000, and to name a customer account that Microsoft manages. Registration is scoped to IP co-sell opportunities; Microsoft states that only IP solutions are eligible, listing Azure IP co-sell, Biz apps premium, and Biz apps standard.

## Currency warning

The USD 25,000 registration threshold, the 72-hour gap, the 14-day seller participation window, and the list of help types are Partner Center program mechanics captured from a page dated 2026-07-23 and are subject to change. Eligibility rules for deal registration in particular have been revised repeatedly. The stage-3 exit criteria themselves are more stable, but verify against the Microsoft Learn MCEM page before quoting them in a contractual or incentive context.
