# MCEM Stage 4: Realize value

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the purpose of MCEM Stage 4, Realize value?

Stage 4 is deployment and the proof that the deployment was worth doing. Microsoft calls it "crucial in our co-sell journey, where Microsoft and our partners drive a successful implementation and deliver exceptional outcomes to our valued clients." The framing throughout is about honouring a bet the customer has already placed: "our customers have put their trust in our joint solution, and here's where this confidence pays off."

The named collaborators change at this stage. Microsoft describes working "hand in hand with our Customer Success Managers and Solution Architects, collaborating with the customer and various teams to bring the production design to life and provide the necessary training and support for successful implementation." The account executive who carried stages 1 to 3 is no longer the centre of gravity; delivery and customer success roles are.

Microsoft states two explicit goals for the stage: "Achieve the highest customer satisfaction" and "Ensure seamless alignment and implementation of the project with our partners."

## What are the exit criteria for MCEM Stage 4?

Microsoft publishes the stage-4 exit in the narrative and gives an entry condition in the Partner Center mapping table. Both are quotable:

- **From the MCEM narrative:** "As we reach the end of the **Realize value** stage, the customer should be satisfied with partner implementation, and both the partner and Microsoft project outcomes should be met with metrics in place."
- **From the Partner Center sales-stage mapping table**, the *Won* sales stage at 100% maps to Realize Value with the criterion: "Customer has executed the agreement and deployment can begin."

Three conditions therefore have to hold to leave stage 4: the customer is satisfied with the implementation the partner delivered; the project outcomes agreed by both the partner and Microsoft have actually been met; and metrics are in place to demonstrate it. That third clause is the one most often missing. Microsoft reinforces it in the narrative — the solution must be "ready to be handed over for productive use, and appropriate metrics are in place to confirm success." Without instrumentation, stage 5 has nothing to measure against and the renewal conversation has no evidence behind it.

## What does a partner actually do during MCEM Stage 4?

The work is delivery, but Microsoft describes it as delivery under continuous risk management: "Throughout the delivery and deployment process, our teams stay vigilant, making sure any technical issues are swiftly addressed and mitigated, consistently validating the solution architecture, and recognizing and addressing any potential emerging risks."

There is also a formal handover step before the stage closes. Microsoft states: "When we're confident that all technical and operational preparations are complete, we present the production solution design to the customer for their feedback. It's imperative that the solution meets their needs, functions impeccably, is ready to be handed over for productive use." A production design review with the customer, followed by their sign-off, is therefore part of the stage rather than an optional courtesy.

Microsoft's FY26 partner-community MCEM deck summarises the stage-4 actions as ensuring successful deployment, facilitating change management, and monitoring and reporting on progress, with change management requirements and support and training resources named as things the partner needs to know going in. That deck is a distributor-hosted partner asset, not Microsoft Learn documentation, so treat the wording as a gloss on the published narrative rather than as canonical.

## Why is change management part of MCEM Stage 4 rather than an afterthought?

Because the exit criterion is customer satisfaction and met outcomes, not deployed licences. A tenant that is provisioned but unused fails the stage-4 test as published. Microsoft's stage-4 description pairs implementation with "the necessary training and support for successful implementation," which puts adoption work inside the stage rather than after it.

This is also where a partner's commercial interest and Microsoft's align most cleanly. Microsoft's partner role taxonomy in Partner Center includes *Adoption and change management* and *Deployment services* as recognised roles a services partner can play on a co-sell opportunity, which means adoption work is something a partner can be attached to a deal to deliver rather than something absorbed into project overhead.

## How does MCEM Stage 4 generate the next opportunity?

Microsoft explicitly refuses to treat stage 4 as terminal: "The **Realize value** stage isn't limited to implementation. Together with our partner teams, we actively look for other ways to serve our joint customer. As the solution takes shape, new opportunities might arise, and we encourage our account team to be involved in identifying avenues for expansion based on the customer's evolving needs."

The practical implication for a partner is that delivery staff are in the best position in the whole engagement to spot the next deal, and usually the worst-instrumented to do anything with it. A partner that has a route for a delivery engineer to raise an expansion signal — and to get it into Partner Center as a new stage-1 opportunity — converts stage 4 into pipeline. A partner that does not treats every project as a one-off sale.

## Currency warning

The stage-4 narrative quoted here comes from a Microsoft Learn page dated 2024-11-13 and the sales-stage mapping from a Partner Center page dated 2026-07-23. Partner role options, the sales-stage percentages, and the mechanics of marking a deal *won* are Partner Center program details that change. Note also that *won* is a terminal state in Partner Center from which deals cannot be edited, so the Partner Center record generally stops tracking the engagement at the point stage 4 begins — the delivery phase itself lives in the partner's own systems.
