# MCEM Stage 1: Listen and consult

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the purpose of MCEM Stage 1, Listen and consult?

Stage 1 exists to make sure nobody proposes a solution before they understand the problem. Microsoft's description is direct: "Before offering any advice or solutions, we listen to and consult together with our joint customers to fully understand their business and their desired outcomes." The output of this stage is not a proposal or a quote — it is a qualified, well-understood opportunity that both the partner and Microsoft agree is real.

Microsoft is explicit that the stage begins before you ever meet the customer: "This stage starts even before meeting the customer. Understanding the customer's market, goals and historical performance is critical. Research the customer or opportunity and talk with colleagues about the customer." The in-meeting behaviour Microsoft describes is active listening to "comprehend their priorities, the challenges they encounter, and the potential for addressing their requirements."

The strategic framing is that listening is what earns the right to be a trusted advisor rather than a vendor. Microsoft describes the payoff as being able to "engage in co-innovation, co-development, and commit to our customers' success," establishing the joint team "as trusted advisors capable of meeting both their immediate and future challenges."

## What are the exit criteria for MCEM Stage 1?

Microsoft publishes the stage-1 exit in two places, and they say the same thing in different words. Quote them as checkable statements:

- **From the MCEM narrative:** "By the end of the **Listen and consult** stage, the partner will be able to identify a customer referral, or their solution should be mapped to a customer need."
- **From the same narrative:** "At this stage, a Qualified Opportunity is created in Partner Center and shared with Microsoft sellers."
- **From the Partner Center sales-stage mapping table**, the exit criterion for Listen and Consult is stated in three words: "Qualified opportunity."

Turned into a checklist a partner can actually run against a live deal, stage 1 is complete when: the customer's business need has been documented in the customer's own terms; that need has been mapped either to a specific solution of yours or to a referral you will pass on; the opportunity has been created in Partner Center; and it has been shared with Microsoft sellers. If you cannot state the customer need without describing a product, you are not out of stage 1.

## What artifacts does MCEM Stage 1 produce?

The primary artifact is the qualified opportunity record in Partner Center. Per the Partner Center co-sell documentation, creating that record requires a deal name, a location, an estimated value, and an estimated close date, plus at least one team member with full contact details. For Services co-sell it also requires a **Solution area** and a **Solution play**, and a **Partner role**. There is an optional but strongly encouraged **Customer need** field; Microsoft's guidance is to "provide a clear business problem statement that outlines what the customer is trying to solve or improve," because that text is what Microsoft sellers use to triage the deal.

Behind the Partner Center record, the working artifacts are discovery notes, an account or stakeholder map, and a view of the customer's market and historical performance gathered before the first meeting. Microsoft's FY26 partner deck for MCEM frames the stage-1 actions as conducting thorough discovery sessions, identifying solution areas and plays, collaborating on building trust, and documenting and qualifying opportunities — that deck is a partner-community asset rather than a Microsoft Learn page, so treat the wording as illustrative rather than definitive.

## What does a partner actually do in Stage 1?

In practice, stage 1 is prospecting and discovery, plus one administrative act that partners routinely skip: getting the opportunity into Partner Center early. Sharing at stage 1 rather than at proposal time is what gives a Microsoft seller time to bring whitespace data, funding, or a specialist to the deal. It also means the deal is visible to Microsoft before a competitor's is.

The other stage-1 discipline is choosing the right solution play. Microsoft's stage-2 guidance says the first step there is to "confirm the correct Solution Plays align to the customer's needs," which means the candidate play should already have been identified during stage 1. Because Solution area and Solution play are mandatory fields on a Services co-sell deal, a partner who has not formed a view on the play cannot cleanly create the record.

## Where do partner and Microsoft activity intersect in Stage 1?

The intersection point is the moment the qualified opportunity is shared. Before that, stage 1 is largely partner-run: your marketing, your prospecting, your discovery calls. After that, a Microsoft seller can see the deal and decide whether to engage. Note the timing mechanics published for Partner Center: a Microsoft sales representative has a 14-day window to decide whether to participate in a co-sell deal, and an inbound opportunity that a partner does not respond to within 14 days is archived as *expired*.

Traffic also runs the other way. Microsoft sellers and Microsoft marketing generate leads and referrals that are routed to partners through the Referrals workspace in Partner Center, which means a partner's stage 1 sometimes begins with an inbound deal from Microsoft rather than with their own prospecting. Microsoft's guidance for being on the receiving end of those is to publish a business profile, respond quickly, and be selective — accepting poorly-fitting deals degrades the quality of what you are sent next.

## Currency warning

Partner Center field requirements, the 14-day response and participation windows, and the mandatory Solution area / Solution play rules are program mechanics that Microsoft changes without much notice, and the solution play list itself is refreshed each fiscal year. The stage-1 exit criteria quoted above come from a Microsoft Learn page dated 2024-11-13 and a Partner Center page dated 2026-07-23. Re-verify both before quoting mechanics to a customer.
