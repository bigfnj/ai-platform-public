# What parts of MCEM are NOT publicly documented?

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities · https://partner.microsoft.com/en-us/asset/collection/microsoft-customer-engagement-methodology-mcem-for-partners
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## Is MCEM a public framework or a Microsoft-internal one?

Both, partially. MCEM is fundamentally a Microsoft-internal sales methodology that governs how Microsoft's own field organisation runs opportunities, and the majority of it — the operational detail, the internal role assignments, the forecasting rules — is not published. What Microsoft has published is a partner-facing subset: an overview page on Microsoft Learn describing the five stages, a stage-to-exit-criteria mapping table inside the Partner Center co-sell documentation, and an MCEM for Partners learning path on partner.microsoft.com.

That subset is genuinely useful and is what this collection is built on. But a partner should go in understanding that they are seeing an abridged version by design, and that a Microsoft seller referencing MCEM internally has more structure available to them than the partner-facing pages show.

## What specifically is published and can be quoted?

Four things can be quoted with confidence because Microsoft publishes them directly. First, the five stage names: Listen and consult, Inspire and design, Empower and achieve, Realize value, and Manage and optimize. Second, a narrative description of the purpose and activities of each stage on the Microsoft Learn MCEM for partners page. Third, an explicit stage-exit sentence in that narrative for stages 1 through 4. Fourth, the Partner Center table mapping deal sales stages to MCEM stages with a short exit criterion for each — "Qualified opportunity", "Customer Aligned to solution & business case", "Customer Agreement in place", and "Customer has executed the agreement and deployment can begin".

Microsoft also confirms that a fuller apparatus exists behind these, stating that the methodology "includes detailed checklists, the primary roles, supporting roles, resources, and exit criteria for the activity at each stage" — while adding the caveat that these "are intended as a framework and not every activity might be needed."

## What is NOT published, and should not be invented?

The detailed checklists Microsoft refers to are not published. Neither are the primary and supporting role assignments per stage, the internal resource lists, nor any per-stage activity criteria beyond the short exit statements. There are no published quantitative stage gates — no minimum deal qualification score, no required number of stakeholders, no probability thresholds beyond the Partner Center sales-stage percentages, which are a referrals-system weighting rather than an MCEM construct.

Stage 5 has **no published exit criterion**. The Partner Center mapping table stops at *Won* mapped to Realize Value, and no sales stage corresponds to Manage and optimize. Any document presenting a crisp "stage 5 exit criteria" as Microsoft's official wording is either paraphrasing the narrative or making it up. Similarly, there is no published MCEM-specific artifact list — the artifacts described in this collection are inferred from the narrative and from Partner Center's required fields, and are labelled as such.

Do not fabricate stage numbers, sub-stages, gate names, or scoring models. If a partner asks for something in this category, the correct answer is that Microsoft has not published it and they should ask their Partner Development Manager.

## Why do third-party sources disagree about the MCEM stage names?

Because MCEM has been revised since it was introduced to partners in 2023, and consultancies, distributors, and training providers have published interpretations at different points in that evolution. The most common discrepancy concerns stage 5: several third-party sources call it "Sustain and grow", while Microsoft's own Learn documentation and a Microsoft partner development manager's October 2025 deck both call it "Manage and Optimize" / "Manage and Optimise".

There is also casing and phrasing drift between Microsoft's own pages — the Learn narrative uses "Realize value" while the Partner Center table uses "Realize Value", and the table renders the stages as "Listen and Consult", "Inspire and Design", "Empower and Achieve". These are cosmetic. The substantive rule is to prefer Microsoft Learn over any third-party restatement, and to treat partner-community decks, distributor training material, and consultancy blog posts as commentary rather than as sources of stage definitions.

## What did this knowledge base deliberately leave out?

Several things a partner might reasonably expect to find here are absent because no Microsoft-published source could be found for them. There is no per-stage role matrix showing which Microsoft job function owns which stage, beyond the roles Microsoft names incidentally in the narrative (Customer Success Managers and Solution Architects at stage 4; technology specialists, cloud solution architects, account executives and industry solutions delivery personnel at stage 3). There is no list of the internal checklist items per stage. There is no mapping of MCEM stages to Microsoft's incentive or funding programs, because those programs are documented separately and their eligibility rules do not reference MCEM stage names.

There is also no published statement that MCEM applies differently in the SMB segment than in enterprise. The methodology is presented as universal. Any claim that SMB deals skip stages, or that partner-led SMB motions use a compressed MCEM, would be an inference — a reasonable one given deal sizes, but not something Microsoft has published.

## How should this collection be used given those gaps?

Use the published exit criteria as the authoritative answer whenever a partner asks what has to be true to advance a deal — those four statements are quotable verbatim and are the highest-value content in this collection. Use the stage narratives to explain intent and to coach on what good looks like within a stage. Use the Partner Center mechanics for anything operational, and re-verify them, because they change most often.

Where a question falls outside what is published, say so rather than reconstructing an answer. Microsoft directs partners to the MCEM for Partners learning path on partner.microsoft.com and to the current fiscal year's Cloud Solution Area Partner Playbooks for the material that sits behind the public overview, and a Partner Development Manager is the route to anything beyond that.

## Currency warning

This assessment of what is and is not public reflects sources retrieved on 2026-08-14: a Microsoft Learn MCEM page dated 2024-11-13, a Partner Center co-sell page dated 2026-07-23, and a solution areas page dated 2025-07-17. Microsoft could publish additional MCEM detail at any time, and the partner-facing learning path is refreshed periodically. If a partner cites MCEM detail not covered here, check whether Microsoft has published something newer before assuming it is wrong.
