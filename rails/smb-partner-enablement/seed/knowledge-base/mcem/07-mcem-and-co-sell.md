# Where in MCEM does a co-sell motion get initiated?

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/referrals · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/joint-planning-leads · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/understanding-solution-areas
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## At which MCEM stage should a partner initiate co-sell?

Stage 1. Microsoft's MCEM documentation places the Partner Center action inside Listen and consult: "At this stage, a Qualified Opportunity is created in Partner Center and shared with Microsoft sellers." The Partner Center mapping table agrees — the *Created* and *Accepted* sales stages, both at 10%, map to Listen and Consult.

This is earlier than most partners' instinct, which is to involve Microsoft once there is something concrete to show. The reason MCEM puts it at stage 1 is sequencing: a Microsoft seller who sees the deal during discovery can still influence the design, bring a specialist, surface whitespace or account intelligence, or flag a competing motion already running in the account. A seller who first sees the deal at proposal stage can do almost none of that, and has only a 14-day window to decide whether to engage at all.

There is a second, blunter reason. Microsoft sales representatives have "a 14-day window to decide whether they'll participate in the deal." If you submit at proposal time on a deal closing in three weeks, the window itself may consume your remaining runway.

## What is a co-sell opportunity in Microsoft's definition?

Microsoft defines it as "a collaboration of multiple parties to sell products that meet a customer's needs," and elaborates that "one or more Microsoft sales representatives actively engage in a deal to help solve a customer problem." Crucially, direction is not fixed: "Co-sell opportunities can originate from a customer account when a Microsoft sales representative invites a partner to participate in a co-selling activity. Co-sell opportunities can also originate from a partner who needs more help from Microsoft sales to close a deal."

Partner Center reflects that in its layout, with **Inbound** ("opportunities sent to the partner from Microsoft sellers for collaborative selling") and **Outbound** ("opportunities created by the partner") tabs. For an SMB partner, inbound volume is largely a function of having a published business profile — Microsoft's stated advice is that "the most important step you can take to start getting sales leads from us is to create a business profile that showcases your company's offerings, solutions, and expertise."

## What types of co-sell opportunity exist?

Microsoft publishes six deal types, and choosing the right one is what determines who sees the deal and what you can claim from it. **Azure IP co-sell** applies where a software development company co-sells "using at least one Azure IP co-sell eligible solution." **Services co-sell** is for a services partner, defined as "a partner who has atleast one Solutions Partner designation," co-selling on a customer opportunity. **Partner-to-partner (P2P)** lets one partner invite others in the Microsoft co-sell ecosystem to collaborate, optionally with a Microsoft seller. **Solution assessments** are restricted to partners vetted by the solution assessments business team. **Partner-led** gives Microsoft visibility without requesting active help. **Private** keeps the deal invisible to Microsoft until the partner chooses otherwise.

For most SMB-focused services partners, the relevant choice is between Services co-sell, partner-led, and private. Microsoft notes that private deals are still listed on the co-sell tab "to provide an easy path to upgrade any pipeline deal to a co-sell opportunity when the deal in not in a terminal stage of won, lost, declined, or expired" — so starting private and upgrading later is a supported pattern.

## What has to be true before I can create a co-sell deal?

The gating requirement is a co-sell-ready solution. Microsoft states it as: "To create a new co-sell deal, you need at least one co-sell-ready solution. Once this is achieved, you can also co-sell with in-market solutions." For services co-sell specifically, eligibility runs off partner program status — Microsoft describes Microsoft sellers sending co-sell opportunities "to Services partners who have Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program designations such as Azure Expert MSP or Specializations or Solutions Partner designations."

The account you select also matters, and this catches partners out. Search results separate **Microsoft Managed** accounts, **Microsoft Unmanaged** accounts, and **Other** (from the Moody's database). Picking from the Microsoft Managed tab makes the deal eligible for Azure IP co-sell deal registration if other criteria are met; picking from Microsoft Unmanaged means the deal "*isn't* eligible for IP co-sell deal registration." Because most SMB customers are not Microsoft-managed accounts, SMB partners should expect deal registration to be unavailable on the bulk of their pipeline and should not build their economics around it.

## How does the solution play affect co-sell routing?

Selecting the right solution play is how the deal reaches the right Microsoft team. Microsoft's stated purpose for the fields is to "connect the right Microsoft Sales team and support a successful customer outcome," and the reason given for making them mandatory on Azure IP co-sell is "faster seller identification in Microsoft sales ecosystem."

Solution area and solution play are required for Services co-sell opportunities and are now required for new Azure IP co-sell deals created through the Partner Center portal or bulk upload. Microsoft also populates these fields on seller-initiated opportunities "to help partners in opportunity sizing and qualification," so on an inbound deal the play tells you what Microsoft thinks the deal is before you accept it. As of Partner Center documentation dated 2025-07-17 there are four solution areas: AI Business Solutions, Cloud and AI Platforms, Microsoft Unified, and Security.

## What role can a services partner claim on a co-sell deal?

Partner Center exposes a **Partner role** field for services co-sell, and the published options describe where in the MCEM lifecycle you are contributing: presales envisioning, solution design, proof of concept, business strategy, deployment services, adoption and change management, transaction, and managed services.

The list is worth reading as a map of the whole methodology rather than a form field. Presales envisioning and business strategy are stage 1 and 2 roles; solution design and proof of concept are stage 2 and 3; transaction closes stage 3; deployment services and adoption and change management are stage 4; managed services is stage 5. A partner that can only claim *transaction* is describing a reseller motion, and will be routed accordingly.

## What is Joint Planning, and how does it differ from co-sell?

Joint Planning is a separate, seller-initiated motion aimed specifically at the segment most SMB partners work in. Microsoft describes it as "Microsoft's always-on approach for aligning Microsoft Small Medium Enterprises & Channel (SME&C) sellers with partners **earlier in the sales cycle** to collaborate on customer territory planning and drive co-selling success." Microsoft characterises what it is as "A seller-led SME&C motion for customer planning," and what it is not: "Not for all customer segments — specific to SME&C."

The distinction from ordinary co-sell matters in two ways. Joint Planning happens at territory-planning time rather than deal time, so it sits earlier than MCEM stage 1 for any individual opportunity — it is about agreeing *which* accounts to work before there is an opportunity to share. And the routing is privileged: Microsoft states that "Joint Planning leads converted to opportunities **bypass qualification by the Co-sell Operation Service** and are routed directly back to the SME&C seller who initially sent the lead."

The response mechanics are stricter than they look. A partner is expected to accept a Joint Planning lead within **14 days** and to action it within **60 days**. A partner that treats these like ordinary inbound referrals and lets them age will stop receiving them.

## What does a good co-sell submission contain?

Microsoft's guidance centres on the notes and customer-need fields, because those are what a Microsoft seller reads when deciding whether to engage. The instruction is to provide "the customer business problem statement" and "the specific help they require from Microsoft," and to be "as detailed and specific as possible," because "this information helps Microsoft sellers evaluate the opportunity and align their expertise and resources effectively."

Six help types are selectable: workload-specific value proposition, customer technical architecture, proof of concept or demo, quotes and licensing, post-sales customer success, and general or other. Partner Center now applies a referral confidence score — described as scoring outbound opportunities High, Medium, or Low — and offers generative-AI assistance that suggests customer-need statements from the solution area and play you have selected. Microsoft's own tip for improving conversion is to supply "detailed BANT criteria (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)". Microsoft also states that responsiveness feeds back into what you are sent: respond quickly and "we'll increase your visibility in future partner search results progressively," and be selective, because "accepting deals that aren't a good fit won't improve your search results."

## Currency warning

Everything in this file except the MCEM stage framing is Partner Center program mechanics, captured from documentation dated 2026-07-23 and 2025-07-17. Deal types, eligibility rules, mandatory fields, the 14-day windows, the confidence-score rollout, and the AI-assisted notes feature are all subject to change, and several are described in the source as recently introduced or still rolling out. The four solution areas are refreshed annually with Microsoft's fiscal year. Verify in Partner Center itself before relying on any of these mechanics.
