# MCEM Stage 2: Inspire and design

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/manage-co-sell-opportunities · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/understanding-solution-areas
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the purpose of MCEM Stage 2, Inspire and design?

Stage 2 is where a qualified need becomes an agreed solution. Microsoft describes it as the point where "we look to go deeper into the customer's needs and goals in more detail while reaching a solution design." The word *inspire* is doing real work in the name — this is the stage where the customer is shown a future state worth paying for, not just a fix for the problem they walked in with.

Ownership of the design is deliberately flexible. Microsoft states that "based on the situation and customer preference, Microsoft or our partners take the lead in developing the solution strategy." In an SMB or partner-led deal, that lead is almost always the partner; the Microsoft role is to validate, to supply solution-play assets, and to bring specialists where the design touches something the partner does not build.

Microsoft also flags stage 2 as the natural moment to widen the conversation beyond the immediate deal: it "is ideal for bringing in joint teams to assess the customer's short and long-term needs more comprehensively to inform an overall strategic account plan."

## What are the exit criteria for MCEM Stage 2?

Microsoft publishes two statements of the stage-2 exit. Both are quotable as checkable conditions:

- **From the MCEM narrative:** "By the end of the **Inspire and design** stage, both the partner and Microsoft accounts teams should have clarity and agreement on which solution addresses the customer's need."
- **From the Partner Center sales-stage mapping table**, the exit criterion for Inspire and Design is: "Customer Aligned to solution & business case."

Read together, the bar has three parts and all three must be true: the customer is aligned to the solution, the customer is aligned to the *business case* for it, and the partner and Microsoft account teams agree with each other on which solution it is. Internal agreement without customer alignment does not clear the gate, and customer enthusiasm without a business case does not either. In Partner Center terms the corresponding sales stage is **Developed**, carried at 40% complete, described as "developing the deal further to understand the detailed requirements to either prepare a POC or any other artifacts required for a formal proposal."

## What is the role of solution plays in MCEM Stage 2?

Confirming the solution play is the first thing Microsoft tells you to do in stage 2: "The first step at this stage is to confirm the correct Solution Plays align to the customer's needs." Microsoft defines solution plays as "a set of customer scenario frameworks that drive focus on specific solutions for addressing a customer's needs," and elsewhere as go-to-market frameworks that "group Microsoft technologies into customer-centric solutions to help focus your sales efforts on key personas and business outcomes."

As published in Partner Center documentation dated 2025-07-17, there are four solution areas — AI Business Solutions, Cloud and AI Platforms, Microsoft Unified, and Security — with more granular solution plays beneath each. Choosing correctly is not a formality: Microsoft's stated reason for the field is to "connect the right Microsoft Sales team and support a successful customer outcome," so the play you select determines which Microsoft specialists your deal is routed to. Solution area and solution play are mandatory on Services co-sell opportunities and on new Azure IP co-sell deals created through the Partner Center portal or bulk upload.

## What artifacts does MCEM Stage 2 produce?

The tangible outputs are a solution design and a business case, plus the solution-play designation on the Partner Center record. The business case is the artifact partners most often under-build — the published exit criterion names it explicitly, and a design the customer likes but cannot justify to whoever signs will stall in stage 3.

Microsoft's FY26 partner-community MCEM deck describes the stage-2 actions as collaborating on solution design, developing and presenting a business case, validating with the customer, and leveraging solution area playbooks. That deck is a distributor-hosted asset authored by a Microsoft partner development manager, so treat it as a useful gloss rather than as canonical documentation. What *is* canonical is the pairing in the exit criterion: solution plus business case, both validated with the customer.

## What does "acts in unity" mean at MCEM Stage 2?

Microsoft calls out its engagement principles specifically at this stage: "In addition to putting the customer at the center, this stage requires that the team acts in unity, with Microsoft and partners collaborating through the customer journey." In practical terms this is the stage where a partner and a Microsoft seller most need to avoid presenting two different architectures, two different price expectations, or two different timelines to the same customer.

The mechanism for staying in unity is the Partner Center record itself. Keeping estimated value, close date, solutions, and the customer-need statement current on the deal is what lets a Microsoft seller show up saying the same thing you are saying. Deal details including estimated value, notes, solutions, and team members remain editable after the deal is created precisely so the record can track the design as it evolves.

## Currency warning

The set of solution areas and solution plays is refreshed every Microsoft fiscal year (July to June), and the four solution areas listed here reflect Partner Center documentation dated 2025-07-17. Microsoft directs partners to the current fiscal year's Cloud Solution Area Partner Playbooks collection on partner.microsoft.com for the live play list. Mandatory-field rules for co-sell deals have changed at least once recently and may change again. Re-verify play names against Microsoft's current playbook collection before using them with a customer.
