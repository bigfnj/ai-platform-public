# MCEM Stage 5: Manage and optimize

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/mcem-for-partners · https://info.cloudchampion.fi/rs/779-DUO-006/images/FY26%20MCEM%20for%20Cloud%20Champion%20-%20shared.pdf
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is the purpose of MCEM Stage 5, Manage and optimize?

Stage 5 is the post-deployment stage, and its job is to verify that the customer is actually getting the value they were sold and to convert that verified value into the next engagement. Microsoft's description: "In the final stage, Manage and Optimize, we aim to ensure our joint customer remains satisfied with the deployed solution and evaluate whether the expected business value is being achieved."

The measurement is against a baseline set earlier: "This includes comparing the solution's actual performance results with the customer's previously established key performance indicators." This is why the stage-4 exit criterion insists on metrics being in place. Stage 5 also carries an operational monitoring duty — "We actively monitor the overall health of the customer's solution to identify and resolve any unforeseen issues."

Two specific optimisation actions are named: Microsoft says "we proactively assess whether a customer's solution requires rightsizing or if legacy systems need to be transitioned off, further enhancing the business value for the customer."

## What are the exit criteria for MCEM Stage 5?

This is the one stage where Microsoft's public documentation does **not** state a crisp exit criterion, and the honest answer is that there isn't one in the same sense as stages 1 to 4. The Partner Center sales-stage mapping table stops at *Won* mapped to Realize Value, so stage 5 has no corresponding deal sales stage at all — by the time an engagement reaches stage 5, the originating Partner Center opportunity is in a terminal state.

What Microsoft does publish is the condition under which stage 5 loops back to the beginning: "New opportunities based on discussions with a customer might also come up. These might be related to expansion, incremental business objectives, or value the customer is trying to achieve. In these cases, the partner and Microsoft account teams should engage to further explore with the customer – which brings us to stage one of a new customer decision cycle in this engagement methodology."

The workable formulation for a partner is therefore: stage 5 does not end, it recurs. The checkable conditions available are that actual performance has been compared against the customer's stated KPIs, that solution health is being actively monitored, that right-sizing and legacy-transition opportunities have been assessed, and that any resulting expansion opportunity has been raised as a new stage-1 opportunity. Do not present these as an official Microsoft exit gate — they are derived from the published narrative, not quoted from a published criteria table.

## Why is MCEM described as cyclical rather than linear?

Because Microsoft says so explicitly, and the consequence matters for how a partner runs an account. The published statement is: "While this stage is the final one, MCEM for partners is cyclical. Each successful deployment acts as force multiplier, initializing one or more Listen and Consult stages across multiple departments in the enterprise."

That means a healthy account does not sit at a single MCEM stage. A customer three years into a relationship might have a stage-5 monitoring motion on their productivity estate, a stage-3 negotiation on security, and a stage-1 discovery running with a different department. Partners that maintain a single "customer status" field lose this; partners that track stage per opportunity see the whole picture. It also reframes delivery quality as a pipeline input rather than a cost centre — the deployment is what initialises the next cycle.

## Where is the partner's commercial opportunity in MCEM Stage 5?

Microsoft is unusually direct about this. It states that "Partners can benefit either by providing solution health and optimization solutions and services or through incremental solutions for the customer," and that "Based on this established credibility with the customer, partners have a significant opportunity to grow their business by driving realization of business value through increased solution consumption, as well as customer need discovery and engagement."

There are two distinct revenue motions in that sentence. The first is a services annuity: monitoring, health, optimisation, right-sizing — work that is naturally delivered as a managed service and billed monthly. The second is expansion: consumption growth on what is already deployed, plus net-new solutions surfaced by being in the account. For an SMB-focused partner, the first is what makes the practice financially stable and the second is what makes it grow, and both depend on having stayed in the account after go-live rather than moving on to the next project.

## Is Stage 5 called "Manage and optimize" or "Sustain and grow"?

Microsoft's current documentation calls it **Manage and Optimize**. This appears both on the Microsoft Learn MCEM for partners page and in a Microsoft partner development manager's FY26 MCEM deck dated October 2025, which lists "Stage 5: Manage and Optimise."

Some third-party consultancy and distributor material describes stage 5 as "Sustain and grow." A partner encountering that name should treat it as either an older label or a third-party paraphrase, not as a separate stage — the described activities are substantially the same. Where the two conflict, the Microsoft Learn page is the source to follow. This is worth knowing because a partner quoting "Sustain and grow" to a Microsoft seller may get a blank look.

## Currency warning

MCEM stage naming has demonstrably shifted since the methodology was introduced to partners in 2023, and stage 5 is the stage where third-party sources most often disagree with Microsoft's own documentation. The description here reflects the Microsoft Learn page dated 2024-11-13, corroborated by an October 2025 partner-community deck. The FY26 deck cited is hosted by a distributor rather than by Microsoft; it is used here only to confirm stage naming. Re-check the Microsoft Learn MCEM page for the current stage-5 name before using it in written material.
