# What are the main SMB solution plays and what customer problems do they address?

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/referrals/understanding-solution-areas · https://microsoftpartners.microsoft.com/abs/ · https://microsoftpartners.microsoft.com/Microsoft-Security-Partners/ · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/business-premium/microsoft-365-business-faqs · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-june · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/migrate/migrate-services-overview · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/across-business-functionality
> As of: 2026-07 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What is a solution play, and which taxonomy applies?

A solution play is Microsoft's packaging of technologies into a customer-outcome motion. Microsoft's Partner Center documentation defines them as "customer scenario frameworks that drive focus on specific solutions for addressing a customer's needs," and elsewhere as frameworks that "group Microsoft technologies into customer-centric solutions to help focus your sales efforts on key personas and business outcomes."

Partners must keep three overlapping taxonomies straight, because they are not the same list. The **co-sell solution areas** used in Partner Center referrals are AI Business Solutions, Cloud and AI Platforms, Microsoft Unified, and Security — these are the values you select on a co-sell deal. The **Solutions Partner designations** used for partner-program scoring are a different six-way split (Business Applications, Data & AI, Digital & App Innovation, Infrastructure, Modern Work, Security). And the **go-to-market solution plays** are the named motions beneath the solution areas, refreshed every fiscal year.

The one that determines co-sell routing is the first. The one that determines your partner-program status is the second. The one that shapes your campaign and pitch is the third.

## Which fiscal year's solution plays are current?

Microsoft FY27 runs 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and FY27 play names are current as of retrieval. This matters more than it sounds, because a great deal of FY26 material is still in circulation and some of it is still linked from Microsoft Learn — the Partner Center page listing the four co-sell solution areas carries a date of 2025-07-17 and still links to the FY26 playbook collection.

Under FY27, Microsoft groups its partner go-to-market under an umbrella it calls **Frontier Accelerate**, described as "Microsoft's unified FY27 customer value framework that represents the end-to-end package of customer benefits for AI transformation," organised around three pillars — Trusted Expertise, Savings & Investments, Skilled Teams — and branches covering Copilot, AI-Ready Productivity, Business Processes, Azure, Security and Marketplace.

The practical rule for a partner: never quote a play name without checking the fiscal year it belongs to, and expect the names to change again next July. The customer problems underneath them change far more slowly than the labels do.

## Which solution play covers Modern Work and productivity for SMB?

In FY27 the productivity motions sit under AI Business Solutions, in a group Microsoft labels "AI-Ready Productivity & Security for Every Employee," containing the plays **AI-Ready Productivity** and **AI-Ready Anywhere Workspace**. A separate group, "AI in the Flow of Human Ambition," contains **Enabling Frontier Workers** and **AI and Agent Platform for Your Business**.

One naming trap deserves flagging: Microsoft's "Enabling Frontier Workers" play is about organisation-wide AI adoption and is framed around enterprise products; it is **not** the frontline-worker (F-SKU) motion despite the similar word. Do not conflate Frontier Workers with frontline workers.

The customer problem underneath the Modern Work motion in SMB is consistently the same regardless of the label: a business running a mixture of consumer-grade tools, an ageing on-premises server, unmanaged personal devices and separate subscriptions for email, storage and meetings, with nobody accountable for any of it. The play is consolidation onto one identity and one management surface, and the product ladder is the Microsoft 365 Business family.

## What is the Microsoft 365 Business ladder for SMB, and where does each rung fit?

The family has three rungs and one hard boundary. **Business Basic** provides web and mobile Office apps, Teams, business email and 1 TB of OneDrive but no desktop applications. **Business Standard** adds the desktop Office applications plus webinars and Clipchamp. **Business Premium** is documented as "everything in Microsoft 365 Business Standard, plus" the security stack.

The boundary: "Our Microsoft 365 Business base per user plans are designed for organizations with up to 300 users only," and the cap is per tenant across the whole family, not per SKU.

Business Premium is the rung that matters most commercially, because it is where a licence sale becomes a security practice. Its documented components are Microsoft 365 Apps, **Microsoft Entra ID P1**, **Microsoft Intune Plan 1**, **Microsoft Defender for Business**, **Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 1**, and **Microsoft Purview Information Protection and Data Loss Prevention** for email and files. Microsoft confirms separately that Business Premium subscribers "are licensed to use full Intune capabilities for iOS, Android, Mac, and other cross-platform device management."

## Which solution play covers Security for SMB?

Microsoft publishes an SMB-specific security motion for FY27 called **"Run your Business Securely,"** covering security posture, secure AI adoption and streamlined compliance, and explicitly leading with Microsoft 365 Business Premium under the framing "more impact, less complexity." The broader FY27 Security plays are **Get ready for AI** ("reduce exposure and strengthen the core environment that AI depends on"), **Secure AI** ("protect and govern AI systems and agents") and **Defend with AI** ("transform security operations with AI at scale").

The core SMB security product is Microsoft Defender for Business, documented as "an endpoint security solution based on Microsoft Defender for Endpoint… designed for small and medium sized business up to 300 users," offering "protection from ransomware, malware, phishing, and other threats on devices." It includes the features of Defender for Endpoint Plan 1, some Plan 2 features, and SMB-specific additions such as simplified firewall and antivirus configuration and monthly security summary reporting.

Above Business Premium, Microsoft has added SMB-scoped upsell suites — a **Defender Suite for Business Premium** (adding Entra ID P2, Defender for Identity, Defender for Endpoint P2, Defender for Office 365 P2, Defender for Cloud Apps, Defender XDR and Defender for IoT) and a **Purview Suite for Business Premium** (adding eDiscovery Premium, Insider Risk Management, Audit Premium, Communication Compliance, Compliance Manager and more). Both are capped at 300 seats and require a Business Premium subscription. These give a partner a second and third security sale into the same customer without leaving SMB licensing.

## Which solution play covers Copilot adoption in SMB?

Copilot in SMB changed shape materially on 1 July 2026. Microsoft's Partner Center announcement states that "Starting July 1, 2026, Microsoft 365 Business Standard with Copilot and Microsoft 365 Business Premium with Copilot become **permanent SKUs**," and explains why that matters to partners: "This shift removes the friction of selling Copilot as an add-on or as a short-term promotional offer."

Microsoft's partner guidance in the same announcement is explicit about the default motion: "Lead your default SMB offers with *Business Standard with Copilot* and *Business Premium with Copilot*," and to identify upcoming renewals as Copilot upgrade opportunities. Separately, **Microsoft 365 Copilot Business** exists as an SMB add-on documented as delivering "the same capabilities as the Microsoft 365 Copilot offering," supporting "up to 300 seats per tenant," and attachable to Business Basic, Business Standard or Business Premium.

Two cautions worth carrying into a customer conversation. Copilot Business carries an annual commitment with no month-to-month option, and Microsoft documents a lock-in: "You can't upgrade to an Enterprise plan from a Microsoft 365 Business Standard or Business Premium plan with Copilot Business. You must wait until your commitment end date." Also note the targeting insight from CloudAscent — 80% of Copilot licences are sold into customers who already have consolidated productivity estates and mature security adoption, which means Copilot is usually the third sale, not the first.

## Which solution play covers Azure migration for SMB?

In FY27 the Azure motions sit under Cloud and AI Platforms, with the migration play named **"Modernize with Confidence"** — "modernize your customers entire estate across infrastructure, applications, and data to unlock AI-ready innovation." Its FY26 predecessor was "Migrate and Modernize Your Estate," and partners will still see that name in circulating material.

The tooling is Azure Migrate, documented as "a service that helps you decide on, plan, and execute your migration to Azure… support for servers, databases, web apps, virtual desktops, and large-scale offline migration," and explicitly a free service. It runs in three phases — Decide (discovery plus a business case with TCO and capital-to-operating-expenditure analysis), Plan (readiness, right-sizing, cost estimation, dependency analysis) and Execute — with supported sources including VMware, Hyper-V, and physical or other-cloud servers.

For SMB, the single most concrete migration trigger available is end of support. **Windows Server 2016 extended support ends 13 January 2027**, which is a dated, external, non-negotiable event affecting a large installed base of exactly the on-premises servers small businesses still run. That is a far stronger opening than a general cloud-benefits conversation.

## Which solution play covers Business Applications for SMB?

Dynamics 365 Business Central is the SMB ERP, positioned by Microsoft as providing "functionality for common business processes in small and medium-sized companies, mainly within wholesale and professional services," while also supporting "more complex processes, such as assembly, manufacturing, service, and directed warehouse management."

The FY27 play name is **Agentic ERP**, which Microsoft describes in terms of building an "Agentic Finance and Operations practice with Dynamics 365… first-party ERP agents, MCP-enabled extensibility, and repeatable services that surround or replace incumbent ERP." Related FY27 plays in the same group are Agentic Customer Engagement and Agentic Apps.

Business Central licensing has three tiers — Essentials, Premium (adding enhanced service management and manufacturing) and Team Members (limited access to read data, approve workflows and update select information). Note that the AI agents, such as the Sales Order Agent and Payables Agent, are available across tiers but "Requires Copilot Credits (sold separately)" — a consumption element that must be scoped into any proposal rather than assumed included.

The customer problem here is the most tangible in the whole SMB portfolio: a business running its finances in an accounting package that no longer fits, with stock, orders and projects tracked in spreadsheets alongside it. It is also the play with the longest sales cycle and the highest services attach, which makes it the anchor of a profitable practice rather than a transactional sale.

## Currency warning

This is the fastest-moving file in this collection and should be treated as a snapshot. **Solution play names change every fiscal year** — the FY27 names here (Enabling Frontier Workers, AI-Ready Productivity, Run your Business Securely, Modernize with Confidence, Agentic ERP, Get ready for AI, Secure AI, Defend with AI) replaced FY26 names, and FY26 material is still in circulation and still linked from some Microsoft Learn pages. Verify the current fiscal year's playbook collection before using any play name with a customer or on a co-sell deal.

Product and licensing facts were retrieved 2026-08-14 from Microsoft Learn pages dated between 2025-08-27 and 2026-07-29. **This file deliberately omits prices**, because Microsoft moved to annual local-currency price updates each January starting in FY27, prices differ by geography, currency and channel (the direct storefront and CSP promotional terms were observed to disagree on end dates), and at least one Microsoft-owned page was found showing obsolete Copilot pricing. Always confirm final pricing, eligibility, seat minimums and promotion IDs in Partner Center at checkout — that is Microsoft's own published instruction.

The 300-seat cap and the Windows Server 2016 end-of-support date of 13 January 2027 are the two most stable facts here.
