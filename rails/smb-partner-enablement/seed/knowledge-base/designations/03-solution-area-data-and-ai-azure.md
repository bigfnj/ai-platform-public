# Solutions Partner for Data & AI (Azure) — what it covers and how it is scored

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/solutions-partner-azure · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/partner-capability-score
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What does the Data & AI (Azure) designation cover?

Data & AI (Azure) is one of the three Azure solution areas and covers the data estate and AI workloads a partner builds on Azure — analytics, data platform, databases, machine learning and AI services. It is scored entirely from Azure consumed revenue (ACR) and Azure certifications; there is no Microsoft 365 component. Under the August 2026 badge rollup it contributes to the **Solutions Partner for Cloud & AI Platforms** badge, alongside Infrastructure and Digital & App Innovation. Partners are automatically classified into either the Enterprise or the SMB qualification track based on their customer base.

## Which customer problems does Data & AI (Azure) map to?

This designation is the credential to lead with when the customer conversation is about getting value out of data: consolidating scattered databases, migrating SQL estates, standing up a lakehouse or Fabric analytics environment, building reporting that the business trusts, or putting an AI or copilot capability on top of the customer's own data. The certifications Microsoft requires tell you what it expects the practice to look like — Azure administration and solution architecture as the foundation, then database administration, Fabric data engineering and analytics engineering, SQL AI development, Azure Databricks data engineering, data science, and AI apps and agents development on top.

## How is my organisation classified as Enterprise or SMB for Data & AI?

Microsoft classifies your organisation as either Enterprise or Small and Medium Business based on your customer base: "you're classified as SMB if 80% or more of your customers are under the SMB segment. Otherwise, you're classified as Enterprise." The classification is recalculated from the last month's data. The classification matters because the Performance and Skilling thresholds differ between the two tracks — Customer success scoring (Usage growth and Deployments) is identical for both.

## How does the Net customer adds metric score in Data & AI (Azure)?

Performance is worth a maximum of **30 points** and each net new customer contributes **10 points**, so three net adds reaches the ceiling. The calculation is `Net customers added = (Eligible Customers last month) – (Eligible Customers same month last year)`. What counts as an eligible customer is the key difference between tracks: on the **Enterprise** track it is a unique customer tenant contributing at least **USD 1,000** in Azure consumed revenue in one of the last two months; on the **SMB** track the bar is **USD 500** in at least one of the last two months. Eligible workloads are all Azure Service Level 2 services, all offers except Support, Internal, Benefit Program and Trial, and the eligible association types are CSP (Tier 1 and Tier 2), Digital Partner of Record (DPOR) and Partner Admin Link (PAL).

## How does the Intermediate certifications metric score in Data & AI (Azure)?

Intermediate certifications is worth a maximum of **40 points**, earned from up to **10 individuals at 4 points each** — the largest skilling weight of any Azure area. Scoring runs as a three-step gate. Step 1 requires people holding **Azure Administrator Associate**; step 2 requires people holding **Azure Solutions Architect Expert**; step 3 awards the points for individuals holding at least one of a listed set of data and AI certifications. On the **Enterprise** track, steps 1 and 2 each need at least two people and award no points themselves. On the **SMB** track, each of steps 1 and 2 needs only one person and each awards a flat 4 points, capped at 4 regardless of how many people complete it. Steps 1 and 2 are mandatory gates on both tracks: skip either and step 3 scores zero. The same person may count in more than one step, but only once per step.

## Which certifications count for step 3 of Data & AI (Azure) skilling?

Microsoft's listed step-3 certifications are Azure Database Administrator Associate, Azure AI Engineer Associate, Azure Data Scientist Associate, Data Analyst Associate, Azure Cosmos DB Developer Specialty, Fabric Analytics Engineer Associate, Fabric Data Engineer Associate, SQL AI Developer Associate, Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate, Machine Learning Operations Engineer Associate, and Azure Databricks Data Engineer Associate. Two of these carry published retirement dates: Azure AI Engineer Associate retires 30 June 2026 and Azure Data Scientist Associate retires 1 June 2026 — in both cases, if completed before retirement the certification remains valid for one year.

## Does Data & AI (Azure) have an Advanced certifications metric?

No. Microsoft states plainly that "advanced certifications aren't applicable to the Data & AI solution area." This is why the Intermediate certifications weight is 40 points here rather than the 20 points used in Infrastructure and Digital & App Innovation — the whole skilling weight sits in one metric. Note the tension with the page's own opening summary, which lists five metrics including advanced certifications as needing more than zero points; the per-metric section is the more specific statement and should be trusted. If in doubt, check what metrics Partner Center actually displays for your Data & AI card.

## How does the Usage growth metric score in Data & AI (Azure)?

Usage growth is worth a maximum of **20 points** and is measured as percentage growth in Azure consumed revenue: **each one percent of ACR growth contributes one point**, so 20% growth maxes the metric. The formula is `Percentage usage growth = (Total qualifying ACR last month - Total qualifying ACR same month last year) * 100 / (Total qualifying ACR same month last year)`. Qualifying ACR is the trailing-twelve-month total across your entire customer base from eligible Azure services, eligible offers and eligible association types, and there is a floor: **total ACR must be at least USD 1,000 to qualify for scoring at all**. This metric is identical for Enterprise and SMB.

## How does the Deployments metric score in Data & AI (Azure)?

Deployments is worth a maximum of **10 points** and counts breadth of Azure services rather than customers: **each eligible deployment contributes two points**, so five distinct services reaches the cap. An eligible deployment is a unique Azure service (a top-level workload, identified by Service Level 2) contributing more than zero ACR over the last 12 months across your entire customer base. The important exclusion is that **Virtual Machines and Virtual Machines Licenses do not count** — raw IaaS lift-and-shift earns nothing here, which is deliberate. Offers exclude Support, Internal, Benefit Program and Trial; association types are CSP Tiers 1 and 2, DPOR and PAL. Scoring is identical for Enterprise and SMB.

## What does a qualifying Data & AI (Azure) score look like in practice?

The four scored metrics sum to exactly 100 points: Performance 30, Intermediate certifications 40, Usage growth 20, Deployments 10. Because 70 points is the bar and every metric must be non-zero, the arithmetic is unforgiving — you cannot ignore any one metric. A realistic SMB-track shape is full skilling (40) plus three net customer adds (30), which already reaches 70, but you would still need at least one point of usage growth and at least one eligible deployment to actually qualify. Conversely a partner with strong consumption but only one certified person on the SMB track would earn 4 points of skilling and be far short.

## Currency warning

Point weights, ACR thresholds and certification lists in this file come from the Microsoft Learn Solutions Partner for Data & AI, Infrastructure, and Digital & App Innovation page carrying a page date of 30 June 2026 and last updated 3 August 2026. Certification names and retirement dates change frequently — several listed certifications retire during 2026 — and Microsoft explicitly warns that "workload eligibility is subject to change." Microsoft's own worked examples on this page appear to mislabel step 2 and step 3 in the Enterprise Data & AI section (an example shows step 2 earning points despite the rule stating it does not), so treat the stated rules as authoritative over the examples and verify against Partner Center. Re-verify all numbers before quoting them to a partner.
