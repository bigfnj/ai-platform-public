# Solutions Partner for Security — what it covers and how it is scored

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/solutions-partner-security · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/partner-capability-score
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What does the Security designation cover?

Security is the only solution area that spans both sides of the Microsoft estate. Microsoft states that "Security partners can target providing services and solutions on Microsoft 365 workloads or on Azure services. So, valuation requirements focus on both Microsoft 365 usage and performance, and on Azure revenue." Calculations for Azure and Microsoft 365 are performed separately and then totalled into the final metric scores, and "one eligible customer can contribute points to both Azure and Microsoft 365 calculations." Security maps to its own badge, **Solutions Partner for Security**, under the August 2026 badge rollup.

## Which customer problems does Security map to?

This designation covers identity, endpoint, data and cloud security: a customer with no MFA or conditional access, an unmanaged device estate, sensitive data with no classification or DLP, a mailbox estate under phishing pressure, a cloud footprint with no posture management, or a business that needs a SOC capability it cannot staff. On the Microsoft 365 side the scored workloads are Entra ID P1, Defender for Office 365, Defender for Endpoint (P2 only), Defender for Identity, Microsoft Purview Information Protection and Intune. On the Azure side they include Microsoft Sentinel, Defender for Cloud, Azure Firewall, Azure DDoS Protection, Azure Bastion, Key Vault, Application Gateway, Front Door, Network Watcher, Azure IoT Security and the Entra external-identity services.

## How many metrics does the Security capability score have?

Security has **four** scored metrics, not five — it is the only solution area with no Advanced certifications metric. To attain the designation you must earn at least 70 points and have one or more points in each of: **Performance — Net customer adds**, **Skilling — Intermediate certifications**, **Customer success — Usage growth**, and **Customer success — Deployments**. The weights are Performance 20, Intermediate certifications 40, Usage growth 20 and Deployments 20, totalling 100. Skilling is therefore twice as heavy in Security as in any other area — a certified team is the single biggest lever on this score.

## How do the Enterprise and SMB attainment paths work in Security?

As in Modern Work, Microsoft evaluates you on both paths and takes the better result: "Microsoft evaluates partners on both paths and selects the highest score from either path... at the solution area level, rather than at the individual metric level." You do not elect a track. On the Microsoft 365 side, an **Enterprise** eligible tenant has at least one workload with **more than 300 paid licences**, while an **SMB** eligible tenant has at least one workload with **5 to 300 paid licences** — note the SMB floor is 5 licences here, lower than Modern Work's 11. A tenant promoted to Enterprise remains eligible for SMB if it was classified SMB the previous year, and the same tenant can be counted in both tracks.

## How does the Net customer adds metric score in Security?

Performance is worth up to **20 points**, and the tracks are mirror images of each other. On the **Enterprise** track each net new customer contributes **2 points**, reaching the cap at **ten customers**. On the **SMB** track each net new customer contributes **4 points**, reaching the cap at **five customers** — the SMB track is materially easier here. On the Azure side an eligible customer is a unique tenant contributing at least **USD 600** of Azure consumed revenue in the last trailing twelve months, and that USD 600 figure is the same for both tracks. Adds from Azure security services and Microsoft 365 are counted together, so you can reach the maximum from either side or a mix. Eligible association types are PAL and CSP Tiers 1 and 2 for Azure, and CPOR and CSP Tiers 1 and 2 for Microsoft 365.

## How does the Intermediate certifications metric score in Security?

Skilling is worth up to **40 points** and runs as a three-step gate. **Step 1** requires people holding **Azure Security Engineer Associate** or **Cloud and AI Security Engineer Associate**. **Step 2** requires people holding **Microsoft Security Operations Analyst**. **Step 3** awards the points for individuals holding at least one of Microsoft Cybersecurity Architect Expert, Microsoft Identity and Access Administrator, Microsoft Information Protection Administrator, or Microsoft Certified: Information Security Administrator Associate. On the **Enterprise** track steps 1 and 2 each require two people and award no points, and each step-3 individual adds **6.67 points**. On the **SMB** track steps 1 and 2 each require one person and each awards a flat **4 points** (capped), and each step-3 individual adds **8 points**. Steps 1 and 2 are hard gates on both tracks — miss either and step 3 scores zero.

## How much of the Security score can a small team realistically earn from certifications?

A lot, and this is the defining characteristic of the Security designation for a small partner. On the SMB track, one person holding a step-1 certification earns 4 points, one person holding Security Operations Analyst earns 4 points, and every individual with a step-3 certification adds 8 points. Microsoft's own SMB example shows two people at step 1, two at step 2 and three at step 3 producing **32 points**; four step-3 individuals would reach the full 40. Because the same individuals can count across steps, a genuinely small security practice — four or five certified engineers — can carry 40 of the 70 points needed. That is why Security is often more attainable for a small partner than the Azure areas, despite sounding harder.

## How does the Deployments metric score in Security?

Deployments is worth up to **20 points** and, unusually, the scoring is identical on both tracks: "each net new customer contributes 3.3 points for up to a maximum of 20 points, obtained from six deployments." The calculation is `Net new deployments = (Eligible deployments last month) - (Eligible deployments same month last year)`. What counts as eligible differs by track and by cloud. On **Azure**, an Enterprise-eligible deployment is a tenant contributing **more than USD 12,000** ACR over the past 12 months, while an SMB-eligible deployment is a tenant contributing **between USD 750 and USD 12,000** over the trailing twelve months. On **Microsoft 365**, an eligible deployment is a tenant where **at least 15% of paid licences are active** on eligible security workloads. Deployments from Azure and Microsoft 365 are counted together toward the six.

## How does the Usage growth metric score in Security?

Usage growth is worth up to **20 points** and is the metric where the Enterprise and SMB thresholds diverge most cleanly. On **Azure**, every **USD 1,250** of security ACR growth earns one point on the Enterprise track, versus every **USD 750** on the SMB track. On **Microsoft 365**, every **125** additional protected users earns one point on the Enterprise track, versus every **50** on the SMB track. Growth is measured as last month against the same month a year earlier. Points from Azure and Microsoft 365 are combined up to the shared 20-point ceiling, so a partner can reach the maximum from Microsoft 365 alone — 1,000 additional protected users on the SMB track — without any Azure security consumption at all.

## What counts as a protected user and which tenants are eligible for Security usage growth?

Protected users are "the number of active paid licenses across all eligible customer tenants from eligible Microsoft 365 workloads for specific partner association types" — that is, active licences on Entra ID P1, Defender for Office 365, Defender for Endpoint P2, Defender for Identity, Purview Information Protection and Intune. Tenant eligibility follows the track split: at least one workload with more than 300 paid licences for Enterprise, or between 5 and 300 paid licences for SMB. On the Azure side, an Enterprise-eligible tenant has more than USD 12,000 ACR in the trailing twelve months, while an SMB-eligible tenant has ACR greater than zero and no more than USD 12,000.

## What does a qualifying Security score look like in practice?

The four metrics sum to 100: Performance 20, Skilling 40, Usage growth 20, Deployments 20. On the SMB track a credible route to 70 is full skilling (40 points, from roughly four to six certified individuals counted across the three steps), five net new customers (20 points), plus non-zero usage growth and at least a couple of deployments. The two things that most often block a small partner are the Deployments metric — which needs six customer tenants at 15% active security licences or in the Azure ACR band — and the step-1/step-2 skilling gate, since a partner with four Identity and Access Administrators but nobody holding Security Operations Analyst scores zero on the heaviest metric in the area.

## Currency warning

All weights, thresholds and certification lists here come from the Microsoft Learn Solutions Partner for Security page carrying a page date of 14 April 2025 and last updated 31 July 2026 — note this is an older page date than the other solution areas, so it is more likely to lag. Microsoft explicitly warns that "the eligible workload list is subject to change. Consult this list frequently." Azure Security Engineer Associate retires 31 August 2026 and Microsoft Information Protection Administrator retired 31 May 2025, each remaining valid for one year if passed before retirement. The source page also mislabels a step in one Enterprise example ("3 people complete Step 2, earning 20 points" where the rules place those points at step 3). Re-verify against Partner Center before quoting a threshold.
