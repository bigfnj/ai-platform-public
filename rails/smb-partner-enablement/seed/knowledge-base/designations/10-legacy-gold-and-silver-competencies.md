# Legacy Gold and Silver competencies — what they were, when they ended, and what replaced them

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/silver-gold-membership-competency · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/solutions-partner-faq · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/introduction-to-pcs
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What were Microsoft Gold and Silver competencies?

Gold and Silver competencies were the tiering model of the old Microsoft Partner Network. A partner earned a *competency* in a technology area — Cloud Productivity, Cloud Platform, Small and Midsize Cloud Solutions, Data Analytics, Security, Enterprise Resource Planning and so on — by meeting exam, certification and performance requirements, and held it at either Silver or Gold level, with Gold demanding the higher bar. "Gold Partner" became the shorthand customers and marketing material used for a credible Microsoft partner. That model no longer exists, and any SMB collateral still leading with a Gold badge is describing a status Microsoft has retired.

## When were Gold and Silver competencies retired?

**Legacy Silver and Gold competencies were retired on 30 September 2022** and replaced with Solutions Partner designations. Microsoft's own wording in the Solutions Partner FAQ is: "legacy silver/gold competencies were retired on September 30, 2022 and replaced with Solutions Partner designations to offer differentiated benefits and badging for partners." Note that the competency itself ended in 2022 even though the associated *benefits* were allowed to run on for a further two-plus years, which is the source of most confusion about the date.

## When did legacy Gold and Silver benefits actually stop?

Partners with active Silver or Gold competencies were allowed to keep renewing the associated benefits package on each anniversary date with a renewal fee — but that ended too. **The last day to purchase or renew legacy Silver/Gold benefits was 21 January 2025**, and from **22 January 2025** Microsoft no longer sells them. Partners who purchased on or before 21 January 2025 keep their existing benefits until the expiry date but cannot renew afterwards. If a renewal window was already open on 22 January 2025, Microsoft honoured it for 30 days from the date it opened. Partners holding active legacy Silver/Gold status as of 21 January 2025 remained eligible for applicable Microsoft Commerce Incentives for the rest of the FY25 CSP incentive term, which ran to **30 September 2025**.

## Can I still call my company a Microsoft Gold Partner?

No. Microsoft states it directly on the Solutions Partner introduction page: "Microsoft no longer offers Silver and gold memberships. Partners should stop using references to Silver and gold membership in the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program." This is worth handling explicitly in SMB sales conversations, because customers still ask for a "Gold Partner" and older partner websites, tender responses and capability decks still claim it. The correct current claim is the Solutions Partner designation you actually hold, plus any specializations, using the badge assets available from Logo Builder in Partner Center.

## How do I map an old Gold competency to today's Solutions Partner designations?

**Microsoft does not publish a one-to-one mapping from legacy competencies to Solutions Partner designations, and there is no automatic conversion.** That is the accurate answer and the one to give. What Microsoft does say is directional: partners with legacy *gold* benefits are recommended to transition to **Solutions Partner designations**, while partners with legacy *silver* benefits are recommended to transition to **Partner Success Expanded Benefits** — an offer that does not require a designation. Microsoft is explicit that "you can't transfer the benefits from Silver or Gold to the recommended offers." Anyone presenting a tidy competency-to-designation lookup table is inferring it, not quoting Microsoft.

## What is the closest approximate mapping between old competencies and new designations?

This orientation is inference from the technology scope of each area, not Microsoft guidance, and should be labelled as such whenever it is used. Broadly, the old **Cloud Productivity** and **Collaboration and Content** competencies sit closest to today's **Modern Work** designation. **Cloud Platform**, **Datacenter** and **Windows Server** map toward **Infrastructure (Azure)**. **Data Analytics**, **Data Platform** and **AI** map toward **Data & AI (Azure)**. **Application Development**, **DevOps** and **Application Integration** map toward **Digital & App Innovation (Azure)**. **Enterprise Resource Planning**, **Cloud Business Applications** and **Small and Midsize Cloud Solutions** map toward **Business Applications**. The old **Security** competency maps to the **Security** designation. The mapping is only ever approximate because the measurement basis changed completely — competencies were earned largely on exams plus a revenue or seat threshold, whereas designations are earned on a composite capability score that also weighs customer adds, consumption growth and deployment depth.

## Why can a former Gold partner fail to qualify for the equivalent designation?

Because the two schemes measure different things. A Gold competency was, in essence, a skills-and-sales credential: pass enough exams, hit a revenue or seat number, renew annually. A Solutions Partner designation is a **composite** requiring at least 70 of 100 points **and** a non-zero score in every single metric — including net customer adds over the trailing twelve months, growth in customer usage year over year, and breadth of deployed workloads. A partner with a stable, profitable, non-growing installed base could comfortably hold Gold and still score zero on usage growth today, which disqualifies them regardless of their total. This is the single most common reason a long-standing SMB partner finds the new scheme harder than the old one.

## What happens if I hold legacy benefits and then qualify for a Solutions Partner designation?

Microsoft upgrades you automatically as you qualify. Their published answer: "Microsoft automatically upgrades you to a Solutions Partner designation as you qualify. On your next anniversary date, if you still qualify for the Solutions Partner designation, you can purchase the Solutions Partner designation benefits." There is no route back — asked whether a qualifying partner can choose legacy Silver or Gold benefits instead, Microsoft answers "No. Renewals of legacy silver or gold benefits aren't available... If you qualify for the Solutions Partner designation benefits, Microsoft only supports purchase of the Solution Partner designation benefits."

## How does a legacy Gold or Silver partner qualify for their first designation?

Through the same partner capability score as everyone else, but with a purchasing shortcut. The capability-score page sets out the rule for legacy partners specifically: if you are a legacy Gold/Silver partner not enrolled in any solution area, you need to achieve **Qualified** status during the qualification eligibility window — and then "you don't need to purchase the Solutions Partner designation. Once you qualify for any solution area, you're automatically enrolled into the solution area." That contrasts with a partner who is neither a Solutions Partner nor a legacy Gold/Silver partner, who must qualify first and then purchase the designation on the Membership offers page in order to become Enrolled.

## What should I do with old SMB partner material that still references Gold competencies?

Treat any mention of Gold or Silver as a dating signal for the whole document. Material written before October 2022 predates the designation model entirely; material written before January 2025 may still assume legacy benefits are renewable. Specific things to check and rewrite: badge claims (replace with the actual designation or specialization held), benefit descriptions (the licence and support entitlements changed substantially in the February 2026 benefits guide refresh), incentive references (legacy-status MCI eligibility ended 30 September 2025), and any statement that competencies are earned by passing exams — the capability score also measures customer adds, usage growth and deployments.

## Currency warning

The retirement dates in this file are firm and verified: competencies retired 30 September 2022; last purchase or renewal of legacy benefits 21 January 2025; no sales from 22 January 2025; MCI incentive continuity to 30 September 2025. These come from the Microsoft Learn Silver or Gold Membership page (page date 26 May 2025, last updated 25 September 2025) and the Solutions Partner FAQ (last updated March 2026). The competency-to-designation mapping section is explicitly an inference, not Microsoft guidance — Microsoft publishes no such mapping and it must never be presented as official. Benefit entitlements referenced here changed with the February 2026 benefits guide and change again at each refresh.
