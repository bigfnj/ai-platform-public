# Solutions Partner for Business Applications — what it covers and how it is scored

> Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/solutions-partner-business · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/membership/partner-capability-score
> As of: 2026-08 · Retrieved: 2026-08-14

## What does the Business Applications designation cover?

Microsoft's definition is direct: "as a Solutions Partner for Business Applications, you demonstrated your broad capability to deliver solutions with Dynamics 365 and Power Platform." It spans the whole Dynamics estate — Business Central, Finance, Supply Chain, Sales, Customer Service, Field Service, Contact Center, Human Resources, Customer Insights, Commerce and Project Operations — plus Power Apps, Power Automate, Power BI, Power Pages and Copilot Studio. Under the August 2026 badge rollup it contributes, together with Modern Work, to the **Solutions Partner for AI Business Solutions** badge.

## Which customer problems does Business Applications map to?

Lead with this designation when the customer problem is a business process rather than a platform: an SMB running finance and operations on spreadsheets or an unsupported ERP, a sales team with no CRM discipline, a service desk drowning in email, a manual back-office process that could be automated, or a reporting layer that nobody trusts. For an SMB-focused reseller the centre of gravity is Dynamics 365 Business Central plus the Power Platform — which is also why Business Applications carries the only specialization explicitly named for that segment, **Small and Midsize Business Management**.

## How is my organisation classified as Enterprise or SMB for Business Applications?

Classification here is workload-and-revenue based rather than customer-count based. Microsoft's rule: "a partner is classified as Enterprise if [the] partner has five or more unique workloads contributing more than $5,000 in revenue for each customer Tenant in both current month-1 and current month-13. A partner is classified as SMB if the partner has four or fewer unique workloads contributing more than $5,000 in revenue for each customer Tenant in both current month-1 and current month-13." The classification drives different thresholds in the Performance, Skilling and Customer success categories — and the SMB thresholds are dramatically easier, particularly on skilling.

## What are the five scored metrics and their point weights in Business Applications?

Business Applications is the one solution area where Microsoft publishes the full weighting explicitly in a single list. To attain the designation you need at least one point in each of five subcategories and at least 70 of 100 points overall, weighted as follows: **Performance — Net customer adds: 15 points; Skilling — Intermediate certifications: 20 points; Skilling — Advanced certifications: 15 points; Customer success — Usage growth: 30 points; Customer success — Deployments: 20 points.** Usage growth is the single heaviest metric in this solution area, which tells you Microsoft is primarily rewarding driving adoption inside existing customers rather than logo count.

## How does the Net customer adds metric score in Business Applications?

Net customer adds is worth up to **15 points**, with **each qualifying net customer add worth three points**, reaching the cap at five net additions — and the target is five for both Enterprise and SMB. What differs is the revenue bar per workload: on the **Enterprise** track an eligible add is a unique workload rolled up at tenant level contributing at least **USD 1,500** revenue in a given month; on the **SMB** track the bar is **USD 250** in a given month. Note that the metric counts *workloads* added, not customers — the same customer adding three qualifying workloads counts three times. Eligible workloads span the full Dynamics and Power Platform list including AI Builder, Business Central, Copilot Studio, Customer Insights, Power Apps, Power Automate, Power BI and Power Pages. Multiple partners can get credit for the same customer's new workload in the same period.

## How do the Skilling metrics score in Business Applications, and why is the SMB track so much easier?

Skilling is where the Enterprise/SMB gap is widest. For **Intermediate certifications** (20 points max), the Enterprise track needs **20 certified people at 1 point each**; the SMB track needs only **5 certified people at 4 points each**. For **Advanced certifications** (15 points max), the Enterprise track needs **7 certified people at 2.14 points each**; the SMB track needs only **2 certified people at 7.5 points each**. Each individual counts once no matter how many certifications they hold, and the same or different people may satisfy the intermediate and advanced metrics. An SMB-classified partner can therefore reach the full 35 skilling points with seven certified individuals — or fewer, since one person can count in both metrics.

## Which certifications count for Business Applications skilling?

The **intermediate** list includes the Dynamics 365 functional consultant associate certifications for Finance, Supply Chain Management, Customer Service, Field Service and Business Central; Finance and Operations Apps Developer Associate; Power BI Data Analyst Associate; Power Platform Functional Consultant Associate; Power Platform Developer Associate; Power Automate RPA Developer Associate; Dynamics 365 Customer Experience Analyst Associate; Dynamics 365 Sales AI Consultant Associate; Intelligent Applications Builder Associate; and Dynamics 365 Contact Center AI Engineer Associate. The **advanced** list is much shorter: Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management Functional Consultant Expert, Finance and Operations Apps Solution Architect Expert, Power Platform Solution Architect Expert, Dynamics 365 Business Central Developer Associate, and Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect. Several of these carry 2026 retirement dates and remain valid for one year if completed before retirement.

## What is monthly consumption value (MCV) and why does Business Applications use it?

Business Applications does not score customer success on monthly active users the way Modern Work does. Instead it uses **monthly consumption value**, calculated as `Monthly consumption = (monthly active users or capacity) * consumption rate`. Microsoft explains the reason: workloads have different usage units (users, profiles, capacity) and different expected deployment sizes, so a weighting rate normalises them and lets partners of any workload mix reach the requirement without workload-specific thresholds. Microsoft is emphatic that MCV "isn't a dollar amount, but a weighted calculation based on active customer usage," and it is calculated at the end of every month.

## What are the MCV consumption rates by workload?

The published rate card assigns a rate to each workload: Business Central 1000 (per MAU), Core HR 1000, Guides 1000, Remote Assist 1000, Talent Attract 1000, Talent Onboard 1000, Contact Center 200, Common Data Service / Customer Engagement 200, Power Apps 200, Power Automate 200, Power BI 200, Finance & Operations 100, Portals 100, Retail 100, and Customer Insights 0.2 (per profile). Microsoft's worked example: a Business Central deployment of 10 MAU is 10,000 MCV, and a Customer Insights deployment of 50,000 profiles is also 10,000 MCV, together contributing 20,000 MCV for the month. The Customer Engagement rate covers usage from Sales, Sales Insights, Customer Service and Field Service; Finance & Operations covers Finance and Project Operations.

## How does the Usage growth metric score in Business Applications?

Usage growth is the heaviest metric at **30 points**, and **each one percent of MCV growth contributes one point** — so 30% growth maxes it, and the 30% threshold is the same for Enterprise and SMB. The calculation is `(Eligible usage last month) – (Eligible usage same month last year) / (Eligible usage same month last year) X 100`. There is a hard prerequisite baseline before you can score anything: **Enterprise partners need a baseline consumption value of 50,000 twelve months ago; SMB partners need 25,000.** A partner below the baseline scores zero on the heaviest metric in the area and therefore cannot qualify. Eligible attributions are CPOR OSU, CSP Tier 1, CSP Tier 2 and DPOR for Dynamics 365, and PAL for Power Platform and Customer Insights.

## How does the Deployments metric score in Business Applications?

Deployments is worth up to **20 points**, with **each qualifying deployment worth four points**, reaching the cap at five deployments — the same target for Enterprise and SMB. The calculation is `(Eligible deployments last month) - (Eligible deployments same month last year)`. What counts as an eligible deployment is a unique workload rolled up at customer tenant level contributing at least **10,000 MCV** on the Enterprise track or at least **5,000 MCV** on the SMB track. In Business Central terms, using the rate card, 5,000 MCV is five monthly active users — which makes the SMB deployment bar genuinely small-business shaped.

## What does a qualifying Business Applications score look like in practice?

For an SMB-classified partner, full skilling (20 + 15 = 35 points from about seven certified people), five net workload adds at USD 250+ each (15 points) and five deployments at 5,000+ MCV each (20 points) totals 70 — but only if usage growth is also non-zero, which requires clearing the 25,000 MCV baseline from twelve months ago. That baseline is the real gate. A Business Central reseller with a stable installed base but no growth will find usage growth is the metric that blocks them, since a flat or declining MCV scores zero and zero in any metric disqualifies regardless of total.

## Currency warning

All weights, thresholds, MCV rates and certification lists in this file come from the Microsoft Learn Solutions Partner for Business Applications page carrying a page date of 30 June 2026 and last updated 31 July 2026. Microsoft states directly that the MCV rate card "is subject to change" and that the eligible workload lists "are growing" and should be checked frequently. Note also an internal inconsistency in the source: the Net customer adds calculation formula quotes a "> 1500 USD" figure for both tracks while the eligibility text specifies USD 1,500 for Enterprise and USD 250 for SMB — the eligibility text is the more specific statement. Several listed certifications retire during 2026. Re-verify against Partner Center before quoting any number.
