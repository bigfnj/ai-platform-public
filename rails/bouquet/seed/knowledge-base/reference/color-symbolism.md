# Color Symbolism

Color often carries as much meaning as the flower itself. A red rose and a
yellow rose send opposite messages. This is the cross-cutting lens applied to
every bloom in a bouquet.

**Rule of thumb:** meaning = flower symbolism + color symbolism + cultural
context. When they conflict, culture wins (a white flower is joyous at a
Western wedding and funereal at an East Asian one).

## General Western color meanings
| Color | Core associations | Cautions / double meanings |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Love, passion, romance, desire, courage, respect | Can read as aggressive/intense; deep red = commitment, mourning in some funerary use |
| **White** | Purity, innocence, peace, reverence, new beginnings, sympathy | Mourning/death in East & South Asia; sympathy even in the West |
| **Pink** | Grace, gratitude, admiration, gentleness, femininity, joy | Light pink = admiration/sympathy; hot pink = gratitude/energy |
| **Yellow** | Friendship, joy, cheer, optimism, new beginnings | Historically jealousy/infidelity (Victorian); death/parting in parts of Latin America & Iran |
| **Orange** | Energy, enthusiasm, warmth, fascination, desire, confidence | Bold; less traditional for solemn occasions |
| **Purple / Lavender** | Royalty, dignity, admiration, enchantment, spirituality, success | Deep purple can signal sorrow/apology (e.g. purple hyacinth) |
| **Blue** | Peace, tranquility, trust, serenity, the rare/unattainable | Truly blue flowers are uncommon (often dyed or bred) |
| **Green** | Health, renewal, harmony, fertility, good fortune, youth | Neutral, calming; used to signal "wellness" |
| **Peach / Coral** | Sincerity, gratitude, modesty, enthusiasm, warmth | Softer alternative to red/orange |
| **Burgundy / Deep red** | Unconscious beauty, deep passion, commitment | Rich, formal |
| **Black (very dark maroon)** | Farewell, mourning, elegance, mystery, rebirth | Almost always cultivar-dark, not true black |

## Cultural color notes (critical — these override the table above)
- **China & broader East Asia:** Red = luck, joy, celebration, weddings.
  White and sometimes yellow/white chrysanthemums = mourning/funerals. Avoid
  gifting white flowers to the living outside sympathy contexts.
- **Japan:** White chrysanthemums are strongly funerary. Giving potted/rooted
  plants to the sick is avoided (illness "takes root"). See *hanakotoba* in
  floriography-and-history.md.
- **India (Hindu tradition):** Marigold and jasmine are auspicious — weddings,
  festivals (Diwali), garlands, temple offerings. White is for mourning.
- **Latin America (esp. Mexico):** Marigolds (cempasúchil) are the flower of
  the dead (Día de los Muertos). Yellow/red flowers can carry death or passion
  connotations depending on setting.
- **Iran / Persian tradition:** Hyacinth (sonbol) marks Nowruz and spring
  renewal. Yellow flowers can signal parting in some readings.
- **Western Europe & North America:** White = weddings & purity; red =
  romance; white lilies and (in much of continental Europe) chrysanthemums =
  funerals.

## How to use this in an analysis
1. Note the dominant palette of the bouquet.
2. For each flower, combine its base symbolism with its specific color.
3. Check whether the intended (or photo's implied) culture flips any meaning.
4. Read the palette as a whole: all-white + greenery → sympathy/wedding;
   red-dominant → romance; bright multi-color → celebration/cheer; pastel
   blush → romance/wedding; jewel tones → autumn/formal.
