# Bouquet Types & Floral-Design Roles

Two things to read from a photo: the **arrangement form** (the overall shape/
style) and the **role each flower plays** within it. Both inform what occasion
and skill level the bouquet represents.

## Floral-design roles (the most useful lens for identification & pairing)
Florists build arrangements from four functional flower roles plus greenery.
Knowing the role also predicts pairings.

| Role | What it does | Typical flowers |
|---|---|---|
| **Line flowers** | Tall, spiky stems that set height, outline, and structure | Snapdragon, delphinium, larkspur, gladiolus, stock, liatris, bells of Ireland |
| **Mass / focal flowers** | Round, solid blooms that form the visual weight and focal point | Rose, carnation, peony, hydrangea, chrysanthemum, dahlia |
| **Form flowers** | Distinctive, eye-catching silhouettes that draw the eye | Orchid, calla lily, lily, iris, protea, bird of paradise, anthurium |
| **Filler flowers** | Small blooms that fill gaps and add texture/softness | Baby's breath, wax flower, statice, aster, solidago |
| **Foliage / greenery** | Backdrop, framing, and volume | Eucalyptus, ruscus, fern, salal, ivy |

A "complete" arrangement usually balances all of these. Spotting a lone role
(e.g. all mass roses + baby's breath filler) tells you the style at a glance.

## Arrangement forms (the overall shape)
| Form | Description | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| **Posy / Nosegay / Tussie-mussie** | Small, round, hand-held bunch | Bridesmaids, gifts; historically message-bearing |
| **Round / Biedermeier** | Tightly packed dome, often concentric rings | Classic bridal & gift bouquets |
| **Hand-tied** | Stems gathered and tied, natural/loose look | Modern gifting, "just-picked" style |
| **Cascade / Shower / Teardrop** | Flowers spill downward in a trailing tail | Formal bridal |
| **Crescent** | Curved, asymmetric arc | Stylized bridal/design |
| **Presentation / Pageant / Arm sheaf** | Long stems cradled in the arm | Pageants, congratulations, curtain-call |
| **Composite (Glamelia / Duchesse rose)** | Many petals wired to look like one giant bloom | Showpiece bridal |
| **Pomander / Kissing ball** | Sphere of flowers on a ribbon | Flower girls, décor |
| **Corsage / Boutonnière** | Single/small cluster worn on wrist or lapel | Proms, weddings, formals |
| **Standing spray / Wreath / Casket spray** | Large display forms on easels or frames | Funerals, ceremonies |
| **Ikebana** | Minimalist Japanese art of arrangement; line, space, balance | Artistic/ceremonial |

## Reading style from composition
- **Tight dome, uniform blooms, ribbon-wrapped** → traditional gift/bridal.
- **Loose, asymmetric, lots of greenery & textural filler** → modern/"garden"
  or "organic" style.
- **Long stems cradled, few large blooms** → presentation/congratulations.
- **All-white, formal, standing form** → sympathy/funeral.
- **Wired, sculptural, single-species showpiece** → high-end bridal design.

## Color-arrangement conventions
- **Monochromatic** — one color in varying shades → elegant, formal.
- **Analogous** — neighboring colors (e.g. red-orange-yellow) → warm, harmonious.
- **Complementary** — opposite colors (e.g. purple + yellow) → vivid, energetic.
- **Whites + greenery** → weddings and sympathy (context decides which).
