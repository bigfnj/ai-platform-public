# Artisan Bread

*~3½ hours from scratch or 1½ hours with refrigerated dough.*

## Ingredients
- 3 cups lukewarm water
- 1 1/2 tablespoons granulated yeast or 2 (7¼ g) packets granulated yeast
- 1 tablespoon kosher salt or 1 tablespoon other coarse salt
- 6½ cups unsifted, unbleached all-purpose flour

## Instructions
1. Warm the water slightly. It should feel just a little warmer than body temperature.
2. Add the yeast to the water in a 5-quart bowl or, preferably, in a resealable, lidded (not airtight) plastic food container or food-grade bucket. Don't worry about getting it all to dissolve.
3. Mix in the flour and salt (kneading is unnecessary). Add all of the flour at once, measuring it in with dry ingredient measuring cups, by gently scooping up the flour, then sweeping the top level with a knife or spatula. Don't press down into the flour as you scoop or you'll throw off the measurement.
4. Mix with a wooden spoon. If you're hand mixing and it becomes too difficult to incorporate all the flour with the spoon, you can reach into your mixing vessel with very wet hands and press the mixture together (it is not necessary…a wooden spoon works just fine).
5. Allow to rise. Cover with lid (not airtight or it could explode the lid off). Allow the mixture to rise at room temperature until it begins to collapse (or at least flattens on the top), approx 2 hours, depending on room temperature, and initial water temperature.
6. On baking day: prepare your loaf tin, tray, or whatever you're baking it in/on. Sprinkle the surface of your refrigerated dough with flour.
7. Pull up and cut off a grapefruit-size piece of dough (c 1 lb), using a serrated knife. Hold the mass of dough in your hands and add a little more flour as needed so it won't stick to your hands.
8. Gently stretch the surface of the dough around to the bottom on all 4 sides, rotating the ball a quarter-turn as you go. Most of the dusting flour will fall off (that's fine, it isn't meant to be incorporated).
9. The correctly shaped final product will be smooth and cohesive.
10. Rest the loaf and let it rise in the form, on the tray/pizza peel, for about 40 minutes. Depending on the age of the dough, you may not see much rise during this period. That's fine, more rising will occur during baking.
11. Twenty minutes before baking, preheat the oven to 450°F. Place an empty broiler tray for holding water on any other shelf that won't interfere with the rising bread.
12. Dust and slash. Dust the top of the loaf liberally with flour, which will allow the slashing knife to pass without sticking. Slash a quarter inch deep cross, diagonal lines, or 3cm-tactile pattern on top using a serrated knife.
13. After a 20 min preheat you're ready to bake, even though the oven thermometer won't be at full temperature yet. Put your loaf in the oven. Pour about 1 cup of hot water (from the tap) into the broiler tray and close the oven door to trap the steam.
14. Bake for about 30 minutes, or until the crust is nicely browned and firm to the touch.

## Shopping List
- all-purpose flour
- coarse salt
- granulated yeast
- water
