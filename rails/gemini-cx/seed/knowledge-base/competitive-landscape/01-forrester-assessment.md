# What Forrester said about GECX — the critical read

> Source: Forrester blog, "Google Has Entered The Contact Center Chat", 2026-01-20, by Colleen Fazio, Emily Pfeiffer and Kate Leggett
> As of: 2026-08 · Verified: 2026-08-18 · Status: analyst opinion

## What did Forrester conclude about Gemini Enterprise for CX?

That the integration is the innovation, and the individual components mostly are not. Published
nine days after launch by three Forrester analysts, the assessment credits one genuinely novel
aspect — **"the connection between e-commerce, customer service, and insights"** — and is blunt
about the rest.

The central criticism: most of the customer-service features are **"practically table stakes for
contact-center-as-a-service vendors"**, including real-time rep guidance and QA. Several features
remain in **beta**, limiting immediate deployment. And the **"agentic" label is misleading** —
Forrester characterises the capabilities as **"assistive, conversational experiences"** rather than
truly autonomous agents.

Forrester also flags **Personal Intelligence** (described as beta) as offering potential for "a
single platform that has both the data and the customer-facing layer to deliver contextual
interactions." Note that Personal Intelligence was not found in Google's own GECX product or
documentation pages during this research, so treat the name as analyst-sourced and unverified.

## The caution Forrester gives buyers

**"Avoid the trap of simply buying a box of CX."** The analysts stress that "creating effective,
connected experiences requires a sound strategy just as much as the tech to deliver it."

This is the most useful sentence in the piece for a delivery conversation, and it should be taken
as a compliment to the work rather than a criticism of the product. A GECX purchase without a
journey design, a containment strategy, and a measurement baseline will underperform regardless of
how good the models are. Lead with that, because it is both true and the thing a customer most
needs to hear before signing.

## Market framing

Forrester positions this as potentially **"the first major moment for customer service finding its
way into answer engine shopping"**, noting that exclusive partnerships between "commerce or
payments vendors and answer engines (e.g., ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Perplexity)" are becoming
common. **No direct contact-centre competitors are named** in the assessment.

## How to handle the "not really agentic" criticism

Do not argue with it — concede the precise version and redirect. Today's GECX is strongest as
assistive and conversational, and the genuinely autonomous action surface (the Commerce Agents that
execute consented checkout) is the part the documentation still marks "coming soon". So the
criticism and the documented status agree with each other. The defensible position is that GECX's
differentiator right now is **context continuity across commerce and service**, not autonomy, and
that autonomy is a roadmap item to be verified rather than a current claim.
