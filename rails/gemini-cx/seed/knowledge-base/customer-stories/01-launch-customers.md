# The GECX launch customers — and exactly what each announced

> Source: googlecloudpresscorner.com press release, 2026-01-11
> As of: 2026-08 · Verified: 2026-08-18 · Status: announced intent, not published results

## Who are the named GECX customers?

Four were named at the January 2026 launch: **Kroger**, **Lowe's**, **Woolworths Group**, and
**Papa Johns**. Read what follows precisely — these are announcements of adoption and plans, not
published outcomes. No containment rates, cost savings, CSAT movements, or handle-time reductions
were released for any of them.

**Kroger** is applying GECX to personalised meal and shopping solutions, and to analysing store
calls in order to proactively resolve issues. **Yael Cosset**, EVP and Chief Digital Officer,
framed it as "creating a simplified shopping experience for our customers that fits into their
day."

**Lowe's** is enhancing its existing AI advisor, **"Mylow"**, for personalised home-improvement
guidance. **Seemantini Godbole**, Chief Digital and Information Officer, described "using AI to
simplify and personalize the experience." Note that Mylow pre-existed GECX — this is an
augmentation of a live assistant, which makes it the most credible of the four as evidence that
GECX integrates with something already running.

**Woolworths Group** is evolving its digital assistant **"Olive"** for meal planning and
budget-conscious recommendations. **Amanda Bardwell**, CEO and Managing Director, called it "a
global game changer for retail" — the most quotable and least substantiated claim in the release.

**Papa Johns** is pursuing omnichannel ordering with personalisation and menu management across
locations. **Kevin Vasconi**, Chief Digital and Technology Officer, said it would "help Papa Johns
harness the power of AI to bring back the feeling of 'being known'."

## How to use these stories honestly

They are strong evidence of **category commitment by serious retailers**, and that is genuinely
useful: four large, recognisable brands with real transaction volume chose to build on GECX within
weeks of launch. They are **not** evidence that GECX delivers a particular result, because no
result has been published.

The right framing in a customer conversation is "here is who is betting on this and what they are
building" — not "here is the ROI others achieved." If asked for outcomes, say plainly that Google
has not published them yet and offer to model expected impact from the customer's own baseline
instead. Two of the four (Woolworths' Olive, Lowe's Mylow) are named assistants you can reference
by name, which is more concrete than most launch logo slides and worth using.

## Note the sector concentration

All four are retail, grocery, home improvement, or food service — consistent with an NRF launch.
There were **no named launch customers in financial services, telecommunications, healthcare,
insurance, or the public sector**, which are the sectors with the largest contact centres and the
heaviest compliance requirements. For a prospect in one of those, do not imply peer adoption that
has not been announced; lead instead with the Agent Assist overlay path, which is lower risk and
does not depend on the commerce story at all.
