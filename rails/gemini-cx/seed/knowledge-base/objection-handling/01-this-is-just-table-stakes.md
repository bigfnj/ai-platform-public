# Objection: "This is just table stakes — our CCaaS vendor already does all of this"

> Source: Forrester 2026-01-20 assessment; GECX component documentation
> As of: 2026-08 · Verified: 2026-08-18 · Status: practitioner guidance

## Objection: "This is just table stakes — our CCaaS vendor already does all of this"

Concede the feature comparison, then move the argument. Forrester made this exact criticism, and it
is correct at the feature level: real-time rep guidance and automated QA are "practically table
stakes for contact-center-as-a-service vendors". Arguing that Google's summarisation is better than
Genesys' summarisation is a fight you will lose on the customer's own evaluation matrix, and it is
the wrong fight.

The defensible claim is the one Forrester also conceded as genuinely novel: **the connection between
e-commerce, customer service, and insights**. No CCaaS vendor owns the shopping journey. The
argument is not "our summarisation is better" but "your CCaaS does not know what the customer was
looking at ten minutes ago on your website, and cannot act on it." GECX's Shopping-agent-to-Agent-
Studio link is explicitly designed so "every support interaction is informed by historical
context."

## The second defensible difference: you keep your CCaaS

Agent Assist integrates with **Genesys Cloud**, **LivePerson**, **Salesforce**, **Twilio Flex**, and
custom desktops. This means the objection contains a false premise — it assumes a replacement
decision. It usually is not one. The honest position is that GECX can be an AI layer over the
contact centre they already own, which reframes the conversation from "rip and replace" to "add
capability where yours is weakest", and removes most of the risk they are objecting on behalf of.

## When to agree and walk away from the component

If the customer's use cases are entirely within-contact-centre — deflect FAQs, help agents,
automate QA — and their incumbent CCaaS does those adequately, say so. The GECX advantage in that
scenario is thin, and pushing it damages credibility for the conversation where it is strong. Ask
instead whether commerce, ordering, or account actions ever come up in their contacts. If the
answer is yes, the cross-journey argument reopens; if it is genuinely no, this is not their project
right now.

## What not to say

Do not claim differentiating containment or CSAT figures — Google published none for GECX, and the
launch customers announced adoption rather than results. Do not claim the individual features are
best-in-class; that is precisely the claim Forrester rejected. Do not lead with model quality
either, since all three supported models are **Flash**-class and the customer's incumbent may well
run a comparable model. Lead with journey continuity and with the overlay path.
